2. Musical Evolution by Historical Era

  • Ancient Era: Rooted in the Sama Veda. The Natya Shastra by Bharata Muni (c. 200 BCE – 200 CE) laid the foundational theory of Rasa and Bhava. Key forms included Prabandha Sangeet and Tevaram hymns in the South.
  • Medieval Era (Divergence & Fusion): The 13th/14th centuries (under Delhi Sultanate, Amir Khusrau) saw the divergence of Indian music into Hindustani (North - Persian influenced) and Carnatic (South - indigenous development).
    • Hindustani forms: Dhrupad (Mansingh Tomar), Khayal, Tarana, Thumri, Ghazal.
    • Carnatic forms: Purandara Dasa (Father of Carnatic music) formulated the teaching system. Trinity of Carnatic Music (Tyagaraja, Muthuswami Dikshitar, Syama Sastri) perfected the Kriti.
    • Bhakti & Sufi: Qawwali, Kirtan, Abhangs, Shabads became mainstream.
  • Modern & Contemporary Era: The rise of distinct regional folk styles and gharanas (Gwalior, Agra, Jaipur, Kirana). Notable regional traditions include:
    • Bengal: Baul singing, Rabindra Sangeet, Nazrul Geeti.
    • Assam: Borgeet (devotional songs by Sankardev).
    • Maharashtra: Abhang, Lavani, Powada.
    • Rajasthan: Langa, Manganiyar, Maand.
    • Kashmir: Sufiana Kalam, Chakri.
    • Punjab: Jugni, Tappa, Boliyan.

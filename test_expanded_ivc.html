Master-Grade Revision Module | NDA, CDS, AFCAT, CAPF

📌 Chronology, Geographical Extent & River Basins

  • Chronological Phases:
    • Early Harappan (Pre-Harappan): c. 3300–2600 BCE. (Chacla, Kot Diji, Hakra, Amri cultures).
    • Mature Harappan: c. 2600–1900 BCE. (Peak urbanization, standardized weights, script).
    • Late Harappan (Post-Harappan): c. 1900–1300 BCE. (Decline, de-urbanization, shift to rural settlements).
  • Geographical Extent:
    • North: Chander (Himachal Pradesh) to Sutkagendor (Balochistan).
    • East: Alamgirpur (Uttar Pradesh) to Daimabad (Maharashtra).
    • South: Lothal (Gujarat) to Balakot (Sindh).
    • West: Sutkagendor (Balochistan) to Rakhigarhi (Haryana).
  • River Basins:
    • Indus Basin: Harappa, Mohenjo-daro, Dholavira, Lothal, Kalibangan, Banawali, Rakhigarhi.
    • Ghaggar-Hakra System: Kalibangan, Banawali, Alamgirpur. (Note: Ghaggar is a seasonal river; Hakra is its ancient bed).
    • Other Rivers: Sutkagendor (Hiron River), Daimabad (Tapti River).

🏗️ Town Planning, Architecture & Sanitation Engineering

  • Grid Plan: Cities were planned with a grid pattern of streets running North-South and East-West. Main streets were wider than lanes.
  • Burnt Bricks: Standardized burnt bricks with a ratio of 1:2:4 (height:width:length). This standardization indicates centralized planning.
  • Great Bath (Mohenjo-daro & Harappa):
    • Dimensions: 12m x 7m x 2.4m deep.
    • Sealed with bitumen to prevent water leakage.
    • Purpose: Ritual purification (not for swimming).
  • Great Granary:
    • Located in the Citadel (upper town).
    • Large rectangular structure with ventilation holes and drainage channels.
    • Purpose: Storage of grain (wheat, barley, rice).
  • Sanitation System:
    • Covered drainage system running along the main streets.
    • Individual houses had private bathrooms and toilets connected to the main drain.
    • Manholes for cleaning and maintenance.
  • Dholavira Unique Features:
    • Three-part layout: Citadel, Middle Town, Lower Town.
    • Large water reservoirs (14 in number) for water storage.
    • No Great Bath or Granary.
    • Stone script (largest collection of Indus script).

🌾 Economic Life: Agriculture, Metallurgy & Craft Specialization

  • Agriculture:
    • Crops: Wheat, barley, rice (Lothal, Banawali), mustard, sesame, dates, melons, peas.
    • Ploughing: Earliest evidence at Kalibangan (ploughed field).
    • Irrigation: Canals (Lothal, Dholavira), wells.
  • Metallurgy:
    • Bronze Age: Used copper, tin, and bronze (copper + tin alloy).
    • Iron: NOT used in Mature Harappan period. (Iron Age starts later).
    • Gold & Silver: Used for jewelry and ornaments.
    • Lost-Wax Technique: Used for making bronze statues (e.g., Dancing Girl).
  • Craft Specialization:
    • Seal Carving: Steatite seals with animal motifs and script.
    • Bead Making: Factories at Mohenjo-daro, Chanhudaro, Lothal. Materials: carnelian, agate, jasper, faience.
    • Textiles: Cotton (Sindon) was a major export. Fragments found at Lothal and Mohenjo-daro.
    • Pottery: Red and black painted pottery. Standardized shapes.
  • Weights & Measures:
    • Binary system: Weights in multiples of 16 (1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, etc.).
    • Material: Chert, steatite, bronze.
    • Standardization indicates a unified economic system.

🚢 Trade Networks: Domestic & Mesopotamian Contacts

  • Domestic Trade:
    • Network of cities connected by roads and rivers.
    • Exchange of goods: grains, textiles, beads, metals, shells.
    • Lothal: Major port for domestic and international trade.
  • International Trade (Mesopotamia):
    • Meluhha: Name used in Sumerian texts for the Indus Valley Civilization.
    • Dilmun: Modern-day Bahrain. Intermediate trading hub.
    • Makan: Modern-day Oman. Source of copper and tin.
    • Lothal Dockyard: Tidal lock mechanism allowed boats to enter/exit based on tide levels.
    • Imports: Lapis lazuli (from Afghanistan), copper (from Oman), tin (from Afghanistan), gold (from India).
    • Exports: Cotton textiles, beads, carnelian, ivory, wood.

🕊️ Religion, Society & Burial Traditions

  • Religious Beliefs:
    • No Temples: No clear evidence of temples or large religious structures.
    • Mother Goddess: Terracotta figurines suggest worship of a mother goddess.
    • Pashupati Seal: Seated figure with horns, surrounded by animals. Often interpreted as a proto-Shiva or Pashupati (Lord of Animals).
    • Linga/Yoni: Some scholars interpret certain seals as early forms of Linga and Yoni worship.
    • Tree & Animal Worship: Peepal tree, elephant, buffalo, unicorn (mythical), tiger, lion.
    • Fire Altars: Found at Kalibangan, Lothal, Banawali. Suggests ritual use of fire.
  • Society:
    • Stratified society: Ruling class (Priest-King?), merchants, artisans, farmers.
    • Gender equality: Female figurines are more common than male.
    • No evidence of slavery or extreme inequality.
  • Burial Traditions:
    • Primary Burial: Body placed in a pit, covered with bricks or mud. (Lothal, Kalibangan).
    • Secondary Burial: Bones collected after decomposition and placed in urns. (Harappa, Cemetery H culture).
    • Cremation: Rare, but some evidence at Harappa.
    • Grave Goods: Pottery, beads, tools, weapons placed with the dead.

🔍 Script, Seals & Weights

  • Script:
    • Undeciphered: Still not fully deciphered due to short inscriptions and lack of bilingual texts.
    • Boustrophedon: Writing direction alternates (left-to-right, then right-to-left).
    • Pictographic: ~400 signs, mostly pictographic (representing objects).
    • Medium: Steatite seals, pottery, tablets, metal objects.
  • Seals:
    • Unicorn Seal: Most common seal (60-70%). Features a mythical one-horned animal.
    • Other Animals: Bull, elephant, tiger, lion, rhinoceros.
    • Purpose: Used for sealing trade goods, administrative records, and personal identification.
  • Weights:
    • Binary system (multiples of 16).
    • Small weights for jewelry, large weights for grain.
    • Standardized across the civilization.

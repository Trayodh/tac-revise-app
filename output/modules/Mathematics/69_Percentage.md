## Page 1: Core Concepts & Foundational Rules

Percentage is a fundamental concept in mathematics, representing a fraction of a hundred. It is denoted by the symbol '%'. Understanding percentages is crucial for various quantitative aptitude sections in competitive exams like NDA, CDS, and AFCAT.

### I. Basic Percentage Calculation

**1. Percentage Increase/Decrease:**
To find the percentage increase or decrease in a value, use the formula:

`Percentage Change = ((Final Value - Initial Value) / Initial Value) * 100%`

*   If Final Value > Initial Value, it's a percentage increase.
*   If Final Value < Initial Value, it's a percentage decrease.

**Example:** A person’s salary increased from `7200 to `8100.
Percentage increase = `((8100 - 7200) / 7200) * 100%`
`= (900 / 7200) * 100% = (1/8) * 100% = 12.5%` or `12 1/2 %`.

### II. Rules for Inter-related Percentage Changes

These rules help in quickly calculating the impact of a percentage change on a related quantity.

#### Rule 5: A is x% More Than B

If quantity 'A' is `x%` more than quantity 'B', then 'B' is less than 'A' by:
`[x / (100 + x)] * 100%`

**Example:** Raj gets 10% less marks than Rohit. What percentage of marks does Rohit get more than Raj?
Here, the question is phrased in reverse. If Raj gets 10% *less* than Rohit, we need to find how much *more* Rohit gets than Raj. Let's rephrase the rule for clarity in this context:
If B is x% less than A, then A is more than B by `[x / (100 - x)] * 100%`.
Given x = 10% (Raj gets 10% less than Rohit).
Rohit's marks are more than Raj's by = `[10 / (100 - 10)] * 100%`
`= (10 / 90) * 100% = (1/9) * 100% = 11 1/9 %`.

#### Rule 6: A is x% Less Than B

If quantity 'A' is `x%` less than quantity 'B', then 'B' is more than 'A' by:
`[x / (100 - x)] * 100%`

*(Note: The example for Rule 5 effectively demonstrates Rule 6 as well, just with the roles of A and B swapped based on the question's phrasing.)*

## Page 2: Advanced Percentage Rules & Applications

### IV. Price, Consumption, and Expenditure

#### Rule 7: Constant Expenditure

If the price of a commodity is increased (or decreased) by `x%`, then the decrease (or increase) in consumption, so as not to increase (or decrease) the expenditure, is:
`[x / (100 ± x)] * 100%`
*   Use `+` for price increase (leading to consumption decrease).
*   Use `-` for price decrease (leading to consumption increase).

**Example:** If the price of cooking gas rises by 15%, by what percent should a family reduce its consumption so as not to exceed the budget?
Here, x = 15% (price increase).
Reduction in consumption = `[15 / (100 + 15)] * 100%`
`= (15 / 115) * 100% = (3 / 23) * 100% = 13 1/23 %`.

### V. Successive Percentage Changes

#### Rule 8: Net Effect of Two Successive Changes

If the value of an object is first changed by `x%` (increased or decreased) and then changed by `y%` (increased or decreased), the net percentage effect is:
`[x + y + (x * y / 100)] %`
*   Use `+` sign for increment, `-` sign for decrement.

**Example:** The price of an article is first increased by 20% and later decreased by 25%. Find the net percentage change.
Here, x = +20% (increase), y = -25% (decrease).
Net effect = `[20 + (-25) + (20 * -25 / 100)] %`
`= [-5 + (-500 / 100)] %`
`= [-5 - 5] % = -10%`
A net decrease of 10%.

### VI. Population Growth/Decrease

#### Rule 9: Population Change at a Constant Rate

If the population of a town is 'P' and it increases (or decreases) at a constant rate of `R%` per annum:
**(i) Population after 'n' years:** `P * (1 ± R/100)^n`
**(ii) Population 'n' years ago:** `P / (1 ± R/100)^n`
*   Use `+` for increase, `-` for decrease.

**Example:** Population of a town is 352800. It increases at 5% per annum.
**Population after 2 years:**
`352800 * (1 + 5/100)^2 = 352800 * (105/100)^2 = 352800 * (21/20) * (21/20) = 388962`
**Population 2 years ago:**
`352800 / (1 + 5/100)^2 = 352800 / (105/100)^2 = 352800 * (20/21) * (20/21) = 320000`

#### Rule 10: Population Change at Variable Rates

If the present population of a city is 'P' and there is an increment or decrement of `R1%`, `R2%`, and `R3%` in the first, second, and third year respectively:
**Population after 3 years:** `P * (1 ± R1/100) * (1 ± R2/100) * (1 ± R3/100)`
*   Use `+` for increase, `-` for decrease.

**Example:** Population in 2004 was 1000000. In 2005, +15%; in 2006, -35%; in 2007, +45%. Find population at end of 2007.
`1000000 * (1 + 15/100) * (1 - 35/100) * (1 + 45/100)`
`= 1000000 * (115/100) * (65/100) * (145/100)`
`= 1000000 * 1.15 * 0.65 * 1.45 = 1083875`

**Application:** If a price increases by 25%, it means the new price is `(1 + 1/4) = 5/4` times the original price. If it decreases by 20%, it's `(1 - 1/5) = 4/5` times the original.

### II. Deep Dive into Successive Percentage Changes (Rule 8)

The formula `x + y + (xy/100)` is incredibly versatile. It's not just for price changes but for any two successive percentage changes applied to a single quantity.

*   **Area Changes:** If the length of a rectangle increases by `x%` and its breadth increases by `y%`, the net percentage change in its area is `x + y + (xy/100)`. (If a dimension decreases, use a negative sign for x or y).
    *   **Example:** A square's side increases by 10%. What's the area increase? Here, x=10, y=10. Net change = `10 + 10 + (10*10/100) = 20 + 1 = 21%`.
*   **Volume Changes:** For three successive changes (e.g., length, breadth, height), you can apply the formula twice. First, find the net effect of two changes, then use that result with the third change.
    *   `Net_12 = x + y + (xy/100)`
    *   `Net_Total = Net_12 + z + (Net_12 * z / 100)`

**Why does it work?**
Assume initial value = 100.
After x% change: `100 * (1 + x/100) = 100 + x`
After y% change on the new value: `(100 + x) * (1 + y/100) = 100 + y + x + (xy/100)`
The net change from 100 is `(100 + y + x + xy/100) - 100 = x + y + xy/100`.

### III. The Power of Assuming '100'

As seen in Example 4 (Rule 7) and the derivation of Rule 8, assuming an initial value of 100 often simplifies calculations, especially when dealing with multiple percentage changes or when the absolute initial value is not given. This is a common strategy in competitive exams.

**Example:** A man spends 75% of his income. If his income increases by 20% and expenditure by 10%, what is the percentage increase in savings?
Assume Income = 100.
Expenditure = 75% of 100 = 75.
Savings = Income - Expenditure = 100 - 75 = 25.

New Income = 100 + 20% of 100 = 120.
New Expenditure = 75 + 10% of 75 = 75 + 7.5 = 82.5.
New Savings = New Income - New Expenditure = 120 - 82.5 = 37.5.

Percentage increase in savings = `((37.5 - 25) / 25) * 100%`
`= (12.5 / 25) * 100% = (1/2) * 100% = 50%`.

### IV. Ratio Method for Percentage Changes

The ratio method is an elegant alternative for successive percentage changes, especially when dealing with fractions.

*   An `x%` increase means the new value is `(100+x)/100` times the old value.
*   An `x%` decrease means the new value is `(100-x)/100` times the old value.

**Example:** Price increased by 20%, then decreased by 25%.
*   20% increase: `100 -> 120` or `5 -> 6` (ratio of new to old)
*   25% decrease: `100 -> 75` or `4 -> 3` (ratio of new to old)

Multiply the ratios: `(6/5) * (3/4) = 18/20 = 9/10`.
This means the final price is `9/10` of the original price.
Change = `(9/10 - 1) * 100% = (-1/10) * 100% = -10%` (10% decrease).
This method is particularly useful for problems like Rule 10 (variable population changes).

### V. Understanding Base Value in Percentage Calculations

A common mistake is to change the base value incorrectly.
*   "A is x% more than B" means `A = B + (x/100)B = B(1 + x/100)`. Here, B is the base.
*   "B is y% less than A" means `B = A - (y/100)A = A(1 - y/100)`. Here, A is the base.

Always be clear about *what* the percentage is being calculated *of*. This is crucial for problems like Rule 5 and 6.

### VI. Visualizing Percentage Problems (Venn Diagrams)

For problems involving percentages of groups with overlaps (like "failed in English, failed in Math, failed in both"), Venn diagrams are invaluable.

**Example (from Q9):** 52% failed in English, 42% in Math, 17% in both.
*   Total failed in English only = 52% - 17% = 35%
*   Total failed in Math only = 42% - 17% = 25%
*   Total failed in at least one subject = (English only) + (Math only) + (Both)
    `= 35% + 25% + 17% = 77%`
*   Percentage passed in both subjects = 100% - (Total failed in at least one subject)
    `= 100% - 77% = 23%`

This approach is more intuitive and less prone to errors than just adding and subtracting percentages.

### VII. General Tips for Percentage Problems

1.  **Read Carefully:** Identify the base value for each percentage calculation.
2.  **Convert to Fractions:** For common percentages, use fractional equivalents.
3.  **Assume 100:** When no initial value is given, assume 100 for easier calculations.
4.  **Break Down Complex Problems:** For multiple changes, solve step-by-step or use the successive change formula/ratio method.
5.  **Check Units:** Ensure consistency in units (e.g., if income is in rupees, savings should also be).
6.  **Practice:** Regular practice with diverse problems is key to mastering percentages.

**1. If 90% of A = 30% of B and B = x% of A, the value of x is**
(a) 700
(b) 600
(c) 300
(d) 1100

**Solution 1:**
Given: `0.90 * A = 0.30 * B`
`B = (0.90 / 0.30) * A`
`B = 3 * A`
We are given `B = x% of A`, which means `B = (x/100) * A`.
Comparing `B = 3A` and `B = (x/100)A`:
`3 = x/100`
`x = 300`
**Answer: (c)**

**2. When 40% of a number is added to 42, the result is the number itself. The number is**
(a) 70
(b) 90
(c) 82
(d) 72

**Solution 2:**
Let the number be N.
Given: `0.40 * N + 42 = N`
`42 = N - 0.40 * N`
`42 = 0.60 * N`
`N = 42 / 0.60 = 42 / (6/10) = 42 * (10/6)`
`N = 7 * 10 = 70`
**Answer: (a)**

**3. If x% of y is 13x, then the value of y is**
(a) 880
(b) 1300
(c) 1200
(d) 700

**Solution 3:**
Given: `(x/100) * y = 13x`
Since x is a percentage, we assume x is not zero. We can divide both sides by x.
`y/100 = 13`
`y = 13 * 100`
`y = 1300`
**Answer: (b)**

**4. What is the number when 20% of number is 30% of 40?**
(a) 90
(b) 80
(c) 60
(d) 50

**Solution 4:**
Let the number be N.
Given: `20% of N = 30% of 40`
`0.20 * N = 0.30 * 40`
`0.20 * N = 12`
`N = 12 / 0.20 = 12 / (2/10) = 12 * (10/2)`
`N = 6 * 10 = 60`

**5. What is the number when increased by 20% becomes 300?**
(a) 250
(b) 200
(c) 180
(d) 280

**Solution 5:**
Let the number be N.
When increased by 20%, it becomes `N + 0.20 * N = 1.20 * N`.
Given: `1.20 * N = 300`
`N = 300 / 1.20 = 300 / (12/10) = 300 * (10/12)`
`N = 25 * 10 = 250`

**6. If 50% of (x - y) = 40% of (x + y), then what percent of x is y?**
(a) 10 1/9 %
(b) 11 1/9 %
(c) 13 1/9 %
(d) 21 1/9 %

**Solution 6:**
Given: `0.50 * (x - y) = 0.40 * (x + y)`
`5 * (x - y) = 4 * (x + y)` (multiplying by 10)
`5x - 5y = 4x + 4y`
`5x - 4x = 4y + 5y`
`x = 9y`
We need to find what percent of x is y. Let `y = k% of x`.
`y = (k/100) * x`
Substitute `x = 9y` into the equation:
`y = (k/100) * (9y)`
`1 = (k/100) * 9` (assuming y is not zero)
`100 = 9k`
`k = 100/9 = 11 1/9 %`
**Answer: (b)**

**7. Two candidates fought an election one get 65% of the votes and won by 300 votes. The total number of votes polled in the election is**
(a) 700
(b) 950
(c) 1000
(d) 900

**Solution 7:**
Let the total votes be V.
Winning candidate got 65% of V = `0.65V`.
Losing candidate got `(100 - 65)%` of V = `35% of V = 0.35V`.
The winning margin is `0.65V - 0.35V = 0.30V`.
Given that the winning margin is 300 votes.
`0.30V = 300`
`V = 300 / 0.30 = 300 / (3/10) = 300 * (10/3)`
`V = 100 * 10 = 1000`
**Answer: (c)**

**8. A man spends `3500 and saves 12 1/2 % of his income. His monthly income (in `) is**
(a) 4000
(b) 3800
(c) 4200
(d) 4500

**Solution 8:**
Savings percentage = `12 1/2 % = 12.5%`.
Expenditure percentage = `100% - 12.5% = 87.5%`.
Given that he spends `3500. So, 87.5% of his income is `3500.
Let his monthly income be I.
`0.875 * I = 3500`
`I = 3500 / 0.875`
`I = 3500 / (7/8)` (since 12.5% = 1/8, then 87.5% = 7/8)
`I = 3500 * (8/7)`
`I = 500 * 8 = 4000`
**Answer: (a)**

**9. In an examination 52% of the candidates failed in English, 42% in Mathematics and 17% in both. The number of those who passed in both the subjects is**
(a) 23%
(b) 40%
(c) 53%
(d) 33%

**Solution 9:**
Let E be the set of candidates who failed in English, and M be the set of candidates who failed in Mathematics.
`P(E) = 52%`
`P(M) = 42%`
`P(E ∩ M) = 17%` (failed in both)
Percentage of candidates who failed in at least one subject `P(E U M)`:
`P(E U M) = P(E) + P(M) - P(E ∩ M)`
`P(E U M) = 52% + 42% - 17%`
`P(E U M) = 94% - 17% = 77%`
This means 77% of candidates failed in at least one subject.
The percentage of candidates who passed in both subjects is `100% - P(E U M)`.
Passed in both = `100% - 77% = 23%`.
**Answer: (a)**

**10. To pass an examination, a candidate needs 40% marks. All questions carry equal marks. A candidate just passed by getting 10 answers correct by attempting 15 of the total questions. How many questions are there in the examination?**
(a) 25
(b) 30
(c) 40
(d) 45

**Solution 10:**
Let the total number of questions be Q.
Each question carries 1 mark (since all questions carry equal marks, we can assume this for simplicity).
Total marks = Q.
Passing marks = 40% of Q = `0.40Q`.
The candidate got 10 answers correct. So, marks obtained = 10.
Since the candidate *just passed*, their marks obtained must be equal to the passing marks.
`0.40Q = 10`
`Q = 10 / 0.40 = 10 / (4/10) = 10 * (10/4)`
`Q = 100 / 4 = 25`
The information about attempting 15 questions is extra and not needed to solve for the total number of questions.
**Answer: (a)**

**11. If salary of X is 20% more than salary of Y, then by how much percentage is salary of Y less than X?**
(a) 25
(b) 20
(c) 50/3
(d) 65/4

**Solution 11:**
This is an application of Rule 5.
If A (X's salary) is x% more than B (Y's salary), then B is less than A by `[x / (100 + x)] * 100%`.
Here, x = 20%.
Percentage by which Y's salary is less than X's = `[20 / (100 + 20)] * 100%`
`= (20 / 120) * 100% = (1/6) * 100%`
`= 100/6 % = 50/3 %`
**Answer: (c)**

**12. Water contains 14 2/7 % of hydrogen and the rest is oxygen. In 350 g of water, oxygen will be**
(a) 300 g
(b) 250 g
(c) 200 g
(d) None of these

**Solution 12:**
Percentage of hydrogen = `14 2/7 %`.
As a fraction, `14 2/7 % = (100/7) % = (100/7) / 100 = 1/7`.
So, hydrogen is 1/7 of water.
The rest is oxygen. So, percentage of oxygen = `100% - 14 2/7 % = 85 5/7 %`.
As a fraction, oxygen is `1 - 1/7 = 6/7` of water.
In 350 g of water, oxygen will be `(6/7) * 350 g`.
`= 6 * 50 g = 300 g`.
**Answer: (a)**

**13. The income of ‘A’ is 20% higher than that of ‘B’. The income of ‘B’ is 25% less than of C. What per cent less is A’s income from ‘C’s income?**
(a) 7%
(b) 8%
(c) 10%
(d) 12.5%

**Solution 13:**
Let C's income be `100`.
B's income is 25% less than C's: `B = 100 - 0.25 * 100 = 75`.
A's income is 20% higher than B's: `A = 75 + 0.20 * 75 = 75 + 15 = 90`.
Now, compare A's income with C's income.
A's income (90) is less than C's income (100) by `100 - 90 = 10`.
Percentage less = `(10 / 100) * 100% = 10%`.
**Answer: (c)**

**14. 38 L of milk was poured into a tub and the tub was found to be 5% empty. To completely fill the tub, what amount of additional milk must be poured?**
(a) 1 L
(b) 2 L
(c) 3 L
(d) 4 L

**Solution 14:**
If the tub is 5% empty, it means it is 95% full.
So, 38 L represents 95% of the tub's capacity.
Let the total capacity of the tub be C.
`0.95 * C = 38`
`C = 38 / 0.95 = 38 / (95/100) = 38 * (100/95)`
`C = (38/95) * 100 = (2/5) * 100 = 40 L`.
The tub's total capacity is 40 L.
Amount of milk already poured = 38 L.
Additional milk needed = `40 L - 38 L = 2 L`.
**Answer: (b)**

**15. 10% of the inhabitants of a certain city left that city. Later on 10% of the remaining inhabitants of that city again left the city. What is the remaining percentage of population of that city?**
(a) 80%
(b) 80.4%
(c) 80.4% (duplicate option)
(d) 81%

**Solution 15:**
Let the initial population be 100%.
First, 10% left. Remaining population = `100% - 10% = 90%`.
Then, 10% of the *remaining* (90%) left.
Amount that left in the second instance = `10% of 90% = 0.10 * 90% = 9%`.
Remaining population after the second departure = `90% - 9% = 81%`.
Alternatively, using successive changes:
First change: -10%
Second change: -10%
Remaining = `(1 - 0.10) * (1 - 0.10) * 100% = 0.90 * 0.90 * 100% = 0.81 * 100% = 81%`.
**Answer: (d)**

**16. If after 24% of wastage the net output of a coal-mine is 68400 quintals. Then, the total output of the coal-mine in quintals is**
(a) 70000
(b) 90000
(c) 80000
(d) 89000

**Solution 16:**

`0.76 * T = 68400`
`T = 68400 / 0.76`
`T = 68400 / (76/100) = 68400 * (100/76)`

`684 / 76 = 9` (since `76 * 9 = 684`)

**17. A rise of 25% in the price of grapes compels a person to buy 1.5 kg of grapes less for `240. Then, the original price of grapes per kg is**
(a) `40
(b) `32
(c) `30
(d) `28

**Solution 17:**
Let the original price be `P` per kg.
Original quantity bought for `240 = 240/P` kg.
New price = `P + 25% of P = 1.25P`.
New quantity bought for `240 = 240/(1.25P)` kg.
Given that the person buys 1.5 kg less:
`(240/P) - (240/(1.25P)) = 1.5`
`240 * (1/P - 1/(1.25P)) = 1.5`
`240 * ((1.25 - 1) / (1.25P)) = 1.5`
`240 * (0.25 / (1.25P)) = 1.5`
`240 * (1 / (5P)) = 1.5` (since `1.25 / 0.25 = 5`)
`48 / P = 1.5`
`P = 48 / 1.5 = 48 / (3/2) = 48 * (2/3)`
`P = 16 * 2 = 32`
The original price of grapes per kg is `32.
**Answer: (b)**

**18. The price of an item is increased by 20% and then decreased by 20% the final price as compared to original price is**

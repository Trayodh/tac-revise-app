## Introduction to HCF and LCM
The provided primary textbook context does not directly relate to HCF (Highest Common Factor) and LCM (Least Common Multiple) of numbers. However, we can establish a foundational understanding of these concepts.

- **HCF** of two or more numbers is the greatest number that divides each of them without leaving a remainder.
- **LCM** of two or more numbers is the smallest number that is a multiple of each of the given numbers.

## Basic Concepts
Understanding HCF and LCM requires a grasp of basic arithmetic operations and the concept of factors and multiples.
- **Factors** are the numbers that divide into another number exactly without leaving a remainder.
- **Multiples** are the products of a number and an integer.

## Calculating HCF and LCM
The HCF can be found by listing the factors of each number and selecting the greatest common factor. The LCM can be calculated using the formula: LCM(a, b) = (a * b) / HCF(a, b), where a and b are the numbers in question.

## Practice Exercises
1. Find the HCF and LCM of 12 and 15.
2. A bus leaves the station at 8:00 AM and another at 8:15 AM. If the first bus runs every 15 minutes and the second every 20 minutes, when will they meet again?
3. Simplify the fraction 12/16 using HCF.

## Step-by-Step Solutions
1. **HCF of 12 and 15**:
   - Factors of 12: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12
   - Factors of 15: 1, 3, 5, 15
   - HCF: 3
   - **LCM of 12 and 15**:
   - Using the formula LCM(a, b) = (a * b) / HCF(a, b)
   - LCM(12, 15) = (12 * 15) / 3 = 60

2. **Bus Meeting Time**:
   - LCM of 15 minutes and 20 minutes = (15 * 20) / HCF(15, 20)
   - HCF(15, 20) = 5
   - LCM(15, 20) = (15 * 20) / 5 = 60 minutes
   - They will meet again after 60 minutes, at 9:00 AM.

3. **Simplifying 12/16**:
   - HCF(12, 16) = 4
   - Simplified fraction = (12 / 4) / (16 / 4) = 3/4

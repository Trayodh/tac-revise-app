## Page 1: Core Concepts & Foundational Rules

### Understanding Profit & Loss: The Basics

Profit and Loss are fundamental concepts in business mathematics, dealing with the financial outcome of transactions.

*   **Cost Price (CP):** The price at which an article is purchased.
*   **Selling Price (SP):** The price at which an article is sold.
*   **Profit (Gain):** When SP > CP. Calculated as SP - CP.
*   **Loss:** When CP > SP. Calculated as CP - SP.
*   **Profit %:** (Profit / CP) × 100
*   **Loss %:** (Loss / CP) × 100

### Rule 1: Profit Percentage (r%)

If there is a profit of `r%`:

*   **(i) Selling Price (SP) Calculation:**
    `SP = CP × (100 + r) / 100`
*   **(ii) Cost Price (CP) Calculation:**
    `CP = SP × 100 / (100 + r)`

**Example 1:** CP = ` 720, r = 16 2/3 %
`SP = 720 × (100 + 50/3) / 100 = 720 × (350/3) / 100 = 720 × 350 / 300 = ` 840`

### Rule 2: Loss Percentage (r%)

If there is a loss of `r%`:

*   **(i) Selling Price (SP) Calculation:**
    `SP = CP × (100 - r) / 100`
*   **(ii) Cost Price (CP) Calculation:**
    `CP = SP × 100 / (100 - r)`

**Example 2:** SP = ` 75, r = 12%
`CP = 75 × 100 / (100 - 12) = 75 × 100 / 88 = ` 85.22`

### Rule 3: Successive Profits or Losses (x% and y%)

When there are two successive profits or losses of `x%` and `y%`, the resultant profit or loss percentage is given by:

`Resultant % = [x + y + (x × y / 100)] %`

**Key Notes:**
*   Take positive for profit (+) and negative for loss (-).
*   If the final sign is positive, it's a total gain.
*   If the final sign is negative, it's a total loss.

**Example 3:** Successive profits of 10% and 20%.
`Resultant Profit % = [10 + 20 + (10 × 20 / 100)] % = [30 + 2] % = 32%`

### Rule 4: Two Articles Sold at Same Selling Price

When two different articles are sold at the same selling price, getting gain/loss of `x%` on the first and gain/loss of `y%` on the second:

`Overall % Gain or % Loss = [100(x + y) + 2xy] / [ (100 + x) + (100 + y) ] %`

*   Use positive `x, y` for gain and negative `x, y` for loss.
*   The sign of the result indicates overall gain (+) or loss (-).

**Special Case:** If there is a gain of `x%` on the first and a loss of `x%` on the second (same percentage):
`Overall % Loss = (x / 10)^2 %`
**Note:** In this specific scenario, there is **always a loss**.

**Example 4:** SP = ` 1188 each. Gain 10% (x=10), Loss 12% (y=-12).
`Profit/Loss % = [100(10 - 12) + 2(10)(-12)] / [(100 + 10) + (100 - 12)] %`
`= [100(-2) - 240] / [110 + 88] % = [-200 - 240] / 198 % = -440 / 198 % = -2.22%` (The example in the text uses a simplified formula `(x-y - xy/100)%` which is for a different scenario or a simplified approximation, but the general formula is more robust. Let's use the provided example's method for consistency with the text: `(x - y - (xy/100)) % = (10 - 12 - (10*12/100)) % = (-2 - 1.2) % = -3.2%`. This implies the text's formula is `x + (-y) + (x)(-y)/100` which is `x - y - xy/100`.)
So, `Profit/Loss percentage = (10 - 12 - (10 × 12 / 100)) % = (-2 - 1.2) % = -3.2%` (3.2% loss).

**Example 6 (Special Case):** SP = ` 990 each. Gain 10%, Loss 10% (x=10).
`Overall Loss % = (10 / 10)^2 % = 1^2 % = 1%`

## Page 2: Advanced Rules & Discount Concepts

### Rule 5: Two Items with Combined Cost Price (S) and Same Selling Price

A person buys two items for `S`. One is sold at a loss of `r%` and the other at a gain of `R%`. If each item was sold at the same price:

*   **Cost price of item sold at loss:**
    `CP_loss = S × (100 + R) / [(100 - r) + (100 + R)]`
*   **Cost price of item sold at gain:**
    `CP_gain = S × (100 - r) / [(100 - r) + (100 + R)]`

**Example 7:** S = ` 1300. Profit R = 20%, Loss r = 12%.
`CP_loss = 1300 × (100 + 20) / [(100 - 12) + (100 + 20)] = 1300 × 120 / (88 + 120) = 1300 × 120 / 208 = ` 750`
`CP_gain = 1300 × (100 - 12) / [(100 - 12) + (100 + 20)] = 1300 × 88 / (88 + 120) = 1300 × 88 / 208 = ` 550`

### Rule 6: Dishonest Dealer (False Weights)

If a trader professes to sell goods at cost price but uses false weights:

`Gain % = [(True Weight - False Weight) / False Weight] × 100`

**Example 8:** True weight = 1000 gm (1 kg), False weight = 960 gm.
`Gain % = [(1000 - 960) / 960] × 100 = (40 / 960) × 100 = 4 1/6 %`

### Rule 7: Cost Price of 'n' Articles = Selling Price of 'm' Articles

If the cost price of 'n' articles is equal to the selling price of 'm' articles:

*   **Profit %:** `[(n - m) / m] × 100` (when `n > m`)
*   **Loss %:** `[(m - n) / m] × 100` (when `m > n`)

**Example 9:** CP of 18 chairs = SP of 16 chairs (n=18, m=16).
Since `n > m`, it's a profit.
`Profit % = [(18 - 16) / 16] × 100 = (2 / 16) × 100 = (1/8) × 100 = 12.5%`

### Discount

**Definition:** The reduction allowed on the marked price of an article. Discount is always calculated on the **Marked Price (MP)**.
`Selling Price (SP) = Marked Price (MP) - Discount`

### Rule 8: Discount Percentage (r%)

If discount allowed is `r%`:

`Selling Price (SP) = MP × (100 - r) / 100`

**Example 10:** MP = ` 700, r = 10%.
`SP = 700 × (100 - 10) / 100 = 700 × 90 / 100 = ` 630`

### Rule 9: Marked Price Above CP with Discount

If a shopkeeper marks items at `x%` above the cost price and allows a discount of `y%`:

`Profit/Loss % = [x - y - (x × y / 100)] %`

*   A positive sign indicates profit.
*   A negative sign indicates loss.

**Example 11:** Marked 20% above CP (x=20), Discount 10% (y=10).
`Profit/Loss % = [20 - 10 - (20 × 10 / 100)] % = [10 - 2] % = 8%` (Profit)

### Successive Discounts (Discount Series)

### Rule 10: Single Equivalent Discount

Single discount equivalent to successive discounts `r1%, r2%, r3%`:

`Equivalent Discount % = [1 - (1 - r1/100) × (1 - r2/100) × (1 - r3/100)] × 100%`

*   This formula can be applied for more than three successive discounts.

**Example 12:** Two successive discounts of 20% (r1=20) and 5% (r2=5).
`Equivalent Discount % = [1 - (1 - 20/100) × (1 - 5/100)] × 100%`
`= [1 - (80/100) × (95/100)] × 100% = [1 - (4/5) × (19/20)] × 100%`
`= [1 - 76/100] × 100% = [1 - 0.76] × 100% = 0.24 × 100% = 24%`

### Rule 11: Selling Price with Successive Discounts

If marked price of an item is `x` and successive discount rates are `r1%, r2%, r3%`:

`SP = x × (1 - r1/100) × (1 - r2/100) × (1 - r3/100)`

**Example 13:** MP = ` 1600. Discounts 10% (r1=10), r% (r2=r). SP = ` 1224.
`1224 = 1600 × (1 - 10/100) × (1 - r/100)`
`1224 = 1600 × (90/100) × (1 - r/100)`
`1224 = 1600 × 0.9 × (1 - r/100)`
`1224 = 1440 × (1 - r/100)`
`1 - r/100 = 1224 / 1440 = 0.85`
`r/100 = 1 - 0.85 = 0.15`
`r = 15%`

## Page 3: AI Contextual Enrichment - Deep Dives & Conceptual Clarity

### The Core Relationship: CP, SP, Profit/Loss

At its heart, Profit and Loss is about comparing the Cost Price (CP) and Selling Price (SP).

*   **Profit:** When you sell for *more* than you bought for. `SP > CP`.
    *   `Profit = SP - CP`
    *   `Profit % = (Profit / CP) × 100` (Always calculated on CP)
*   **Loss:** When you sell for *less* than you bought for. `CP > SP`.
    *   `Loss = CP - SP`
    *   `Loss % = (Loss / CP) × 100` (Always calculated on CP)

**Conceptual Flow for Basic P&L:**
```
[Start with CP] --(Add Profit %)--> [Calculate Profit Amount] --(CP + Profit Amount)--> [Reach SP]
[Start with CP] --(Subtract Loss %)--> [Calculate Loss Amount] --(CP - Loss Amount)--> [Reach SP]
```
The formulas in Rule 1 and Rule 2 are simply algebraic rearrangements of these basic definitions, making calculations quicker. For instance, `SP = CP * (100+r)/100` is equivalent to `SP = CP + CP * (r/100) = CP + Profit`.

### Understanding Successive Changes (Rule 3 & 9)

The formula `x + y + (xy/100)` is crucial for understanding how percentages compound. It's not just a simple addition of percentages because the second percentage (`y%`) is applied to the *new* value after the first percentage (`x%`) has been applied.

*   **Analogy:** Imagine a price increases by 10%, then by 20%.
    *   Initial Price = `P`
    *   After 10% increase = `P * (1 + 10/100) = 1.1P`
    *   After 20% increase on the *new* price = `(1.1P) * (1 + 20/100) = 1.1P * 1.2 = 1.32P`
    *   Total increase = `0.32P`, which is 32%.
    *   Using the formula: `10 + 20 + (10*20/100) = 30 + 2 = 32%`.
*   **Sign Convention:** This formula is versatile. If `x` is a profit (+10) and `y` is a loss (-10), then `10 + (-10) + (10 * -10 / 100) = 0 - 1 = -1%`. This means a 1% loss. This is exactly what happens in Rule 9 and the special case of Rule 4.

### The "Always Loss" Scenario (Rule 4 Special Case)

When two articles are sold at the *same selling price*, and one has an `x%` gain while the other has an `x%` loss, there is **always an overall loss**.
*   **Why?** Because the `x%` gain is calculated on a *lower* CP (for the item sold at profit), while the `x%` loss is calculated on a *higher* CP (for the item sold at loss).
    *   Let SP = `S`.
    *   CP for item with `x%` gain = `S * 100 / (100 + x)`
    *   CP for item with `x%` loss = `S * 100 / (100 - x)`
    *   Since `100/(100-x)` is a larger multiplier than `100/(100+x)`, the CP of the losing item is higher. The absolute loss amount on the higher CP will be greater than the absolute profit amount on the lower CP, leading to an overall loss.
*   **Mnemonic:** "Same SP, Same % -> Sure Loss!"

### Dishonest Dealer Logic (Rule 6)

This rule is a clever application of profit calculation. The dealer *claims* to sell at CP, but by using a false weight, they effectively sell *less quantity* for the price of *more quantity*.
*   **Example:** A dealer sells 960g but charges for 1000g.
    *   The customer pays for 1000g.
    *   The dealer effectively *gives* goods worth 960g.
    *   The "profit" is the difference in weight (1000-960 = 40g).
    *   This profit of 40g is made on the *actual quantity sold* (960g).
    *   So, `Profit % = (40 / 960) * 100`.

### CP of 'n' = SP of 'm' (Rule 7)

This is a direct comparison of quantities.
*   If you buy `n` articles for `X` and sell `m` articles for `X`, and `n > m`, it means you still have `(n-m)` articles left after recovering your initial investment. Those remaining articles are pure profit.
    *   The profit is `(n-m)` articles.
    *   The "cost" for this profit calculation is the `m` articles that were sold to recover the initial investment.
    *   Hence, `Profit % = [(n-m) / m] × 100`.
*   Conversely, if `m > n`, you had to sell `m` articles to recover the cost of only `n` articles, meaning you incurred a loss on `(m-n)` articles.

## Page 4: AI Contextual Enrichment - Discount Dynamics & Strategic Tips

### Marked Price (MP) and Discount: The Retailer's Strategy

*   **Marked Price (MP):** The price displayed on the product, often higher than the CP. This is the price from which discounts are typically given.
*   **Discount:** A reduction on the MP. It's a tool to attract customers, clear old stock, or boost sales.
*   **Selling Price (SP) after Discount:** `SP = MP - Discount Amount`.
    *   If discount is `r%`, then `SP = MP × (100 - r) / 100`.
*   **Relationship:**
    ```
    [Cost Price (CP)] --(Mark-up x%)--> [Marked Price (MP)] --(Discount y%)--> [Selling Price (SP)]
    ```
    The ultimate profit/loss is determined by comparing the final SP with the initial CP. Rule 9 directly addresses this common retail scenario.

### Successive Discounts: Not Just Additive! (Rule 10 & 11)

Just like successive profits/losses, successive discounts are not simply added together. A 20% discount followed by a 10% discount is *not* a 30% discount.
*   **Why?** The second discount is applied to the price *after* the first discount has been given.
    *   Original MP = `100`
    *   After 20% discount: `100 - 20 = 80`
    *   After 10% discount on `80`: `80 - (10% of 80) = 80 - 8 = 72`
    *   Final SP = `72`. Total discount = `100 - 72 = 28%`.
    *   Using Rule 10: `[1 - (1 - 20/100) * (1 - 10/100)] * 100% = [1 - (0.8) * (0.9)] * 100% = [1 - 0.72] * 100% = 0.28 * 100% = 28%`.
*   **Mnemonic for Equivalent Discount:** "Multiply the remaining percentages, then subtract from 100."
    *   For 20% discount, remaining is 80%.
    *   For 5% discount, remaining is 95%.
    *   Equivalent remaining = `0.80 * 0.95 = 0.76` (or 76%).
    *   Equivalent discount = `100% - 76% = 24%`. (Matches Example 12)

### Strategic Tips for Problem Solving

1.  **Identify the Base:** Always be clear whether a percentage (profit, loss, discount) is calculated on CP, SP, or MP.
    *   Profit/Loss % is always on **CP** (unless specified otherwise).
    *   Discount % is always on **MP**.
2.  **Convert Mixed Fractions:** For percentages like `16 2/3 %`, convert them to improper fractions or decimals (`50/3 %` or `1/6` as a multiplier) for easier calculation.
3.  **Assume 100:** If no specific CP or MP is given, assume `CP = 100` or `MP = 100` to easily calculate percentages. This simplifies finding relative values.
4.  **Work Backwards:** Sometimes, you might need to find CP from SP and profit/loss, or MP from SP and discount. Use the inverse formulas (e.g., `CP = SP × 100 / (100 + r)`).
5.  **Chain Transactions:** For multiple transactions (A sells to B, B sells to C), calculate each step sequentially. The SP of one transaction becomes the CP for the next.

### Simple Interest (SI) - The Basics

Simple Interest is a quick and easy method of calculating the interest charge on a loan or deposit. It is calculated only on the principal amount, or on that portion of the principal amount that remains unpaid. It does not compound, meaning interest is not earned on previously accumulated interest.

#### Key Terms:
*   **Principal (P):** The initial sum of money borrowed or invested.
*   **Rate (R):** The percentage at which the interest is calculated per annum (per year).
*   **Time (T):** The duration for which the money is borrowed or invested, usually in years.
*   **Simple Interest (SI):** The interest calculated only on the principal amount.
*   **Amount (A):** The total sum of money at the end of the time period, including both the principal and the simple interest (A = P + SI).

#### Fundamental Formulae:

1.  **Calculating Simple Interest (SI):**
    $SI = \frac{P \times R \times T}{100}$
    *   Where P = Principal, R = Rate (in % per annum), T = Time (in years).

2.  **Calculating Principal (P):**
    $P = \frac{100 \times SI}{R \times T}$

3.  **Calculating Rate (R):**
    $R = \frac{100 \times SI}{P \times T}$

4.  **Calculating Time (T):**
    $T = \frac{100 \times SI}{P \times R}$

5.  **Calculating Amount (A):**
    $A = P + SI$
    *   Substituting SI: $A = P + \frac{P \times R \times T}{100} = P \left(1 + \frac{R \times T}{100}\right)$
    *   Alternatively, if A, R, T are known and P is required: $P = \frac{100 \times A}{100 + (R \times T)}$

#### Example Walkthroughs (from Primary Textbook):

**Example 1 (Finding Principal):**
*   Given: SI = ₹400, R = 10%, T = 1 month = 1/12 yr
*   Formula: $P = \frac{100 \times SI}{R \times T}$
*   Calculation: $P = \frac{100 \times 400}{10 \times (1/12)} = \frac{100 \times 400 \times 12}{10 \times 1} = ₹48000$

**Example 2 (Finding Time with Equal SI):**
*   **Part 1: Calculate SI for the second scenario.**
    *   P = ₹1000, T = 4 yr, R = 4%
    *   $SI = \frac{1000 \times 4 \times 4}{100} = ₹160$
*   **Part 2: Use this SI to find time for the first scenario.**
    *   SI = ₹160, P = ₹400, R = 10%
    *   $T = \frac{100 \times SI}{P \times R} = \frac{100 \times 160}{400 \times 10} = \frac{16000}{4000} = 4 \text{ yr}$

**Example 3 (Finding Principal from Amount):**
*   Given: T = 5 yr, R = 4%, A = ₹3120
*   Formula: $P = \frac{100 \times A}{100 + (R \times T)}$
*   Calculation: $P = \frac{100 \times 3120}{100 + (4 \times 5)} = \frac{100 \times 3120}{100 + 20} = \frac{100 \times 3120}{120} = ₹2600$

#### Important Rules and Formulae (from Primary Textbook):

**Rule 1: Sum becomes 'n' times in 'T' years**
If a sum of money becomes 'n' times itself in 'T' years at simple interest, then the rate of interest (R) is given by:
$R = \left(\frac{n-1}{T}\right) \times 100\%$

*   **Conceptual Note:** When a sum becomes 'n' times, it means the Amount (A) = nP. Since A = P + SI, then nP = P + SI, which implies SI = (n-1)P.
    Substituting this into the basic SI formula: $(n-1)P = \frac{P \times R \times T}{100}$
    Cancelling P from both sides gives: $(n-1) = \frac{R \times T}{100}$
    Rearranging for R: $R = \frac{(n-1) \times 100}{T}$

*   **Example 4 (Finding Rate for Doubling):**
    *   Given: n = 2 (double), T = 8 yr
    *   Calculation: $R = \frac{(2-1) \times 100}{8} = \frac{1 \times 100}{8} = \frac{100}{8} = 12.5\% \text{ or } 12 \frac{1}{2}\%$

**Rule 2: Principal and Rate from two different Amounts and Times**
If a certain Principal (P) amounts to $A_1$ in $t_1$ years and to $A_2$ in $t_2$ years at simple interest, then:

*   **Principal (P):**
    $P = \frac{A_2 t_1 - A_1 t_2}{t_1 - t_2}$ (Note: The textbook formula is $P = \frac{A_2 t_1 - A_1 t_2}{t_2 - t_1}$ which is incorrect based on the example solution. The example solution implies $P = \frac{A_1 t_2 - A_2 t_1}{t_2 - t_1}$ or $P = \frac{A_2 t_1 - A_1 t_2}{t_1 - t_2}$. Let's follow the example's calculation logic: $(1729 \times 2 - 1586 \times 3) / (2-3)$ which is $(3458 - 4758) / (-1) = -1300 / -1 = 1300$. So the formula should be $P = \frac{A_1 t_2 - A_2 t_1}{t_2 - t_1}$ or $P = \frac{A_2 t_1 - A_1 t_2}{t_1 - t_2}$. The textbook's formula is $P = \frac{A_2 t_1 - A_1 t_2}{t_1 - t_2}$ which matches the example's calculation if $t_1 < t_2$. Let's stick to the textbook's formula and ensure the calculation aligns.)
    *   *Correction based on example 5 solution:* The calculation in example 5 is $P = \frac{1729 \times 2 - 1586 \times 3}{2 - 3} = \frac{3458 - 4758}{-1} = \frac{-1300}{-1} = 1300$. This matches the formula $P = \frac{A_2 t_1 - A_1 t_2}{t_1 - t_2}$.

*   **Rate (R):**
    $R = \frac{(A_2 - A_1) \times 100}{A_1 t_2 - A_2 t_1} \%$ (Note: The textbook formula is $R = \frac{(A_2 - A_1) \times 100}{A_1 t_2 - A_2 t_1}$ which is consistent with the example solution. The example solution uses $R = \frac{(A_2 - A_1) \times 100}{P \times (t_2 - t_1)}$ after finding P, or more directly, $R = \frac{(A_2 - A_1) \times 100}{(A_1 t_2 - A_2 t_1) / (t_2 - t_1) \times (t_2 - t_1)}$ which simplifies to the given formula. Let's use the textbook's formula as provided.)
    *   *Correction based on example 5 solution:* The example solution calculates $R = \frac{(1729 - 1586) \times 100}{(1586 \times 3 - 1729 \times 2)} = \frac{143 \times 100}{4758 - 3458} = \frac{14300}{1300} = 11\%$. This implies the denominator should be $A_1 t_2 - A_2 t_1$. The textbook formula is correct.

*   **Example 5 (Finding Rate and Sum):**
    *   Given: $A_1 = ₹1586, t_1 = 2 \text{ yr}, A_2 = ₹1729, t_2 = 3 \text{ yr}$
    *   **Principal:** $P = \frac{A_2 t_1 - A_1 t_2}{t_1 - t_2} = \frac{(1729 \times 2) - (1586 \times 3)}{2 - 3} = \frac{3458 - 4758}{-1} = \frac{-1300}{-1} = ₹1300$
    *   **Rate:** $R = \frac{(A_2 - A_1) \times 100}{A_1 t_2 - A_2 t_1} = \frac{(1729 - 1586) \times 100}{(1586 \times 3) - (1729 \times 2)} = \frac{143 \times 100}{4758 - 3458} = \frac{14300}{1300} = 11\%$

**Rule 3: Sum becomes 'n1' times in 't1' years and 'n2' times in 't2' years**
At the same rate of simple interest, if a sum of money becomes $n_1$ times of itself in $t_1$ years and $n_2$ times in $t_2$ years, then:
$\frac{t_2}{t_1} = \frac{n_2 - 1}{n_1 - 1} \implies t_2 = t_1 \times \frac{n_2 - 1}{n_1 - 1}$

*   **Conceptual Note:** From Rule 1, we know $R = \frac{(n-1) \times 100}{T}$. If the rate R is constant, then $\frac{(n_1-1) \times 100}{t_1} = \frac{(n_2-1) \times 100}{t_2}$.
    This simplifies to $\frac{n_1-1}{t_1} = \frac{n_2-1}{t_2}$, which can be rearranged to the given formula.

*   **Example 6 (Finding Time for Multiple Times):**
    *   Given: $n_1 = 3, t_1 = 5 \text{ yr}, n_2 = 6$
    *   Calculation: $t_2 = t_1 \times \frac{n_2 - 1}{n_1 - 1} = 5 \times \frac{6 - 1}{3 - 1} = 5 \times \frac{5}{2} = \frac{25}{2} = 12.5 \text{ yr}$

### Understanding the Linearity of Simple Interest

Simple interest is fundamentally a linear function of time. This means the interest earned each year is constant, and the total interest grows proportionally with time.

**Visualizing Simple Interest:**
Imagine a graph with 'Time (T)' on the x-axis and 'Amount (A)' on the y-axis.
*   The starting point (T=0) would be the Principal (P).
*   As time increases, the Amount (A) will increase along a straight line.
*   The slope of this line represents the annual interest earned ($SI_{annual} = \frac{P \times R}{100}$).
*   The total interest (SI) is the vertical distance between the 'Amount' line and the 'Principal' line at any given time.

**Diagram Descriptor:**
A simple line graph showing 'Amount' on the Y-axis and 'Time' on the X-axis. The line starts at 'Principal (P)' on the Y-axis (at T=0) and slopes upwards linearly. The slope of this line is constant, representing the fixed annual interest. The total Simple Interest at any point 'T' is the difference between the 'Amount' at 'T' and the initial 'Principal'.

### Derivation of Key Formulas (Conceptual Walkthrough)

The core formula $SI = \frac{P \times R \times T}{100}$ is the foundation. Let's see how others are derived:

1.  **Amount (A):**
    *   By definition, Amount is Principal plus Simple Interest: $A = P + SI$.
    *   Substitute the SI formula: $A = P + \frac{P \times R \times T}{100}$.
    *   Factor out P: $A = P \left(1 + \frac{R \times T}{100}\right)$. This is a very useful form.

2.  **Principal (P) from Amount (A):**
    *   From $A = P \left(1 + \frac{R \times T}{100}\right)$, we can isolate P.
    *   $A = P \left(\frac{100 + R \times T}{100}\right)$
    *   $P = A \times \frac{100}{100 + R \times T}$ or $P = \frac{100 \times A}{100 + RT}$. This formula is crucial for problems where the final amount is given, and the initial principal is sought.

### Common Pitfalls and Key Considerations:

*   **Units of Time:** Always ensure 'Time (T)' is in **years**. If given in months, divide by 12. If given in days, divide by 365 (or 366 for a leap year, though usually 365 unless specified).
    *   Example: 1 month = 1/12 year; 73 days = 73/365 = 1/5 year.
*   **Rate (R) as Percentage:** The formula $SI = \frac{P \times R \times T}{100}$ already incorporates the percentage division by 100. So, if the rate is 10%, use '10' in the formula, not '0.10'.
*   **Simple vs. Compound Interest:** Simple interest is linear; compound interest is exponential. While this module focuses on simple interest, be aware that problems often contrast the two. Simple interest always calculates interest on the original principal, whereas compound interest calculates interest on the principal *plus* accumulated interest.

### Mnemonics for Formulas:

While the formulas are straightforward, a simple mnemonic for the core SI formula can be:
**"SIR, P.R.T. / 100"**
*   **SI**R = **SI**mple Interest
*   **P.R.T.** = Principal x Rate x Time
*   **/ 100** = (divided by 100)

For the derived formulas, it's often better to understand the algebraic manipulation from the core formula and A = P + SI, rather than memorizing each one separately.

### Real-World Relevance:

Simple interest is less common in modern banking for savings accounts or loans due to the prevalence of compound interest. However, it is still used in:
*   **Short-term loans:** Some personal loans or car loans might use simple interest calculations.
*   **Bonds:** The interest payments (coupon payments) on many bonds are calculated using simple interest.
*   **Inter-bank lending:** Short-term lending between banks often uses simple interest.
*   **Basic financial literacy:** It's the foundational concept before understanding more complex interest calculations.

## Page 4: AI Contextual Enrichment - Advanced Problem-Solving Strategies

### Strategy for "Sum becomes 'n' times" Problems (Rule 1 & 3):

These problems are common. The key is to recognize that when a sum becomes 'n' times, the Simple Interest earned is $(n-1)$ times the Principal.
*   If P is the principal, and A = nP, then $SI = A - P = nP - P = (n-1)P$.
*   Substitute this into $SI = \frac{P \times R \times T}{100}$:
    $(n-1)P = \frac{P \times R \times T}{100}$
    $(n-1) = \frac{R \times T}{100}$ (Cancelling P from both sides)

From this, you can derive:
*   $R = \frac{(n-1) \times 100}{T}$
*   $T = \frac{(n-1) \times 100}{R}$

**When comparing two scenarios (Rule 3):**
If $R_1 = R_2$, then $\frac{(n_1-1) \times 100}{t_1} = \frac{(n_2-1) \times 100}{t_2}$.
This simplifies to $\frac{n_1-1}{t_1} = \frac{n_2-1}{t_2}$, which is the basis for $t_2 = t_1 \times \frac{n_2 - 1}{n_1 - 1}$.
This proportional relationship is very powerful for quickly solving problems where a sum grows to different multiples over different times at the same rate.

### Strategy for "Amount in $t_1$ years and $t_2$ years" Problems (Rule 2):

This type of problem provides two (Amount, Time) pairs and asks for Principal and/or Rate.

**Conceptual Approach:**
1.  **Find the difference in SI:** The difference between the two amounts ($A_2 - A_1$) is the simple interest earned in the difference of time periods ($t_2 - t_1$).
    *   $SI \text{ for } (t_2 - t_1) \text{ years} = A_2 - A_1$.
2.  **Calculate SI for 1 year:** $SI_{annual} = \frac{A_2 - A_1}{t_2 - t_1}$.
3.  **Calculate SI for $t_1$ years:** $SI_1 = SI_{annual} \times t_1$.
4.  **Find Principal (P):** $P = A_1 - SI_1$.
5.  **Find Rate (R):** Once P and $SI_{annual}$ are known, $R = \frac{SI_{annual} \times 100}{P \times 1}$ (since $T=1$ for annual interest).

**Example 5 Revisited with Conceptual Approach:**
*   $A_1 = ₹1586, t_1 = 2 \text{ yr}$
*   $A_2 = ₹1729, t_2 = 3 \text{ yr}$

1.  Difference in SI: $A_2 - A_1 = 1729 - 1586 = ₹143$.
    Difference in Time: $t_2 - t_1 = 3 - 2 = 1 \text{ yr}$.
2.  SI for 1 year ($SI_{annual}$) = ₹143 (since the time difference is 1 year).
3.  SI for $t_1$ (2 years) = $143 \times 2 = ₹286$.
4.  Principal (P) = $A_1 - SI_1 = 1586 - 286 = ₹1300$.
5.  Rate (R) = $\frac{SI_{annual} \times 100}{P \times 1} = \frac{143 \times 100}{1300 \times 1} = \frac{14300}{1300} = 11\%$.

This conceptual method is often more intuitive and less prone to formulaic errors than directly memorizing the complex Rule 2 formulas.

### Handling Partial Payments or Multiple Investments:

Problems involving partial payments or multiple investments require breaking down the problem into stages or separate calculations.

**For Partial Payments:**
1.  Calculate interest on the initial principal for the period before the first payment.
2.  Add this interest to the principal to find the amount due.
3.  Subtract the payment to find the new principal.
4.  Repeat for subsequent periods and payments.

**For Multiple Investments:**
1.  Calculate SI for each investment separately using its respective P, R, T.
2.  Sum up the individual interests to find the total interest.
3.  If a relationship between rates or principals is given (e.g., one rate is 2% higher), set up equations.

**Example (Conceptual):**
A man invests ₹1000 at R% for 3 years and ₹1500 at (R+2)% for 3 years. Total interest is ₹390. Find R.
*   $SI_1 = \frac{1000 \times R \times 3}{100} = 30R$
*   $SI_2 = \frac{1500 \times (R+2) \times 3}{100} = 45(R+2) = 45R + 90$
*   Total SI = $SI_1 + SI_2 = 30R + 45R + 90 = 75R + 90$
*   Given Total SI = ₹390.
*   $75R + 90 = 390 \implies 75R = 300 \implies R = 4\%$.

This systematic approach helps in breaking down complex problems into manageable steps.

    Rate $R_2 = \frac{(n_2-1) \times 100}{T_2} = \frac{(3-1) \times 100}{12} = \frac{2 \times 100}{12} = \frac{200}{12} = \frac{50}{3}\% = 16 \frac{2}{3}\%$.
    Difference in rates = $R_1 - R_2 = 20\% - \frac{50}{3}\% = \frac{60 - 50}{3}\% = \frac{10}{3}\% = 3 \frac{1}{3}\%$.

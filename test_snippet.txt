The Human Heart consists of four main chambers. The upper chambers are called the left atrium and right atrium, and the lower chambers are called the left ventricle and right ventricle.

Deoxygenated blood from the body enters the right atrium through the superior and inferior vena cava. It then passes through the tricuspid valve into the right ventricle, which pumps it to the lungs via the pulmonary artery for oxygenation.

Oxygenated blood from the lungs returns to the left atrium via the pulmonary veins. It passes through the mitral (bicuspid) valve into the left ventricle. The powerful left ventricle then pumps this oxygenated blood to the rest of the body through the aorta.

This double circulation ensures that oxygenated and deoxygenated blood do not mix in the human heart.

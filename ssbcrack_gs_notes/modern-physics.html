📖 Complete Theory

The study of modern physics began when classical ideas could no longer explain phenomena at atomic scales. The journey starts with the Rutherford scattering experiment (1911), which used a thin gold foil and α‑particles. Most particles passed straight through, but a few were deflected at large angles, proving the existence of a tiny, massive, positively charged nucleus. This overturned the “plum‑pudding” model and gave birth to the planetary model of the atom.

Bohr’s Model (1913)

Niels Bohr introduced two bold postulates:

  1. Electrons move in circular orbits without radiating energy; only certain quantised orbits are allowed.
  2. Radiation occurs when an electron jumps between orbits, with energy difference ΔE = hν.

From these postulates, Bohr derived the Rydberg formula for hydrogen:

1/λ = R_H (1/n₁² – 1/n₂²)

where R_H = 1.097×10⁷ m⁻¹. The series (Lyman, Balmer, Paschen…) are just different choices of n₁.

Quantum Numbers & Electron Configuration

Four quantum numbers (n, ℓ, m_ℓ, m_s) uniquely define an electron’s state. The Pauli Exclusion Principle (1925) states that no two electrons can share the same set of quantum numbers, leading to the Aufbau rule and the periodic table’s electron‑shell structure.

Radioactivity

Radioactivity is the spontaneous emission of particles or electromagnetic radiation from an unstable nucleus. The three primary modes are:

Decay TypeParticle EmittedChange in A, Z
α‑decay⁴He nucleus (α)A‑4, Z‑2
β⁻‑decaye⁻ + ν̄ₑA unchanged, Z + 1
β⁺‑decay / ECe⁺ + νₑ (or capture)A unchanged, Z ‑ 1
γ‑decayHigh‑energy photonA, Z unchanged

The decay constant λ relates to half‑life (t½) by t½ = ln2/λ. Activity (A) = λN (Bq), where N is the number of undecayed nuclei.

Nuclear Binding Energy & Mass Defect

When protons and neutrons bind to form a nucleus, the total mass is less than the sum of individual nucleon masses. This missing mass (Δm) is converted to binding energy (B) via Einstein’s relation B = Δm·c². The semi‑empirical mass formula (Weizsäcker) approximates B for any (A, Z) nucleus and explains why iron‑56 has the highest binding energy per nucleon.

Nuclear Reactions – Q‑Value

A nuclear reaction is written as:

a + X → b + Y

The Q‑value is the net energy released:

Q = (m_a + m_X – m_b – m_Y)·c²

If Q > 0 the reaction is exothermic (energy out); if Q < 0 it is endothermic (needs input). This concept is crucial for designing nuclear weapons and reactors.

Fission vs Fusion

In fission, a heavy nucleus (e.g., ^235U) absorbs a neutron, becomes unstable, and splits into two lighter fragments plus 2–3 neutrons, releasing ~200 MeV per fission. In fusion, light nuclei (e.g., ^2H + ^3H) combine to form a heavier nucleus (⁴He) and a neutron, releasing ~17.6 MeV per reaction. Both processes are harnessed in Indian defence: Kriti‑K (fission) and the upcoming Thermonuclear Test (TM-2025).

Practical Applications in Defence

  • Radioactive tracers for leak detection in aircraft fuel lines.
  • Neutron generators for active interrogation of concealed explosives.
  • Isotope thermoelectric generators (RTGs) powering remote surveillance buoys.
  • Fusion research feeding into high‑energy laser weapons (DRDO’s “Laser‑Induced Fusion” project).

  • “U‑Pu‑Th – “U”niform “P”atrol “T”eam”: Quick recall of Indian fissile isotopes: ^235U, ^239Pu, ^232Th.
  • “Half‑Life = ln2 / λ – “L”ike “L”eading “2””: Visualize a soldier (λ) marching half the distance (ln2) each step.
  • “Q = (Mass In – Mass Out) × c² – “Q”ueen’s “C”rown”: The queen (Q) gets the “crown” (energy) from the mass difference.
  • “Fission = “F”or “F”orceful “F”racture”: Three Fs remind you that heavy nuclei split violently.
  • “Fusion = “F”ast “U”nity”: Two light nuclei unite quickly to form a heavier one.

⭐ Important Facts (Defence Exam Favourites)

  1. Rutherford’s gold‑foil experiment used α‑particles of 5 MeV energy.
  2. Bohr’s model correctly predicts the spectral lines of hydrogen only.
  3. α‑particles have a +2 charge and are the most massive charged radiation.
  4. β⁻ decay converts a neutron to a proton, emitting an electron and antineutrino.
  5. γ‑radiation carries no charge and can penetrate several centimeters of lead.
  6. Half‑life of ^238U is 4.5 × 10⁹ years.
  7. Critical mass of ^239Pu is about 10 kg with a reflector.
  8. Mass defect of ^12C is 0.099 u, giving a binding energy of 92 MeV.
  9. The most stable nucleus is ^56Fe (binding energy per nucleon ≈ 8.8 MeV).
  10. Q‑value of the reaction ^235U + n → ^236U* → ^92Kr + ^141Ba + 3n is ≈ 200 MeV.
  11. Neutron moderation in thermal reactors is usually done with light water.
  12. Fast reactors use liquid Na as a coolant because it does not moderate neutrons.
  13. “Cold fusion” claims have never been reproducibly demonstrated.
  14. India’s first nuclear test “Smiling Buddha” (1974) used ^239Pu.
  15. DRDO’s “KALI” project uses ^99mTc for medical imaging in field hospitals.
  16. Radioactive isotopes of iodine (^131I) are used in thyroid treatment for soldiers.
  17. Neutron capture cross‑section of ^235U is ≈ 585 barns for thermal neutrons.
  18. In a chain reaction, the reproduction factor k must be > 1.
  19. Fusion of deuterium and tritium yields 17.6 MeV and a neutron of 14.1 MeV.
  20. Radiation safety limit for occupational exposure is 20 mSv per year.

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • Using the mass of a neutral atom instead of the nuclear mass when computing Δm – leads to errors of ≈ 0.5 MeV.
  • Confusing λ (decay constant) with activity (A) – λ has units s⁻¹, A is in Bq.
  • Assuming β⁺ decay always emits a positron; sometimes electron capture occurs without a positron.
  • Neglecting the neutrino’s energy in β‑decay Q‑value calculations – the neutrino carries away ~½ of the decay energy.
  • Applying the Bohr formula to multi‑electron atoms – it works only for hydrogen‑like ions.
  • Treating half‑life as a fixed time for all nuclei – it is specific to each isotope.
  • Mixing up “critical mass” with “critical size” – the former is mass, the latter is geometry.
  • Using 1 u = 931 MeV instead of the precise 931.5 MeV – causes small but exam‑relevant errors.

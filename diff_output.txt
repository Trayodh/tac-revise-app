Definition and Standard Form</h4>\n  <p>A <strong>quadratic equation</strong> is a polynomial equation of degree two. In its standard mathematical representation, it is written as:</p>\n  $$ax^{2}+bx+c=0$$\n  <ul>\n    <li><strong>a</strong> \u2013 The <em>leading coefficient</em>; must satisfy <strong>$a \\neq 0$</strong> to preserve the quadratic nature. By substituting $t = |x|$ or $t = e^x$, you solve a standard quadratic first, but must then filter the real values of $x$ based on the constraints of the domain (such as $e^x > 0$ and $|x| \\ge 0$). Always verify the validity of your substituted variable!\n  </div>\n\n  <h4>2. Derivation of the Quadratic Formula from First Principles</h4>\n  <p>To deeply understand how parameters affect roots, we derive the quadratic formula by completing the square:</p>\n  $$ax^{2}+bx+c=0\\quad\\Longrightarrow\\quad x^{2}+\\frac{b}{a}x+\\frac{c}{a}=0$$\n  <p>Isolate the variable terms:</p>\n  $$x^{2}+\\frac{b}{a}x=-\\frac{c}{a}$$\n  <p>Add the square of half the linear coefficient, $\\left(\\frac{b}{2a}\\right)^{2}$, to both sides of the equation to form a perfect square trinomial:</p>\n  $$x^{2}+\\frac{b}{a}x+\\left(\\frac{b}{2a}\\right)^{2}=\\left(\\frac{b}{2a}\\right)^{2}-\\frac{c}{a}$$\n  <p>Condense the left-hand side into a binomial square:</p>\n  $$\\left(x+\\frac{b}{2a}\\right)^{2}=\\frac{b^{2}-4ac}{4a^{2}}$$\n  <p>Extracting the square root from both sides yields:</p>\n  $$x+\\frac{b}{2a}=\\pm\\frac{\\sqrt{b^{2}-4ac}}{2a}$$\n  <p>Isolating $x$ provides the standard quadratic formula:</p>\n  $$\\boxed{x=\\frac{-b\\pm\\sqrt{b^{2}-4ac}}{2a}}$$\n  <p>The radicand expression $\\Delta = b^{2}-4ac$ is the <strong>discriminant</strong>.

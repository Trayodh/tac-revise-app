**Page 1: Properties of Matter**

**1.1 Introduction**

* Properties of matter are characteristics that describe the state, size, shape, and behavior of a substance.
* Common properties of matter include:
	+ Physical properties (density, mass, volume, etc.)
	+ Chemical properties (reactivity, flammability, etc.)

**1.2 Physical Properties**

* **Density:** Mass per unit volume of a substance.
	+ Formula: ρ = m / V
* **Mass:** The amount of matter in an object.
	+ Unit: kg (kilogram)
* **Volume:** The amount of space occupied by a substance.
	+ Unit: m³ (cubic meter)

**Key Points:**

* Density is a measure of how tightly packed the particles of a substance are.
* Mass and volume can be used to determine the density of a substance.

**Example:** If a substance has a mass of 5 kg and a volume of 0.25 m³, what is its density?

Answer: ρ = m / V = 5 kg / 0.25 m³ = 20 kg/m³

**1.3 Chemical Properties**

* **Reactivity:** The ability of a substance to react with other substances.
* **Flammability:** The ability of a substance to catch fire and burn.
* Other chemical properties include:
	+ Corrosiveness
	+ Toxicity

**Key Points:**

* Chemical properties are determined by the arrangement of atoms in a substance.
* Reactivity and flammability are examples of chemical properties that can be hazardous.

**Page 2: Properties of Matter**

**2.1 States of Matter**

* **Solids:** Have a fixed shape and volume.
* **Liquids:** Have a fixed volume but not a fixed shape.
* **Gases:** Have neither a fixed shape nor a fixed volume.
* Other states of matter include:
	+ Plasmas
	+ Bose-Einstein condensates

**Key Points:**

* Solids have a crystalline structure, while liquids and gases have random molecular arrangements.
* The state of matter is determined by the temperature and pressure of a substance.

**2.2 Changes of State**

* **Melting:** A substance changes from solid to liquid.
* **Boiling:** A substance changes from liquid to gas.
* **Condensation:** A substance changes from gas to liquid.
* Other changes of state include:
	+ Sublimation (solid to gas)
	+ Deposition (gas to solid)

**Key Points:**
* Changes of state can be facilitated by changes in temperature and pressure.
* The process of melting is an example of a change from solid to liquid.

**3.1 Phase Diagrams**

* A **phase diagram** is a graphical representation of the states of matter of a substance.
* The x-axis represents temperature and the y-axis represents pressure.

**Key Points:**
* Phase diagrams can be used to determine the state of matter of a substance under different temperature and pressure conditions.
* The triple point is the point on a phase diagram where the solid-liquid, liquid-gas, and solid-gas lines meet.

**Example:** A substance has a triple point at (50°C, 100 kPa). What are the three phases of the substance at this point?

Answer: The substance is a solid, liquid, and gas, all existing simultaneously.

**3.2 Critical Temperature**

* **Critical temperature:** The temperature above which a substance cannot exist as a liquid.

**Key Points:**
* Critical temperature is the temperature at which the liquid-gas line on a phase diagram ends.
* Below the critical temperature, the substance can exist as a liquid.

**Example:** A substance has a critical temperature of 373 K. What is the state of the substance at this temperature?

Answer: The substance is a gas.

**Page 4: Properties of Matter**

**4.1 Properties of Solids**

* **Elasticity:** A solid's ability to return to its original shape after deformation.
* **Rigidity:** A solid's ability to resist deformation.

**Key Points:**
* Elasticity and rigidity are examples of properties of solids.
* Solids have a crystalline structure and do not compress significantly.

**Example:** A substance is elastic and rigid. What is the state of the substance?

Answer: The substance is a solid.

**4.2 Properties of Liquids**

* **Viscosity:** A liquid's resistance to flow.
* **Surface tension:** A liquid's tendency to minimize its surface area.

**Key Points:**
* Viscosity and surface tension are examples of properties of liquids.
* Liquids have a random molecular arrangement and do not have a fixed shape.

**Example:** A substance has a high viscosity and low surface tension. What is the state of the substance?

Answer: The substance is a liquid.

**Page 2: Applications of Modern Physics**

- **Calculating Area and Perimeter of Triangular Regions**:
  - From the given problem (Source 1, Question 92), we know that the sides of the triangular field are 41 m, 40 m, and 9 m.
  - Using Heron's formula for the area of a triangle: $A = \sqrt{s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)}$, where $s$ is the semi-perimeter, $a$, $b$, and $c$ are the side lengths.
  - Calculating the semi-perimeter: $s = \frac{a + b + c}{2} = \frac{41 + 40 + 9}{2} = 45$ m.
  - Plugging in the values, $A = \sqrt{45(45-41)(45-40)(45-9)} = 324.56$ m$^2$.
  - From this area, and the information provided about the average space each rose bed needs, we can determine the number of rose beds.
  - However, this calculation does not seem to use the rose bed space (900 cm$^2$). We might assume we're finding  a space and will then find number of rose beds with that space.

- **Rotational Kinematics**:
  - From the given problem (Source 1, Question 93), we need to find the diameter of the wheel that makes 452 revolutions to move 2 km 26 dm.
  - Converting the distance to meters: 2 km = 2000 m, 26 dm = 2.6 m.
  - So the total distance moved is 2000 m + 2.6 m = 2002.6 m.
  - Since the wheel makes 452 revolutions, we can find the circumference of the wheel using the formula: $C = \frac{2\pi r}{revolutions}$, where $r$ is the radius, $revolutions$ is the number of revolutions.
  - However, we cannot find r since the circumference can be in terms of diameter so we use $C=\frac{1}{2}\pi d$ 
  - $C= \frac{2002.6}{452}$, $c= \pi \frac{2002.6}{452}$
  - And then rearrange to find the diameter: $d= 2\pi \frac{2002.6}{452}$

- **Circular and Cylindrical Regions**:
  - From the given problem (Source 1, Question 94), we have a rectangular sheet of sides 18 cm and 14 cm, from which a semi-circular portion with smaller side as diameter is removed.
  - The area of the semi-circular portion is given by the formula: $A = \frac{1}{2}\pi r^2$, where $r$ is the radius.
  - First, we find the diameter and then the radius: Diameter = smaller side = 14 cm, Radius = $\frac{14}{2}=7$ cm.
  - Plugging in the radius, $A = \frac{1}{2}\pi (7)^2 = 38.48$ cm$^2$.

**Page 3: Relativity and Quantum Mechanics**

- **Introduction to Relativity**:
  - There are two main branches of Relativity: Special Relativity and General Relativity.
  - Special Relativity describes how objects move at speeds close to the speed of light.
  - General Relativity describes how gravity and mass curve spacetime.

- **Solving Relativistic Problems**:
  - We know that time and length are relative and that as an object approaches the speed of light time appears to slow down.
  - Using the formula $t=\frac{t_0}{\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}}$, where $t$ is the proper time, $t_0$ is the measured time, $v$ is the velocity, $c$ is the speed of light.

- **Introduction to Quantum Mechanics**:
  - Quantum Mechanics is a theoretical framework that describes the behavior of particles at an atomic and subatomic level.
  - Quantum Mechanics uses wave functions and probability amplitudes to predict the behavior of particles.

- **Solving Quantum Mechanical Problems**:
  - Using the Schrodinger Equation to solve for wave functions.
  - Calculating expectation values to find the average position and momentum of a particle.

**Page 4: Problem Solving Examples**

- **Example 1:**
  - A particle of mass $m=2$ kg is confined to a one-dimensional box of length $L=1$ m.
  - Find the wave function of the particle and the expectation value of its position and momentum.
  - Using the time-independent Schrodinger Equation to find the wave functions.

- **Example 2:**
  - A photon has an energy of 4 eV. Find the momentum of the photon.
  - Using the formula for the energy of a photon: $E=pc$, where $p$ is the momentum and $c$ is the speed of light.

- **Example 3:**
  - Find the radius of a ring that rotates at a rate of 10 Hz and has a linear speed of 50 m/s.
  - Using the formula for the linear speed of a point on a circle: $v=\omega r$, where $\omega$ is the angular frequency and $r$ is the radius.

- **Example 4:**
  - A car accelerates from rest to a velocity of 50 m/s in 5 s. Find the force applied to the car.
  - Using the equation of motion to find the acceleration and then using Newton's second law to find the force.

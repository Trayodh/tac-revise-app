**Page 1: Introduction to Electric Current**

- **Definition of Electric Current**: Electric current is the flow of electric charge. It is the rate at which electric charge flows through a conductor, typically measured in amperes (A).
- **Factors that Affect Electric Current**: Electric current is affected by three main factors:
  - Voltage (potential difference)
  - Resistance of the conductor
  - Type of conductor
- **Symbol and Unit**: The symbol for electric current is 'I' and the unit is ampere (A).

**Page 2: Formula and Calculations**

- **Ohm's Law**: The relationship between voltage (V), current (I), and resistance (R) is given by Ohm's law:
  V = IR
- **Resistance**: Resistance (R) is related to the length (l), cross-sectional area (A) and resistivity (ρ) of a conductor by:
  R = ρ(l/A)
- **Calculating Electric Current**: Electric current (I) can be calculated by rearranging Ohm's law to:
  I = V/R

**Page 3: Real-Life Applications of Electric Current**

- **Household Appliances**: Electric current is used in various household appliances such as electric lamps, refrigerators, and air conditioners.
- **Electric Power Generation**: Electric current is used to transmit and distribute power from power plants to consumers.
- **Transportation**: Electric current is used in electric vehicles, trains, and buses.

**Page 4: Types of Electric Current**

- **Direct Current (DC)**: Direct current flows in one direction only and is commonly used in batteries and electronic devices.
- **Alternating Current (AC)**: Alternating current changes direction periodically and is commonly used in household electrical systems and power transmission lines.
- **AC vs DC**: Differences between AC and DC including the use of generators, motors, and circuit designs.

**I. Introduction**

- Applied Biology is the application of biological principles to solve problems and improve human life.
- It involves using biological concepts to develop and implement solutions in various fields, such as healthcare, agriculture, and conservation.

**II. Key Concepts**

1. **Genomics and Proteomics**:
	* Genomics: study of genes and their functions.
	* Proteomics: study of proteins and their interactions.
2. **Tissue Engineering**:
	* Creation of functional tissue constructs using biomaterials and cells.
	* Applications in wound repair, organ replacement, and tissue regeneration.
3. **Synthetic Biology**:
	* Design and construction of novel biological systems or biological pathways.
	* Applications in biofuel production, bioremediation, and bioproduct synthesis.

**III. Important Vocabulary**

1. **Debauch**:
	* To destroy or damage something so that it is no longer considered good or moral.
	* Synonyms: abase, corrupt
	* Antonyms: ennoble, uplift
2. **Debilitate**:
	* To make someone very weak and infirm.
	* Synonyms: cripple, disable
	* Antonyms: strengthen, enable
3. **Decadence**:
	* Having low moral standards and behaviour.
	* Synonyms: corruption, debauchery
	* Antonyms: virtue, morality

**IV. Applications of Applied Biology**

1. **Biotechnology in Healthcare**:
	* Development of vaccines and gene therapies.
	* Diagnostic tools and personalized medicine.
2. **Biotechnology in Agriculture**:
	* Improved crop yields and disease resistance.
	* Development of biodegradable pesticides and fertilizers.
3. **Biotechnology in Conservation**:
	* Preservation of endangered species and ecosystems.
	* Development of bioremediation technologies for pollution clean-up.

**V. Important Concepts**

1. **Epigenetics**:
	* Study of gene expression and its relationship to environmental factors.
	* Applications in understanding disease etiology and developing targeted therapies.
2. **Gene Editing**:
	* Techniques for modifying genes, such as CRISPR/Cas9.
	* Applications in treating genetic diseases and developing gene drives for pest control.

7. Difference between ‘on time’, ‘in time’ and ‘in good
time’.
G ‘On time’ signifies absolutely right time, neither
before nor after.
e.g. – The flight is on time.
G ‘In time’ means you are not late for the event.
e.g. – I arrived just in time for my flight.
G ‘In good time’ means with comfortable margin.
e.g. – I arrived at the airport in good time.
8. Difference between at the beginning/at the end and in
the beginning/in the end
G ‘At the beginning’ means literally at the beginning.
e.g. – India scored fast at the beginning of the
match.
G ‘At the end’ means literally at the end.
e.g. – At the end of the book, you’ll find the
bibliography.
G ‘In the beginning’ (or at first) means in the early
stage. It implies that later there was a change.
e.g. – Sachin was nervous in the beginning, later
he settled down.
G ‘In the end’ (or at last) means eventually/after
sometime.
e.g. – At first he was scared, but in the end he
started enjoying.
9. No preposition is placed after the following verbs
when they are used in active voice. Order, request,
reach, attack, resemble, emphasise, accompany, discuss,
investigate, comprise, enter (come into), flee (a place),
join, affect, board, etc.
e.g.
– Our forces attacked on the enemy fort.
– We reached at the station on time.
– We ordered for a cup of tea.
– He resembles to his father.
– He accompanies with her wherever she goes.
– The police are investigating into the case.
– The teacher emphasised on
morality.
– I don’t want to discuss about the problem
with you.

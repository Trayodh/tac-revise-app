**Page 1: Modern Indian National Movement Structure and Timeline**

1. **Introduction**
    - The Indian National Movement, also known as the Indian Independence Movement, was a period of struggle for independence from British colonial rule.
    - The movement began in the late 19th century and continued until India gained independence in 1947.

2. **Early Nationalist Movements (1857-1885)**
    - 1857: The Indian Rebellion against British rule, led by Mangal Pandey and the Sepoy Mutiny
    - 1885: The Indian National Congress (INC) was formed by A.O. Hume to promote Indian nationalism and self-government

3. **Growth of Nationalist Movements (1885-1914)**
    - 1885: The INC held its first session, with Womesh Chandra Bonnerjee as its first President
    - 1895: The INC adopted the slogan 'Swaraj is our birthright' at its 13th session
    - 1914: The INC adopted the resolution of 'Home Rule' in all provinces

4. **Non-Cooperation Movement (1914-1922)**
    - 1919: The Amritsar Massacre led to widespread protests and demands for independence
    - 1920: Mahatma Gandhi launched the Non-Cooperation Movement, calling for Indians to boycott British goods and institutions
    - 1922: The movement was called off due to violence and clashes between Hindus and Muslims

5. **Civil Disobedience Movement (1930-1934)**
    - 1930: Gandhi launched the Salt March, a 24-day march from Ahmedabad to Dandi, protesting the British salt tax
    - 1932: The Government of India Act was passed, giving more powers to the Indian provinces but maintaining British control over key areas

6. **Quit India Movement (1942-1945)**
    - 1942: Gandhi launched the Quit India Movement, calling for immediate independence from British rule
    - 1945: The movement was suppressed, but the British government was forced to reconsider India's status as a dominion

7. **Independence (1947)**
    - August 15, 1947: India gains independence, with Jawaharlal Nehru as its first Prime Minister

**Page 2: Key Figures of the Indian National Movement**

1. **Mahatma Gandhi**
    - Born in 1869, Gandhi was a key leader of the Indian National Movement
    - He promoted non-violent resistance and civil disobedience to achieve independence
    - He was also a strong advocate for Hindu-Muslim unity and Indian culture

2. ** Jawaharlal Nehru**
    - Born in 1889, Nehru was a key leader of the INC and became India's first Prime Minister
    - He promoted socialism, secularism, and democracy in India

3. **Subhas Chandra Bose**
    - Born in 1897, Bose was a key leader of the INC and the Indian National Army
    - He fought for India's independence from British rule and promoted a more active and radical approach to the movement

4. **Veer Savarkar**
    - Born in 1883, Savarkar was a key leader of the Hindu nationalist movement
    - He advocated for Hindu-majority India and clashed with Gandhi and Nehru

5. **Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel**
    - Born in 1875, Patel was a key leader of the INC and played a crucial role in integrating different Indian states into the Indian union
    - He was also a key advisor to Nehru and helped shape India's foreign policy

**Page 3: Major Events of the Indian National Movement**

1. **The Sepoy Mutiny (1857)**
    - The Sepoy Mutiny was led by Mangal Pandey and other Indian soldiers against British rule
    - The mutiny was crushed, but it marked the beginning of the Indian National Movement

2. **The Amritsar Massacre (1919)**
    - The Amritsar Massacre was a brutal suppression of a public gathering in Jallianwala Bagh, Amritsar
    - The incident sparked widespread protests and demands for independence

3. **The Salt March (1930)**
    - The Salt March was a 24-day march from Ahmedabad to Dandi, protesting the British salt tax
    - The march was a key moment in the Civil Disobedience Movement and highlighted Gandhi's commitment to non-violent resistance

4. **The Quit India Movement (1942)**
    - The Quit India Movement was a key moment in the Indian National Movement, with Gandhi calling for immediate independence from British rule
    - The movement was suppressed, but it marked a decisive shift in India's struggle for independence

5. **Partition and Independence (1947)**
    - The Indian subcontinent was divided along communal lines, with India and Pakistan emerging as separate nations
    - India gained independence on August 15, 1947, with Jawaharlal Nehru as its first Prime Minister

## 1. The Stone Age Economy

The Stone Age marked the beginning of human civilization, a time when our ancestors learned to harness the power of stone to create tools that would shape their future. This period is divided into three sub-stages: the Paleolithic, Mesolithic, and Neolithic ages. Each stage saw significant advancements in tool-making, agriculture, and social structures.

### 1.1 Paleolithic Age

The Paleolithic age, often referred to as the Old Stone Age, was a time of hunter-gatherers. People during this era relied on tools made of rough-hewn stones, such as stones, and bones. Their diet consisted mainly of meat, fruits, and berries. As the climate changed, humans adapted to the new environment, developing new tools and strategies to survive.

### 1.2 Mesolithic Age

The Mesolithic age, or Middle Stone Age, represents a transition phase between the Paleolithic age and the Neolithic age. During this period, humans began to domesticate animals and practice agriculture. They used stone tools, such as microliths, pointed cresconic blades, and scrapers. The early evidence of animal domestication comes from sites like Adamgarh in Madhya Pradesh and Bagor in Rajasthan.

### 1.3 Neolithic Age

The Neolithic age, or New Stone Age, marked the beginning of settled agriculture and the domestication of plants and animals. Neolithic people used polished stone tools, such as stone axes, and developed a more complex social structure. They domesticated cattle, sheep, and goats and practiced crop rotation. The art of pottery was also developed during this period.

## 2. The Chalcolithic and Bronze Ages

As the Neolithic period progressed, humans began to use metals, starting with copper. The Chalcolithic age saw the introduction of copper tools, which led to the development of the Bronze Age. People during this era built their first cities and established trade networks. They also developed a more complex social hierarchy.

### 2.1 Chalcolithic Age

The Chalcolithic age saw the emergence of copper tools and early forms of writing. People domesticated animals, practiced agriculture, and developed small villages. They used painted pottery, such as black and red pottery, and venerated a mother goddess.

### 2.2 Indus Valley Civilization

The Indus Valley Civilization, also known as the Harappan civilization, is one of the four earliest civilizations in the world. This civilization dates back to around 2350 BC and spans a vast geographical area from the Indus River in present-day Pakistan to the Ganges River in northern India.

## 3. Economic Sectors

Economic sectors refer to the different branches of the economy, including primary, secondary, and tertiary sectors. The primary sector involves the extraction and production of raw materials, such as agriculture and mining. The secondary sector involves the manufacturing and processing of raw materials into finished goods. The tertiary sector involves the provision of services, such as finance, healthcare, and education.

### 3.1 Primary Sector

The primary sector is further divided into two categories: agriculture and mining. Agriculture involves the production of crops and livestock, while mining involves the extraction of minerals and metals.

### 3.2 Secondary Sector

The secondary sector involves the manufacturing and processing of raw materials into finished goods. This includes industries such as textiles, furniture, and machinery.

### 3.3 Tertiary Sector

The tertiary sector involves the provision of services, such as finance, healthcare, and education. This includes industries such as banking, hospitals, and universities.

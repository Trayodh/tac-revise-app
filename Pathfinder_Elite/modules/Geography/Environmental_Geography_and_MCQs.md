**Page 1: Environmental Geography Revision Notes**

**I. Introduction to Environmental Geography**

- Environmental geography is the study of human and physical environments.
- It involves the analysis of social, economic, and cultural factors that affect the environment.

**II. Key Concepts**

- **Environmental systems**: These are the interconnected components of the environment, including the atmosphere, hydrosphere, lithosphere, and biosphere.
- **Feedback loops**: These are positive or negative cycles of influence between the environment and human activities.

**III. Human Impact on the Environment**

- **Population growth and the environment**: Increased population leads to increased resource use, deforestation, and pollution.
- **Urbanization and the environment**: Urban areas experience air and water pollution, and loss of biodiversity.
- **Climate change and the environment**: Burning of fossil fuels leads to greenhouse gas emissions, contributing to climate change.

**IV. Sustainable Development**

- **Definition**: Sustainable development is meeting the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.
- **Principles of sustainable development**: Reduce, Reuse, Recycle.

**V. Case Studies**

- **Amazon Rainforest**: Human activities have caused significant deforestation and habitat loss in the Amazon Rainforest.
- **Globe and Its Environmental Challenges**: The increasing global population poses environmental challenges like deforestation and pollution.

**I. Atmospheric Environment**

- **Definition**: The atmosphere is the layer of gases surrounding the Earth.
- **Factors affecting the atmosphere**:
  * Temperature
  * Humidity
  * Atmospheric pressure
  * Wind

**II. Climate and Climate Change**

- **Definition of climate**: The general weather conditions of a region over a long period of time.
- **Causes of climate change**:
  * Burning of fossil fuels
  * Deforestation
  * Industrial activities

**III. Hydrology and Water Cycle**

- **Definition**: Hydrology is the study of the processes that control the movement and distribution of water on the Earth's surface.
- **Water cycle processes**: Evaporation, condensation, and precipitation.

**IV. Geomorphology**

- **Definition**: Geomorphology is the study of the processes that shape the Earth's surface.
- **Types of geomorphic processes**: Weathering, erosion, deposition, and tectonics.

**Page 3: Environmental Geography Revision Notes (continued)**

**I. Human Impact on Water Resources**

- **Water pollution**: Pollutants can affect the quality of surface and groundwater.
- **Water scarcity**: Increased demand for water can lead to scarcity.

**II. Human Impact on Land Resources**

- **Deforestation**: Loss of forests can lead to soil erosion and reduced biodiversity.
- **Land degradation**: Soil and water pollution can result from intensive agriculture.

**III. Natural Hazards and Disasters**

- **Definition**: Natural hazards are events or processes that pose a threat to human life and property.
- **Examples of natural hazards**:
  * Earthquakes
  * Hurricanes
  * Floods

**IV. Human Impact on Coastal Environments**

- **Coastal erosion**: Sea level rise and erosion can be caused by coastal development.
- **Marine pollution**: Plastic pollution and oil spills can affect marine ecosystems.

**Page 4: Multiple Choice Questions (MCQs)**

**I. Atmospheric Environment**

1. The main greenhouse gas responsible for climate change is:
A) Carbon dioxide
B) Methane
C) Nitrous oxide
D) Ozone layer

Answer: A) Carbon dioxide

2. The process by which water is transported from the ground to the atmosphere through plants is called:
A) Transpiration
B) Evaporation
C) Precipitation
D) Condensation

Answer: A) Transpiration

3. Which of the following is a natural hazard that can affect coastal environments?
A) Earthquakes
B) Hurricanes
C) Tornadoes
D) All of the above

Answer: D) All of the above

**II. Hydrology and Water Cycle**

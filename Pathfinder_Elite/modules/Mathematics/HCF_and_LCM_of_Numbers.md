Page 1: Definition and Basic Concepts

1.1 Definition of HCF and LCM
- Highest Common Factor (HCF): The greatest number that can divide two or more numbers.
- Least Common Multiple (LCM): The smallest number that can be divided by two or more numbers.

1.2 Basic Properties of HCF and LCM
- HCF is always less than or equal to the smaller of the two numbers.
- LCM is always greater than or equal to the larger of the two numbers.

Page 2: Finding HCF and LCM

2.1 Factors and Multiples
- A factor is a number that divides another number exactly.
- A multiple is a number that can be obtained by multiplying another number.

2.2 Finding HCF Using Prime Factorization
- Break down numbers into their prime factors.
- Take the lowest exponent of each common prime factor.

2.3 Finding LCM Using Prime Factorization
- Break down numbers into their prime factors.
- Take the highest exponent of each prime factor.

3.1 Word Problems Involving HCF and LCM
- Finding the number of days a group of people will take to complete a task, given their individual work rates.
- Finding the least amount of material required for a project, given their individual consumption rates.

3.2 Real-World Applications of HCF and LCM
- Finding the greatest common divisor of two or more numbers, which is essential in cryptography.
- Finding the least common multiple, which is essential in time and frequency calculations.

Example from the Source 1:

94. Sheela can stitch a suit in 2 days, while Meena can stitch a suit in 2 3 days. How many days will both take in stitching 30 suits?

- Calculate Sheela's 1 day's work: 1/2 suit
- Calculate Meena's 1 day's work: 1/2 + 1/3 = 5/6 + 2/6 = 7/6 suit
- Let the number of days both take in stitching 30 suits be x
- Sheela's x day's work: x * 1/2 suit
- Meena's x day's work: 30/x * 7/6 suit
- Total work done in x days by both: x * 1/2 + 30/x * 7/6 = 30
- Multiplying both sides by 12: 6x + 140 = 360
- Multiplying both sides by 7: 42x + 980 = 2520
- Subtracting both sides by 980: 42x = 1540
- Dividing both sides by 42: x = 36.95 (approximately)

Answer: The number of days both take in stitching 30 suits is approximately 37 days.

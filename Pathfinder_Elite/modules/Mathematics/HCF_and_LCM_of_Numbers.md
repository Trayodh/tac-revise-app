## 1.1 Definitions and Notations

The HCF of two numbers a and b is the greatest number that divides both a and b without leaving a remainder. It is represented by the notation hcf(a, b). On the other hand, the LCM of a and b is the smallest number that is a multiple of both a and b. It is represented by the notation lcm(a, b).

### 1.2 Theoretical Background

The HCF and LCM of numbers can be understood through the lens of prime factorization. When we factorize a number into its prime factors, we can use the concept of HCF and LCM to determine the greatest common factor and the least common multiple of those factors.

```latex
$$\text{hcf}(a, b) = \prod_{i=1}^n p_i^{k_{a,i}}$$
$$\text{lcm}(a, b) = \prod_{i=1}^n p_i^{\max(k_{a,i}, k_{b,i})}$$
```

### 1.3 Properties of HCF and LCM

One of the key properties of HCF and LCM is that they are related to each other through the following equation:

```latex
$$\text{hcf}(a, b) \cdot \text{lcm}(a, b) = ab$$
```

## 1.4 Real-World Applications

The HCF and LCM of numbers have numerous applications in various fields such as engineering, physics, and cryptography. For instance, in engineering, the HCF and LCM of numbers are used to determine the strength of materials and the efficiency of mechanical systems. In physics, the HCF and LCM are used to calculate the energy levels of particles and the frequencies of sound waves.

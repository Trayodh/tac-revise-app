**Page 1: Introduction and Properties of Natural Numbers**

**1.1: Definition and Importance of Natural Numbers**

- Natural numbers are positive integers, i.e., 1, 2, 3, 4, 5,....
- They are used to count the number of items.
- Natural numbers have various applications in mathematics and real-life situations.

**1.2: Properties of Natural Numbers**

- Closure Property: The sum or product of two or more natural numbers is always a natural number.
- Commutative Property: The order of addition and multiplication of natural numbers doesn't change their result.
- Associative Property: The result of adding or multiplying three or more natural numbers is independent of the order in which they are operated.
- Existence of Additive and Multiplicative Identity: 0 is the additive identity, and 1 is the multiplicative identity for natural numbers.
- Existence of Additive Inverse: For every natural number, its additive inverse is a negative integer, but natural numbers don't have additive inverses.
- Existence of Multiplicative Inverse: The multiplicative inverse of 1 is 1, but there are no other multiplicative inverses for natural numbers.

**Page 2: Properties of Integers**

**2.1: Definition of Integers**

- Integers are all positive, negative numbers, and zero, i.e.,....., -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, ....
- Integers can be classified into three main categories:
  1. Negative Integers: -1, -2, -3, ....
  2. Zero: 0
  3. Positive Integers: 1, 2, 3, ....

**2.2: Properties of Integers**

- Closure Property: The sum or difference of two integers is always an integer.
- Commutative Property: The order of addition and subtraction of integers doesn't change their result.
- Associative Property: The result of adding or subtracting three or more integers is independent of the order in which they are operated.
- Existence of Additive Inverse: For every integer, its additive inverse is also an integer.
- Distributive Property: a(b + c) = ab + ac

**Page 3: Properties of Rational Numbers**

**3.1: Definition and Importance of Rational Numbers**

- Rational numbers are all numbers that can be expressed in the form \(\frac{p}{q}\), where p and q are integers and q ≠ 0.
- Rational numbers have various applications in mathematics and real-life situations.

**3.2: Properties of Rational Numbers**

- Closure Property: The sum, difference, and product of two or more rational numbers are always rational numbers.
- Commutative Property: The order of addition, subtraction, and multiplication of rational numbers doesn't change their result.
- Associative Property: The result of adding or multiplying three or more rational numbers is independent of the order in which they are operated.
- Existence of Additive and Multiplicative Identity: 0 is the additive identity, and 1 is the multiplicative identity for rational numbers.
- Existence of Additive Inverse: For every rational number \(\frac{p}{q}\), its additive inverse is \(-\frac{p}{q}\).
- Existence of Multiplicative Inverse: The multiplicative inverse of \(\frac{p}{q}\) is \(\frac{q}{p}\).

**Page 4: Properties of Real Numbers**

**4.1: Definition and Importance of Real Numbers**

- Real numbers are all rational and irrational numbers, i.e., all numbers that can be expressed in the form a = {r, r ∈ Q}.
- Real numbers have various applications in mathematics and real-life situations.

**4.2: Properties of Real Numbers**

- Closure Property: The sum, difference, product, and quotient of two or more real numbers are always real numbers.
- Commutative Property: The order of addition, subtraction, and multiplication of real numbers doesn't change their result.
- Associative Property: The result of adding or multiplying three or more real numbers is independent of the order in which they are operated.
- Existence of Additive and Multiplicative Identity: 0 is the additive identity, and 1 is the multiplicative identity for real numbers.
- Existence of Additive Inverse: For every real number a, its additive inverse is -a.
- Existence of Multiplicative Inverse: The multiplicative inverse of a ≠ 0 is \(\frac{1}{a}\).
- Transitive Property: If a = b and b = c, then a = c.

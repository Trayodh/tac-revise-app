**1.1 Introduction**

Rational expressions are fractions that have polynomials in both the numerator and the denominator.

**Key Concepts:**

- **Like and Unlike Rational Expressions**: Like rational expressions are fractions with the same denominator, unlike rational expressions are fractions with different denominators.
- **Simplification of Rational Expressions**: Simplify rational expressions by cancelling common factors in the numerator and the denominator.

**2.3 Simplifying Rational Expressions**

Simplify rational expressions by cancelling common factors in the numerator and the denominator, or by rationalizing the denominator.

**Example 2.1**

Simplify:
a^2 + 3a + 2 / a^2 - a
**Answer and Explanation**


To simplify the rational expression, we need to factor the numerator and the denominator, and then cancel common factors.
a^2 + 3a + 2 = (a + 2)(a + 1)
a^2 - a = a(a - 1)

Now, we can cancel the common factors, (a + 2) and (a - 1)
The simplified expression is:

(a + 2)(a + 1) / a(a - 1)

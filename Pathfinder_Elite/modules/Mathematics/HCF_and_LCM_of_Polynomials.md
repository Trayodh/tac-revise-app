**Revision Note: HCF and LCM of Polynomials**

**Page 1: Introduction and Definitions**

1. **Definition of HCF and LCM of Polynomials:**
   - HCF (Highest Common Factor) of two polynomials is the highest power of a common factor that divides both polynomials.
   - LCM (Lowest Common Multiple) of two polynomials is the lowest power of a common multiple that is divisible by both polynomials.

2. **Examples:**
   - HCF is used to reduce the size of a problem, while LCM is used to combine two quantities and find the least multiple.

3. **Key Points:**
   - HCF and LCM of two polynomials are not unique and can have many different expressions.
   - Factors of a polynomial can be combined and rearranged using the properties of exponents.

**Page 2: Finding HCF and LCM of Polynomials**

1. **Method 1: Listing Factors**
   - List the factors of each polynomial and identify the common factors.
   - Find the highest power of each common factor and multiply them together to get the HCF.
   - Use the HCF to divide both polynomials and find the LCM by multiplying the remaining factors together.

2. **Method 2: Using Euclidean Algorithm**
   - Apply the Euclidean algorithm to find the GCD of the two polynomials.
   - Use the GCD to find the HCF and then use it to divide both polynomials to get the LCM.

**Page 4: Important Formulas and Key Concepts**

1. **Properties of HCF and LCM:**
   - HCF and LCM are reciprocal operations, meaning that LCM(a, b) = (a * b) / HCF(a, b).

2. **Rules for Finding HCF and LCM:**
   - To find the HCF of a polynomial, we find the highest power of each common factor and multiply them together.
   - To find the LCM of a polynomial, we find the lowest power of a common multiple that is divisible by both polynomials.

**Page 1: Gas Laws**

**I. Introduction to Gas Laws**

- Gas laws are a set of rules that describe how gases behave under different conditions.
- Key components: pressure, volume, temperature, and amount of gas (moles).

**II. Boyle's Law**

- States that at constant temperature, the pressure of a gas is inversely proportional to its volume (P1V1 = P2V2).
- P = Pressure (measured in atm or bar)
- V = Volume (measured in L or dm³)
- Example: If the pressure of a gas increases by 2 atm, what happens to its volume if the temperature is kept constant?

**III. Charle's Law**

- States that at constant pressure, the volume of a gas is directly proportional to its temperature in Kelvin (V1/T1 = V2/T2).
- Example: How will the volume of a gas change if the temperature is doubled and the pressure is kept constant?

**IV. Avogadro's Hypothesis**

- States that equal volumes of gases at the same temperature and pressure contain an equal number of moles.
- Example: If 2 moles of gas A occupy 12 L and 3 moles of gas B occupy 15 L at the same temperature and pressure, which gas has a higher density?

**V. Gas Constant (R)**

- Gas constant is used to convert between molar volume and pressure-volume work.
- R = 8.314 J/mol·K
- Example: Calculate the molar volume of a gas at constant temperature and pressure.

**VI. Ideal Gas Equation**

- PV = nRT
- P = Pressure (measured in atm or bar)
- V = Volume (measured in L or dm³)
- n = Number of moles
- R = Gas constant (measured in J/mol·K)
- T = Temperature (measured in K)
- Example: Calculate the pressure of a gas in a 2 L container if it contains 3 moles at 300 K.

**VII. Real Gas Behavior**

- Real gases deviate from ideal gas behavior due to interactions between gas molecules.
- Real gas equations take into account intermolecular forces and molecular size.

**Page 3: Solutions**

**I. Introduction to Solutions**

- Solution is a mixture of two or more substances.
- Components: solute (solute) and solvent (solvent).

**II. Concentration**

- Molarity (M): number of moles of solute per liter of solution (M = moles/L).
- Example: If 2.5 moles of sugar are dissolved in 2 L of water, what is the molarity of the solution?

**III. Solution Formation**

- Solution forms when solute particles dissolve in solvent particles.
- Key factors: temperature, pressure, and stirring.

**IV. Colligative Properties**

- Properties that depend only on the number of solute particles (not the type).
- Examples: boiling point elevation, freezing point depression, vapor pressure lowering, and osmotic pressure.

**V. Osmotic Pressure**

- Equation: π = nMRT
- π = Osmotic pressure (measured in atm or bar)
- n = Number of moles of solute
- M = Molarity (measured in mol/L)
- R = Gas constant (measured in J/mol·K)
- T = Temperature (measured in K)
- Example: Calculate the osmotic pressure of a 0.5 M solution of sucrose at 20°C.

**VI. Raoult's Law**

- States that the vapor pressure of a solution is directly proportional to the mole fraction of the solvent.
- Example: If a solution contains 10 g of solute in 100 g of solvent, what is the mole fraction of the solvent?

**VII. Colligative Properties (Cont.)**

- Additional examples:
  * Boiling point elevation (ΔT = Kb × m × c)
  * Freezing point depression (ΔT = Kf × m)
  * Vapor pressure lowering (ΔP = P° - P = Kb × m)

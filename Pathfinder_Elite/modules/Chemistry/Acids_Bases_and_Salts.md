**Acids, Bases, and Salts Revision Notes**

**Page 1: Introduction to Acids and Bases**

1. **Definition of Acids and Bases**
   - Acids: substances that donate hydrogen ions (H+), making them acidic in nature.
   - Bases: substances that accept hydrogen ions (H+), making them basic in nature.

2. **Arrhenius Definition (1887)**
   - Acids: substances that increase the concentration of hydrogen ions (H+) in a solution.
   - Bases: substances that increase the concentration of hydroxide ions (OH-) in a solution.

3. **Bronsted-Lowry Definition (1923)**
   - Acids: substances that donate a proton (H+).
   - Bases: substances that accept a proton (H+).

4. **Lewis Definition (1923)**
   - Acids: substances that accept a pair of electrons.
   - Bases: substances that donate a pair of electrons.

**Page 2: pH and pOH Calculations**

1. **pH and pOH**
   - pH: measures the concentration of hydrogen ions (H+) in a solution.
   - pOH: measures the concentration of hydroxide ions (OH-) in a solution.

2. **Relationship between pH and pH**
   - pH + pOH = 14 (at 25°C and 1 atm)

3. **Calculating pH and pOH**
   - pH = -log[H+]
   - pOH = -log[OH-]

**Page 3: Properties of Acids and Bases**

1. **Dissociation of Acids and Bases**
   - Acids: partially dissociate in water to produce hydrogen ions (H+).
   - Bases: partially dissociate in water to produce hydroxide ions (OH-).

2. **Strong Acids and Bases**
   - Strong acids: completely dissociate in water, e.g., HCl, HNO3.
   - Strong bases: completely dissociate in water, e.g., NaOH, KOH.

3. **Weak Acids and Bases**
   - Weak acids: partially dissociate in water, e.g., CH3COOH, HCN.
   - Weak bases: partially dissociate in water, e.g., NH3, H2O.

**Page 4: Salts and pH of Salts**

1. **Salts**
   - Salts are formed when an acid reacts with a base.
   - Examples of salts: NaCl, KNO3, CaSO4.

2. **pH of Salts**
   - Salts of strong acids and strong bases are neutral (pH = 7).
   - Salts of weak acids and strong bases are basic (pH > 7).
   - Salts of strong acids and weak bases are acidic (pH < 7).

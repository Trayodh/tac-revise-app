**Page 1: Introduction to Radioactivity**

* **Definition:** Radioactivity is the process by which unstable atomic nuclei lose energy by emitting radiation.
* **History:** Discovery by Henri Becquerel in 1896, followed by Marie and Pierre Curie.
* **Types of Radioactivity:** Alpha, Beta, Gamma.

**Page 2: Types of Radioactivity and Decay**

* **Alpha Decay:**
  * Definition: Emission of 2 protons and 2 neutrons from the nucleus.
  * Example: Uranium-238 (²³⁸U) decays into Thorium-234 (²³⁴Th).
  * Symbol: α
* **Beta Decay:**
  * Definition: Emission of electrons (β-) or positrons (β+).
  * Example: Carbon-14 (¹⁴C) decays into Nitrogen-14 (¹⁴N).
  * Symbol: β- or β+
* **Gamma Decay:**
  * Definition: Emission of high-energy photons.
  * Example: Iodine-131 (¹³¹I) decays into Xenon-131 (¹³¹Xe).
  * Symbol: γ
* **Half-Life (t1/2):** The time taken for the radioactivity of a sample to decrease by half.

**Page 3: Nuclear Reactions and Energy Changes**

* **Types of Nuclear Reactions:**
  * Nuclear fission: Splitting of an atomic nucleus.
  * Nuclear fusion: Combining of two atomic nuclei.
  * Artificial radioactivity: Creation of radioactive isotopes.
* **Energy Changes:**
  * Emission of radiation: Release of energy from the nucleus.
  * Absorption of radiation: Absorption of energy by the nucleus.
  * Radioactive equilibrium: Balance between emission and absorption of radiation.
* **Fission Reactions:**
  * Example: Uranium-235 (²³⁵U) + neutron → Barium-141 (¹⁴¹Ba) + Krypton-92 (⁹²Kr) + 3 neutrons.

**Page 4: Radioactive Isotopes and Applications**

* **Radioactive Isotopes:**
  * Definition: Isotopes that undergo radioactive decay.
  * Examples: Carbon-14 (¹⁴C), Iodine-131 (¹³¹I).
  * Applications: Dating of rocks (¹⁴C), cancer treatment (¹³¹I).
* **Applications of Radioactivity:**
  * Medical: Radioisotopes in diagnosis and treatment of cancer.
  * Industrial: Use of radioactivity in sterilization and detection of defects.
  * Scientific: Use of radioactivity in dating and studying the Earth's crust.

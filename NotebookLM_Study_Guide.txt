--- Acids, Bases & pH Indicators ---
Subject: Chemistry (NDA/CDS)
Chapter: Acids, Bases & Salts

## 1. Theories of Acids and Bases
- Arrhenius Theory : Acid: Releases hydrogen ions (H⁺) or hydronium ions (H₃O⁺) in aqueous solution (e.g., HCl, HNO₃).
- Base: Releases hydroxyl ions (OH⁻) in aqueous solution (e.g., NaOH, KOH).
- Bronsted-Lowry Theory : Acid: Proton (H⁺) donor.
- Base: Proton (H⁺) acceptor.
- Lewis Theory : Acid: Electron-pair acceptor (electron deficient, e.g., BF₃, AlCl₃, H⁺).
- Base: Electron-pair donor (has lone pair, e.g., NH₃, H₂O, F⁻).

## 2. pH Scale & Indicators
- pH represents potential of Hydrogen. Formula: pH = -log[H⁺] or pH = -log[H₃O⁺] .
- At 298 K: pH 7 is basic/alkaline.
- Indicators Table: Indicator Acidic Color Basic Color Litmus Red Blue Phenolphthalein Colorless Deep Pink Methyl Orange Red/Orange Yellow

## 3. Key Chemical Salts and Formulas
- Baking Soda (Sodium Hydrogen Carbonate - NaHCO₃) : Prepared by Solvay process. Releases CO₂ on heating. Used in baking and soda-acid fire extinguishers.
- Washing Soda (Sodium Carbonate Decahydrate - Na₂CO₃ · 10H₂O) : Used in glass, soap paper industries, and for removing permanent hardness of water.
- Plaster of Paris (Calcium Sulphate Hemihydrate - CaSO₄ · ½H₂O) : Obtained by heating Gypsum (CaSO₄ · 2H₂O) at 373 K. Used for plastering fractured bones and making toys.
- Bleaching Powder (Calcium Oxychloride - CaOCl₂) : Formed by action of chlorine on dry slaked lime [Ca(OH)₂]. Used as disinfectant for water and bleaching agent in textile industry.

--- Architecture ---
Subject: History (CDS/NDA)
Chapter: 7. Art, Culture & Heritage

## 🏛️ Indian Architecture
The structural heritage of India, from ancient cave temples to medieval Islamic monuments.

## 1. Temple Architecture Styles
- Nagara Style (North India): Platform base, curved tower (**Shikhara**), assembly hall (*Mandapa*), no boundary walls. E.g., Kandariya Mahadeva (Khajuraho), Sun Temple (Konark).
- Dravida Style (South India): Monumental gateway (**Gopuram**), stepped pyramidal tower (**Vimana**), temple tanks, high boundary walls. E.g., Brihadeshwara Temple (Thanjavur).
- Vesara Style (Deccan): Hybrid of Nagara and Dravida, characterized by star-shaped platform (*Jagati*). E.g., Chennakeshava Temple (Belur).

## 2. Indo-Islamic & Mughal Architecture
- Introduced the arch, dome, minaret, mortar, and geometric designs (avoiding human figures).
- **Delhi Sultanate:** Qutub Minar (Aibak/Iltutmish), Alai Darwaza (Alauddin Khilji), Tomb of Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq.
- **Mughal Architecture:** - *Akbar:* Red sandstone structures. Humayun's Tomb (first double-dome), Fatehpur Sikri (Buland Darwaza, Panch Mahal). - *Shah Jahan:* White marble structures. Taj Mahal, Red Fort (Delhi), Moti Masjid (Agra). Pietra Dura (stone inlay work) reached peak.

## 3. Stupas, Caves & Monuments
- **Sanchi Stupa (MP):** Commemorated by Ashoka. Relics of Buddha inside semicircular mound with ornamental gateways (*Toranas*).
- **Ajanta Caves:** 29 Buddhist rock-cut caves. Murals depicting Jataka tales.
- **Ellora Caves:** 34 caves representing Hinduism, Buddhism, and Jainism. Cave 16 contains the monolithic rock-cut **Kailash Temple** built by Krishna I (Rashtrakuta).

## High-Yield Formulas & Facts
- Nagara: Shikhara | Dravida: Vimana & Gopurams | Vesara: Star-shaped/hybrid | Indo-Islamic: Arch & Dome

--- Bahmani Kingdom & Deccan Sultanates ---
Subject: History (CDS/NDA)
Chapter: 4. Medieval India

## 🏰 Bahmani Kingdom & Deccan Sultanates
The major Islamic state of Deccan, which continuously warred with Vijayanagara over the fertile Raichur Doab.

## 1. Bahmani Kingdom (1347 - 1527)
- Founded in 1347 by **Ala-ud-Din Bahman Shah** (also known as Hasan Gangu) in revolt against Muhammad bin Tughlaq.
- Capital was initially Gulbarga, later shifted to Bidar. Mahmud Gawan (great prime minister) expanded power via administrative reforms.

## 2. Split into Deccan Sultanates
In the early 16th century, the Bahmani kingdom disintegrated into five independent sultanates:
- Adil Shahis of Bijapur: Founded by Yusuf Adil Shah. Famous for building the **Gol Gumbaz** (largest dome in India, featuring a whispering gallery).
- Nizam Shahis of Ahmadnagar: Malik Ahmad. Defended by Chand Bibi against Akbar's forces.
- Qutb Shahis of Golconda: Quli Qutb Shah. Famous for Golconda Diamond mines. Built **Charminar** in Hyderabad.
- Imad Shahis of Berar: Fathullah Imad-ul-Mulk.
- Barid Shahis of Bidar: Qasim Barid.

--- Bhakti Movement ---
Subject: History (CDS/NDA)
Chapter: 4. Medieval India

## 🙏 The Bhakti Movement
A medieval socio-religious reform movement preaching personal devotion to God as the path to salvation, bypassing priests and caste barriers.

## 1. Origins & Schools
- Originated in South India (7th-9th century) led by **Alvars** (Vaishnavite saints) and **Nayanars** (Shaivite saints).
- Spread to North India in the 14th century by **Ramananda**.
- Saguna School (Worshipped God with Form): **Mirabai:** Devoted to Krishna; composed Bhajans in Rajasthani/Braj.
- **Tulsidas:** Wrote *Ramcharitmanas* in Awadhi.
- **Chaitanya Mahaprabhu:** Bengal; popularized Kirtan (musical chanting).
- Nirguna School (Worshipped Formless God): **Kabir:** Disciple of Ramananda. Opposed rituals, caste system, idol worship. Expressed ideas in *Dohas* (couplets) compiled in *Bijak*.
- **Guru Nanak:** Preached monotheism and equality.

--- Buddhism & Jainism ---
Subject: History (CDS/NDA)
Chapter: 3. Ancient India

## 🧘 Buddhism & Jainism
Heterodox religious movements that arose in the 6th century BCE as a reaction to Vedic ritualism and caste rigidity.

## 1. Buddhism (Gautama Buddha: 563 - 483 BCE)
- Born as Siddhartha in Lumbini (Nepal) to Shakya Chief Shuddhodana. Renounced home at 29 (Mahabhinishkramana). Attained Nirvana at Bodh Gaya under a Pipal tree. First sermon at Sarnath (Dhammachakkapavattana). Died at Kusinara (Mahaparinirvana).
- Core Teachings: 4 Noble Truths (Arya Satyas), Eightfold Path (Ashtangika Marga), theory of no-soul (Anatta) and impermanence (Anicca).
- Buddhist Councils: 1st Council (483 BCE): Rajgir. Presided by Mahakasyapa, patron Ajatashatru. Compiled Sutta Pitaka (Ananda) and Vinaya Pitaka (Upali).
- 2nd Council (383 BCE): Vaishali. Presided by Sabakami, patron Kalasoka. Split into Sthaviravadins and Mahasanghikas.
- 3rd Council (250 BCE): Pataliputra. Presided by Moggaliputta Tissa, patron Ashoka. Compiled Abhidhamma Pitaka.
- 4th Council (72 CE): Kundalvana (Kashmir). Presided by Vasumitra/Ashvaghosha, patron **Kanishka**. Split Buddhism into **Hinayana** and **Mahayana**.

## 2. Jainism (Vardhamana Mahavira: 599 - 527 BCE)
- 24th Tirthankara (1st: Rishabhanatha, 23rd: Parshvanatha). Born at Kundagrama near Vaishali to Jnatrika Chief Siddhartha. Attained Kaivalya at Jrimbhikagrama. Died at Pavapuri.
- Core Teachings: Tri-ratnas (Right Faith, Right Knowledge, Right Conduct). 5 Vows (Ahimsa, Satya, Asteya, Aparigraha, Brahmacharya - added by Mahavira). **Anekantavada / Syadvada** (theory of pluralism/relative truth). Extremely strict non-violence.
- Sects: Split into **Digambaras** (sky-clad/naked, led by Bhadrabahu who migrated south during famine) and **Svetambaras** (white-clad, led by Sthulabhadra in Magadha).

## High-Yield Formulas & Facts
- 1st Buddhist Council: Rajgriha (Ajatashatru) | 3rd: Pataliputra (Ashoka) | 4th: Kashmir (Kanishka)

--- Cell Structure & Cell Division ---
Subject: Biology (NDA/CDS)
Chapter: Cell Biology & Genetics

## 1. Cell Theory & Classification
- Cell is the structural and functional unit of life, first discovered by Robert Hooke in 1665 (dead cork cell) and Leeuwenhoek in 1674 (living cell).
- Cell Theory : Proposed by Schleiden and Schwann (1838-1839). Rudolf Virchow added: "Omnis cellula-e-cellula" (all cells arise from pre-existing cells).
- Prokaryotic Cells : Lack a nuclear membrane and membrane-bound organelles (e.g., Bacteria, Blue-green algae). Possess 70S ribosomes.
- Eukaryotic Cells : Have a well-defined nuclear envelope and organelles (e.g., Plants, Animals, Fungi). Possess 80S ribosomes.

## 2. Vital Cell Organelles
- Mitochondria : Double-membraned powerhouse of the cell. Site of aerobic cellular respiration and ATP generation. Contains its own DNA and 70S ribosomes.
- Plastids (Chloroplasts) : Found only in plant cells. Kitchen of the cell, contains chlorophyll to perform photosynthesis. Possesses own circular DNA.
- Ribosomes : Non-membrane bound protein factories. Found free in cytoplasm or attached to Rough Endoplasmic Reticulum (RER).
- Lysosomes : Formed by Golgi apparatus. Known as Suicide Bags because they contain hydrolytic digestive enzymes that destroy worn-out organelles or the cell itself under stress.
- Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER) : RER has ribosomes and synthesizes proteins; Smooth ER (SER) synthesizes lipids and detoxifies poisons/drugs.
- Golgi Apparatus : Performs packaging, modification, and dispatching of materials.

## 3. Cell Division (Mitosis vs Meiosis)
- Mitosis (Equational Division) : Occurs in somatic cells for growth and repair. One diploid cell (2n) divides to produce two identical diploid (2n) daughter cells .
- Meiosis (Reductional Division) : Occurs in germ cells to form gametes. One diploid cell (2n) divides to produce four non-identical haploid (n) daughter cells . Features crossing over in Prophase I (Pachytene stage) which induces variation.

## High-Yield Formulas & Facts
- Powerhouse: Mitochondria (ATP)
- Suicide Bags: Lysosomes (hydrolytic enzymes)
- Protein Factory: Ribosomes
- Mitosis: Growth & Repair (2n -> 2n)
- Meiosis: Gamete formation & Crossing Over (2n -> 4 cells of n)

--- Chalcolithic Age ---
Subject: History (CDS/NDA)
Chapter: 2. Prehistoric India

## ⛏️ The Chalcolithic Age
The transition period from the Stone Age to the Metal Age, characterized by the joint use of Copper and Stone (Chalco = Copper; Lithic = Stone).

## 1. Key Characteristics
- Technology: First use of metals. Copper was the first metal used, followed by bronze. Stone tools (microliths) continued.
- Economy: Mostly rural communities. Practiced farming (barley, wheat, lentils) and cattle rearing. Worshipped the Mother Goddess and the Bull.
- Pottery: Characterized by **Black-and-Red Ware (BRW)**, painted with white designs.
- Burials: Burial practices varied. In Maharashtra, children were buried in urns under house floors in a North-South direction.

## 2. Major Chalcolithic Cultures
- Ahar-Banas Culture (Rajasthan): Sites like Ahar and Gilund. Houses made of stone and mud. Copper smelting furnaces found.
- Kayatha & Malwa Cultures (MP): Malwa pottery is considered the finest Chalcolithic pottery. Kayatha is situated on the Chambal river.
- Jorwe Culture (Maharashtra): Most extensive. Sites: **Inamgaon** and Daimabad. Highly settled, fortified villages with social stratification (chiefs' houses vs commoners). Transitioned to the Iron Age.

## High-Yield Formulas & Facts
- Chalcolithic: Copper + Stone | Jorwe Culture: Inamgaon | Banas Culture: Ahar

--- Cold War ---
Subject: History (CDS/NDA)
Chapter: 6. World History

## ❄️ The Cold War & Decolonisation
An era of ideological, military, and political tension between two superpowers (USA and USSR), coupled with the liberation of colonies in Asia and Africa.

## 1. Ideological Rivalry
- **USA (Western Bloc):** Capitalist, democratic.
- **USSR (Eastern Bloc):** Communist, authoritarian.
- **Military Blocs:** NATO (1949, led by USA) vs. Warsaw Pact (1955, led by USSR).
- **Proxy Wars:** Fought indirectly in Korea (1950-53) and Vietnam (1955-75).
- **Key Confrontations:** *Berlin Blockade (1948):* Soviets blocked West Berlin. Allied airlift broke the blockade. Berlin Wall built in 1961.
- *Cuban Missile Crisis (1962):* Closest the world came to nuclear war. USSR placed missiles in Cuba; USA imposed naval blockade. Resolved when USSR withdrew missiles.
- **Collapse:** Berlin Wall fell in 1989. The **Soviet Union (USSR) disintegrated** in December 1991, ending the Cold War.

## 2. Decolonisation
- The decline of European economic power post-WWII led to independence movements across Asia and Africa. India (1947), Indonesia (1949), Ghana (1957), Algeria (1962).
- **Non-Aligned Movement (NAM):** Formed in Belgrade (1961) by leaders like Nehru (India), Nasser (Egypt), Tito (Yugoslavia), Sukarno (Indonesia), and Nkrumah (Ghana) to remain neutral and avoid joining either US or Soviet blocs.

## High-Yield Formulas & Facts
- NATO: 1949 | Warsaw Pact: 1955 | Cuban Missile Crisis: 1962 | Collapse of USSR: 1991

--- Constitutional Development ---
Subject: History (CDS/NDA)
Chapter: 5. Modern India (Highest Priority)

## 📜 Constitutional Development
The evolution of India's administrative and legal framework through British parliamentary interventions.

## 1. Company Regulation Acts
- Regulating Act of 1773: First step by Parliament to supervise EIC. Governor of Bengal became GG of Bengal. Supreme Court at Calcutta set up.
- Pitts India Act of 1784: Created a **Board of Control** in London to manage political affairs, establishing dual control.
- Charter Act of 1813: Ended EIC's trade monopoly in India (except for tea and China trade). Allowed Christian missionaries.
- Charter Act of 1833: Finished all commercial activities of EIC. GG of Bengal became **GG of India** (Bentinck).
- Charter Act of 1853: Separated legislative and executive functions of GG's Council. Opened Civil Services to Indians.

## 2. Crown Administration Acts
- Government of India Act 1858: Abolished EIC. Indian administration transferred to **British Crown**. Created Secretary of State for India.
- Indian Councils Act 1909 (Morley-Minto Reforms): Introduced **Separate Electorate for Muslims** (legalized communalism). Satyendra Prasad Sinha joined Viceroy's Council.
- Government of India Act 1919 (Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms): Introduced **Dyarchy in Provinces** (Transferred/Reserved subjects). Bicameral legislature at Centre.
- Government of India Act 1935: Proposed All-India Federation (never formed). Abolished dyarchy in provinces, introduced **Provincial Autonomy**. Set up Federal Court, RBI, and divided subjects into Federal, Provincial, and Concurrent lists.

## High-Yield Formulas & Facts
- 1773: Supreme Court | 1833: GG of India | 1909: Separate Electorate | 1919: Provincial Dyarchy | 1935: Provincial Autonomy

--- Economic Impact of British Rule ---
Subject: History (CDS/NDA)
Chapter: 5. Modern India (Highest Priority)

## 💸 Economic Impact of British Rule
Colonial policies transformed India from a major exporter of finished goods into a supplier of raw materials and a market for British manufactures.

## 1. Three Land Revenue Systems
- Permanent Settlement (Zamindari System): Lord Cornwallis (1793) in Bengal, Bihar, Odisha. Zamindars became owners of land. Tax rate fixed permanently; if not paid by sunset on due date, land was auctioned (**Sunset Law**). Extremely exploitative.
- Ryotwari System: Thomas Munro and Alexander Reed (1820) in Madras, Bombay, Assam. Settlement made directly with the peasant (Ryot). Tax rate fixed at 50-60%, revised periodically.
- Mahalwari System: Holt Mackenzie (1822) in Punjab, NW Province, Central India. Settlement made with the village community (Mahal). Individual peasants shared responsibility.

## 2. High-Yield Economic Phenomena
- De-industrialization: Heavy tariffs on Indian textiles in Britain, coupled with duty-free entry of British machine-made goods into India, destroyed India's famous handloom industries, leading to mass "ruralisation".
- Drain of Wealth Theory: Pioneered by **Dadabhai Naoroji** in his book *Poverty and Un-British Rule in India* (1867). He demonstrated how Indian tax revenues and resources were sent to Britain (via home charges, military expenses, profits) without any equivalent material return, impoverishing India.
- Commercialisation of Agriculture: Forced cultivation of cash crops (Indigo, Opium, Tea, Cotton) instead of foodgrains, leading to frequent, devastating famines (e.g., Great Bengal Famine of 1943).

## High-Yield Formulas & Facts
- Permanent Settlement: Cornwallis (Bengal) | Ryotwari: Munro (Madras) | Mahalwari: Mackenzie (North) | Drain Theory: Naoroji

## 🚢 European Entry and British Expansion
The search for spices led European powers to establish trading outposts in India, which eventually turned into territorial ambitions.

## 1. Arrival Sequence (PEDDF)
- **Portuguese (1498):** Vasco da Gama landed at Calicut. Francisco de Almeida (Blue Water Policy). Alfonso de Albuquerque captured Goa (1510) from Bijapur, making it the capital. Introduced printing press (1556) and tobacco.

- **French (1664):** Founded by Colbert under Louis XIV. First factory at Surat (1667). François Martin founded Pondicherry (1674). Carnatic Wars (Anglo-French rivalry) crushed French power, culminating in their defeat at the **Battle of Wandiwash (1760)**.

## 2. Foundation of British Rule
- Battle of Plassey (23 June 1757): Robert Clive defeated Nawab Siraj-ud-Daulah of Bengal. Clive bribed army chief **Mir Jafar**. Established EIC as a political force.
- Battle of Buxar (22 Oct 1764): Hector Munro defeated joint forces of Nawab Mir Qasim of Bengal, Nawab Shuja-ud-Daulah of Awadh, and Mughal Emperor Shah Alam II. Led to **Treaty of Allahabad (1765)** where Clive secured **Diwani Rights** (tax collection rights) of Bengal, Bihar, and Odisha, starting the Dual Government.

--- Freedom Movement ---
Subject: History (CDS/NDA)
Chapter: 5. Modern India (Highest Priority)

## ⚔️ Indian National Movement
The organized struggle for independence from British rule, divided into Moderate, Extremist, and Gandhian phases.

## 1. Early Phases
- Indian National Congress (INC) Founded (1885): Founded by retired civil servant **A.O. Hume**. First session held at Bombay (W.C. Bonnerjee President, attended by 72 delegates).
- Moderate Phase (1885-1905): Led by Naoroji, Gokhale, Banerjea. Believed in constitutional methods (Triple Ps: Prayers, Petitions, Protests).
- Extremist Phase (1905-1919): Led by **Lal-Bal-Pal** (Lajpat Rai, Tilak, Pal) and Aurobindo Ghosh. Preached self-reliance, boycott, and passive resistance. *Partition of Bengal (1905):* Announced by Lord Curzon. Triggered the **Swadeshi and Boycott Movement**.
- *Surat Split (1907):* Congress split into Moderates and Extremists over methods.
- *Lucknow Pact (1916):* Moderates/Extremists reunited. INC and Muslim League signed a joint pact.
- *Home Rule League (1916):* Tilak and Annie Besant started separate leagues demanding home rule.

## 2. Gandhian Era (1919 - 1947)
- Gandhi returned from South Africa on **9 January 1915** (celebrated as Pravasi Bharatiya Divas).
- Early Satyagrahas: Champaran (Bihar, 1917 - first civil disobedience against Indigo Tinkathia system), Ahmedabad Mill Strike (1918 - first hunger strike), Kheda (1918 - first non-cooperation). Mnemonic: **CAK** (Champaran, Ahmedabad, Kheda).
- Rowlatt Act & Jallianwala Bagh (1919): Rowlatt Act allowed detention without trial. On 13 April 1919, General Dyer opened fire on a peaceful assembly at Jallianwala Bagh, Amritsar. Tagore renounced his Knighthood.
- Non-Cooperation Movement (1920-1922): Launched alongside Khilafat movement. Called off by Gandhi in Feb 1922 after the **Chauri Chaura incident** (where a mob burned a police station, killing 22 policemen).
- Quit India Movement (1942): Launched after the failure of the Cripps Mission. Gandhi gave the call **"Do or Die"** at Gowalia Tank, Bombay on 8 August 1942. Leaders were arrested overnight under *Operation Zero Hour*.

--- Fundamental Rights (Art 12-35) ---
Subject: Indian Polity (CDS/NDA)

## 1. Classification of Fundamental Rights
Part III of the Constitution is called the **Magna Carta of India**.
- Right to Equality (Articles 14-18) : Art 14: Equality before law & Equal protection of laws.
- Art 15: Prohibition of discrimination.
- Art 16: Equality of opportunity in public employment.
- Art 17: Abolition of Untouchability.
- Art 18: Abolition of Titles.
- Right to Freedom (Articles 19-22) : Art 19: Guarantees 6 democratic freedoms (speech, assembly, association, movement, residence, profession).
- Art 20: Protection in respect of conviction for offences (no ex-post facto law, no double jeopardy, no self-incrimination).
- Art 21: Protection of Life and Personal Liberty.

## 2. The Five Prerogative Writs
- Habeas Corpus ('To have the body of'): Issued to release a person unlawfully detained. Can be issued against both public and private entities.
- Mandamus ('We command'): Issued to direct a public authority to perform a duty they have failed or refused to do. Cannot be issued against the President, Governors, or private individuals.
- Prohibition ('To forbid'): Issued by a higher court to a lower court or quasi-judicial body to prevent it from exceeding its jurisdiction. (Preventive only).
- Certiorari ('To be certified'): Issued to quash the order of a lower court or transfer case to itself. (Preventive and Curative).
- Quo Warranto ('By what authority'): Issued to inquire into the legality of the claim of a person to a public office, preventing illegal usurpation.

--- Governor-Generals & Viceroys ---
Subject: History (CDS/NDA)
Chapter: 5. Modern India (Highest Priority)

## 🎩 Governor-Generals & Viceroys
The administrative heads of British rule in India who designed expansionist and reformist policies.

## 1. Governor-Generals of Bengal (1773 - 1833)
- Warren Hastings (1773-1785): First GG of Bengal. Ended Dual Government. Established civil/criminal courts. Impeached in London (later acquitted).
- Lord Cornwallis (1786-1793): Father of Civil Services in India. Introduced **Permanent Settlement (Zamindari)** in Bengal (1793). Created Cornwallis Code (separating judiciary and executive).
- Lord Wellesley (1798-1805): Introduced **Subsidiary Alliance System** (Hyderabad first to sign in 1798). Called himself "Bengal Tiger".

## 2. Governor-Generals of India (1833 - 1858)
- Lord William Bentinck (1828-1835): First GG of India. Social Reforms: **Abolished Sati (1829, Regulation XVII)** with help of Raja Ram Mohan Roy. Suppressed Thuggee cult. Introduced English as medium of higher education (Macaulay's Minute 1835).

## 3. Viceroys of India (1858 - 1947)
- Lord Canning (1856-1862): First Viceroy. Suppressed 1857 Revolt. Ended East India Company rule (GoI Act 1858). Abolished Doctrine of Lapse. Introduced portfolio system.
- Lord Lytton (1876-1880): Vernacular Press Act (1878 - censored local papers), Arms Act (1878). Grand Delhi Durbar (1877) amid famine.

## 🌊 Indus Valley Civilization (IVC)
Also known as the Harappan Civilization. India's first urban revolution, flourishing in the Bronze Age (~2500 BCE - 1900 BCE).

## 1. Discovery & Chronology
- First discovered in 1921 at **Harappa** by **Daya Ram Sahni** under John Marshall.
- **Mohenjo-daro** discovered in 1922 by **R.D. Banerji**.
- Dated via Carbon-14. Mature Phase: 2600 - 1900 BCE.

## 2. Town Planning & Drainage (Exhaustively Tested)
- Grid System: Streets cut each other at right angles, dividing the city into rectangular blocks.
- Citadel & Lower Town: Most cities were divided into a raised Citadel (west, housing public buildings, elites) and a Lower Town (east, housing commoners). Exception: **Dholavira** had three divisions (Citadel, Middle, Lower).
- Drainage System: Advanced underground covered drains made of burnt bricks, with manholes/soak pits at regular intervals. House drains connected directly to main street drains.
- Building Material: Standardized burnt bricks with a ratio of **4:2:1** (length:breadth:thickness).

--- Limits & Continuity ---
Subject: Mathematics (NDA/CDS)
Chapter: Calculus

## 1. Standard Limits
These limits resolve common indeterminate forms instantly:
- lim (x→0) sin(x)/x = 1
- lim (x→0) tan(x)/x = 1
- lim (x→0) (e^x - 1)/x = 1
- lim (x→0) ln(1 + x)/x = 1
- lim (x→a) (xⁿ - aⁿ)/(x - a) = n·aⁿ⁻¹
- lim (x→0) (1 + x)^(1/x) = e
- lim (x→∞) (1 + 1/x)ˣ = e

## 2. L'Hopital's Rule
For limits resulting in indeterminate forms like 0/0 or ∞/∞ :
- Differentiate numerator and denominator separately: lim f(x)/g(x) = lim f'(x)/g'(x) .
- Repeat if the result is still indeterminate and functions remain differentiable.

## 3. Continuity & Differentiability
- f(x) is continuous at x = c if: lim (x→c⁻) f(x) = lim (x→c⁺) f(x) = f(c) (LHL = RHL = Value).
- f(x) is differentiable at x = c if: Left Hand Derivative (LHD) = Right Hand Derivative (RHD).
- Important Property : Differentiability ⇒ Continuity. However, Continuity does NOT imply Differentiability (e.g., f(x) = |x| is continuous at x = 0, but not differentiable because of a sharp corner).

- lim(x→a) (xⁿ - aⁿ)/(x - a) = n·aⁿ⁻¹

--- Mauryan Period ---
Subject: History (CDS/NDA)
Chapter: 3. Ancient India

## 🛡️ The Mauryan Empire (322 - 185 BCE)
India's first historical empire, establishing central control over most of the subcontinent.

## 1. Rulers & Triumphs
- Chandragupta Maurya (322 - 298 BCE): Overthrew Dhana Nanda with Chanakya's guidance. Defeated Seleucus Nicator (305 BCE), acquiring Baluchistan and Afghanistan. Megasthenes visited his court. Adopted Jainism under Bhadrabahu and died via *Sallekhana* (fasting) at Shravanabelagola.
- Bindusara (298 - 273 BCE): Called *Amitraghata* (slayer of enemies). Received ambassadors from Syria (Deimachus) and Egypt (Dionysius). Patronised the Ajivika sect.
- Ashoka (273 - 232 BCE): Coronation in 269 BCE. Kalinga War (261 BCE, detailed in **Major Rock Edict XIII**) caused immense casualties, prompting Ashoka to abandon war (*Bherighosha*) and adopt Dhamma (*Dhammaghosha*). Sent Buddhist missions to Sri Lanka (Mahinda/Sanghamitta).

## 2. Ashokan Edicts & Dhamma
- Edicts are classified into Major Rock Edicts (14), Minor Rock Edicts, Major Pillar Edicts (7), and Cave Inscriptions. Written mostly in Prakrit (Brahmi script).
- **Major Rock Edict XIII:** Mentions Kalinga War and contemporary Hellenic kings (Antiochus, Ptolemy).
- **Rummindei Pillar Inscription:** Marks Buddha's birthplace (Lumbini) and records Ashoka reducing land tax (*Bhaga*) to 1/8th.

## 3. Administration & Economy
- Highly centralized bureaucracy. King assisted by *Mantriparishad*. 7 limbs of state defined in Kautilya's **Saptanga Theory** (Swami, Amatya, Janapada, Durga, Kosha, Danda, Mitra).
- Spy network of *Gudhapurushas* (Sanstha - stationary, Sanchari - wandering).
- Tax: Land revenue (*Bhaga*) was usually 1/6th of produce. Punched-marked silver coins (*Panas*) were widely circulated.

--- Newton's Laws of Motion ---
Subject: Physics (NDA/CDS)
Chapter: Mechanics & Motion

## 1. Newton's First Law (Law of Inertia)
- A body continues in its state of rest or uniform motion in a straight line unless compelled by an external unbalanced force.
- Inertia is the inherent property of a body to resist change. Measured by **mass** (greater mass = greater inertia).
- Types: Inertia of Rest (passengers fall backward when bus starts), Inertia of Motion (passengers fall forward when brakes applied), Inertia of Direction (umbrella protects from rain).

## 2. Newton's Second Law (Law of Force)
- The rate of change of momentum of a body is directly proportional to the applied force and takes place in the direction of the force.
- Momentum: p = mv (vector quantity, unit: kg m/s).
- Mathematical derivation: F = dp/dt = d(mv)/dt = m(dv/dt) = **ma** (Force = mass × acceleration).
- Unit of Force: Newton (N). 1 N = 1 kg m/s² = 10⁵ Dynes.

## 3. Newton's Third Law (Law of Action-Reaction)
- To every action, there is always an equal and opposite reaction. Action and reaction act on **two different bodies**.
- Examples: Recoil of a gun (backward force on shoulder), swimming (pushing water backward), flight of rockets (exhaust gases push downward, rocket moves up).

## 4. Friction and Momentum Conservation
- Law of Conservation of Linear Momentum : If no external force acts on a system, the total linear momentum remains constant. (m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂).
- Friction : The opposing force that comes into play when a body moves or tries to move over another surface. Formula: f = μN (where μ is coefficient of friction, N is normal reaction).
- Order of friction: **Static Friction > Limiting Friction > Kinetic Friction > Rolling Friction**. Rolling friction is the smallest.

## High-Yield Formulas & Facts
- F = ma
- Momentum: p = mv
- Friction: f = μN
- Equations of Motion: v = u + at, s = ut + 0.5at², v² - u² = 2as

--- Parliament of India (Art 79-122) ---
Subject: Indian Polity (CDS/NDA)
Chapter: Union Government

## 1. Composition of Parliament
Parliament consists of the **President**, the **Council of States (Rajya Sabha)**, and the **House of the People (Lok Sabha)**.
- Rajya Sabha (Upper House) : Max strength: 250 (238 elected from States/UTs, 12 nominated by President from Art, Literature, Science, Social Service).
- It is a **permanent body** and not subject to dissolution.
- Members have a **6-year term**, with **one-third** retiring every second year.
- Lok Sabha (Lower House) : Max strength: 550 (530 representing States, 20 representing Union Territories). (Anglo-Indian reserved seats abolished by 104th Amendment).
- Normal term: **5 years**, can be dissolved earlier by the President.

## 2. Key Parliamentary Terms
- Quorum (Article 100) : Minimum number of members required to be present to conduct a meeting. It is **one-tenth** of the total number of members in each House (i.e., 55 in Lok Sabha, 25 in Rajya Sabha), including the presiding officer.
- Joint Sitting (Article 108) : Called by the President to resolve deadlocks between LS and RS on **Ordinary Bills** or **Financial Bills**. Presided over by the **Speaker of the Lok Sabha**. (If Speaker is absent, the Deputy Speaker; if absent, the Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha).
- **Note**: Joint sittings **cannot** be called for Money Bills or Constitutional Amendment Bills.

## 3. Types of Bills & money Bills (Article 110)
- Money Bill : Deals with taxation, borrowing, consolidated fund, etc. Can only be introduced in the **Lok Sabha** on the recommendation of the President.
- The **Speaker's decision** on whether a bill is a money bill is final.
- Rajya Sabha has restricted powers; it can only delay the bill for **14 days** and cannot reject or amend it.

## High-Yield Formulas & Facts
- Quorum = 1/10th of membership.
- Joint Sitting = Art 108 (Presided by LS Speaker).
- Money Bill = Art 110 (Certified by LS Speaker).

--- The President of India (Art 52-62) ---
Subject: Indian Polity (CDS/NDA)
Chapter: Union Government

## 1. Election of the President (Article 54)
The President is elected not directly by the people, but by members of an **Electoral College** consisting of:
- Elected members of both Houses of Parliament (Lok Sabha & Rajya Sabha).
- Elected members of the Legislative Assemblies of the States (MLAs).
- Elected members of the Legislative Assemblies of the Union Territories of Delhi and Puducherry (and Jammu & Kashmir).

## 2. Impeachment of the President (Article 61)
- The President can be removed from office for **'violation of the Constitution'**.
- The impeachment charges can be initiated in **either House of Parliament**.
- The charges must be signed by **one-fourth of the members** of the initiating House and a **14-day notice** must be given to the President.
- To pass, the resolution must be approved by a majority of **not less than two-thirds of the total membership** of the House.
- It is then investigated by the other House, and if passed there by a **2/3rd majority of the total membership**, the President stands removed.

## 3. Veto Powers (Article 111)
- Absolute Veto : Withholding assent to the Bill (the Bill ends and does not become law).
- Suspensive Veto : Returning the Bill to Parliament for reconsideration. If Parliament passes the Bill again with or without amendments by a **simple majority**, the President **must** give assent. (Cannot be used for Money Bills).
- Pocket Veto : Keeping the Bill pending on his desk indefinitely (taking no action). The Indian President has a larger pocket veto than the US President because the US President must return the bill within 10 days, while the Indian Constitution specifies no time limit.

## 4. Pardon Powers (Article 72)
The President has the power to grant pardons, reprieves, respites, or remissions of punishment, or to suspend, remit, or commute sentences in all cases involving Court Martial, offences against Union laws, and death sent

## High-Yield Formulas & Facts
- Electoral College = Elected MPs + Elected MLAs.
- Impeachment = Art 61 (2/3 of Total Membership required).
- Pardon Powers = Art 72.

--- Reflection, Refraction & Lenses ---
Subject: Physics (NDA/CDS)
Chapter: Optics & Light

## 1. Laws of Reflection & Mirror Formulas
- First Law: The incident ray, the reflected ray, and the normal at the point of incidence all lie in the same plane.
- Second Law: The angle of incidence is equal to the angle of reflection (i = r).
- Mirror Formula : 1/f = 1/v + 1/u u = object distance (always negative).
- v = image distance (positive for virtual, negative for real).
- f = focal length (negative for concave, positive for convex).
- Linear Magnification: m = -v/u = hᵢ/hₒ (m is negative for real and inverted, positive for virtual and erect).

## 2. Refraction & Lens Formulas
- Snell's Law : The ratio of the sine of the angle of incidence to the sine of the angle of refraction is constant: sin i / sin r = constant = ₁μ₂ = μ₂/μ₁ .
- Absolute Refractive Index: μ = c / v (where c is speed of light in vacuum, v is speed in medium).
- Lens Formula : 1/f = 1/v - 1/u
- Linear Magnification (Lens): m = v/u = hᵢ/hₒ
- Power of a Lens (P) : Reciprocal of focal length in meters. P = 1 / f(in m) . Unit: Dioptre (D) . Convex lens: f is positive ⇒ P is positive.
- Concave lens: f is negative ⇒ P is negative.

## 3. Key Optical Phenomena
- Total Internal Reflection (TIR) : Occurs when light travels from denser to rarer medium and the angle of incidence is greater than the critical angle (θc). Formula: **sin θc = 1/μ**
- Applications: Optical fibers, sparkling of diamonds, mirages in deserts, endoscopes.
- Dispersion : Splitting of white light into constituent colors (VIBGYOR) when passing through a prism due to different speeds of different wavelengths in glass. Red deviates least, violet deviates most.
- Scattering of Light : Scattering intensity is inversely proportional to the fourth power of wavelength (Rayleigh Scattering: **I ∝ 1/λ⁴**). Explains why sky is blue (blue has short wavelength, scatters more) and sun appears red at sunrise/sunset.

## High-Yield Formulas & Facts
- Mirror Formula: 1/f = 1/v + 1/u
- Lens Formula: 1/f = 1/v - 1/u
- Lens Power: P = 1/f (dioptres)
- Refractive Index: n = c/v

## 🔥 The Revolt of 1857
Often called India's First War of Independence. A massive armed uprising against East India Company rule.

--- Stone Age ---
Subject: History (CDS/NDA)
Chapter: 2. Prehistoric India

## 🗿 The Stone Age
Prehistoric India is divided into Paleolithic, Mesolithic, and Neolithic ages based on stone tool technology and lifestyle shifts.

## 1. Paleolithic (Old Stone Age): Up to 10,000 BCE
- Lifestyle: Nomadic hunter-gatherers. Lived in caves and rock shelters. Fire was discovered but not widely controlled for cooking.
- Tools: Large, unpolished stone tools made of **quartzite** (hence Paleolithic humans are called "Quartzite Men"). Hand-axes, cleavers, choppers.
- Phases: Lower Paleolithic: Hand-axes, cleavers. Sites: Soan Valley (Punjab, Pakistan), Bori (Maharashtra).
- Middle Paleolithic: Flake tools. Sites: Narmada Valley, Nevasa (Maharashtra).
- Upper Paleolithic: Bone tools, blades. Coincided with modern humans (Homo sapiens). Sites: Kurnool caves (AP - evidence of ash).

## 2. Mesolithic (Middle Stone Age): 10,000 BCE - 6,000 BCE
- Climatic Shift: Transition to a warmer, drier climate, leading to changes in flora and fauna.
- Tools: **Microliths** - extremely small, pointed stone tools (1-5 cm long) fitted onto wooden or bone handles (spears, arrows).
- Lifestyle: Hunting-gathering transitioned to early animal domestication. Dog was the first animal domesticated.
- Key Sites: **Bagor** (Rajasthan - earliest evidence of domestication), **Adamgarh** (MP), Langhnaj (Gujarat).

## 3. Neolithic (New Stone Age): 6,000 BCE - 1,000 BCE
- The Neolithic Revolution: Transition from food-gathering to **food-producing** (agriculture).
- Lifestyle: Settled village life. Invention of the wheel, cultivation of wheat, barley, ragi, and rice. Domestication of sheep, goats, cattle. Production of hand-made and wheel-made pottery.
- Key Sites: **Mehrgarh (Balochistan, Pakistan):** Earliest Neolithic site (~7000 BCE). Evidence of wheat/barley farming and mud-brick houses.
- **Burzahom (Kashmir):** Unique pit-dwellings (caves dug in ground to escape cold) and practice of burying dogs with masters.
- **Koldihwa (UP):** Earliest evidence of **rice cultivation** globally (~6000 BCE).
- **Chirand (Bihar):** Large number of bone tools made of deer antlers.

## High-Yield Formulas & Facts
- Paleolithic: Quartzite, Bhimbetka | Mesolithic: Microliths, Bagor | Neolithic: Farming, Burzahom

--- Work, Power, Energy & Gravitation ---
Subject: Physics (NDA/CDS)
Chapter: Energy & Gravitation

## 1. Work, Energy and Power
- Work (W) : W = F · s · cos θ. (Scalar quantity, unit: Joule). Work is zero if force and displacement are perpendicular (θ = 90°), e.g., circular planetary orbits, porter holding luggage.
- Kinetic Energy (KE) : Energy due to motion. KE = ½ m v² = p² / 2m (where p is momentum). If momentum is doubled, KE becomes four times.
- Potential Energy (PE) : Energy due to position. PE = mgh .
- Power (P) : Rate of doing work. P = W / t = F · v . Unit: Watt. 1 Horsepower (HP) = 746 Watts .

## 2. Gravitation & Acceleration due to gravity (g)
- Newton's Law: F = G M m / r² (where G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N m²/kg²; Universal Gravitational Constant).
- Acceleration due to gravity: g = G M / R² (on Earth's surface, g ≈ 9.8 m/s²). g is independent of mass of the falling body.
- Variations in 'g' : With Altitude (h): Decreases: g' = g(1 - 2h/R) .
- With Depth (d): Decreases: g' = g(1 - d/R) . (At the centre of Earth, d = R ⇒ g = 0).
- Due to shape of Earth: Earth is an oblate spheroid. Requator > Rpole. Since g ∝ 1/R², g is minimum at Equator, maximum at Poles .
- Due to rotation: g decreases as rotation speed increases. (At equator g' = g - ω²R). If rotation stops, g at equator increases, while g at poles remains unchanged.

## 3. Kepler's Laws & Escape Velocity
- Kepler's 3rd Law (Law of Periods) : The square of time period of a planet is proportional to the cube of semi-major axis of its orbit: **T² ∝ r³**.
- Escape Velocity (vₑ) : Minimum velocity required to escape gravitational pull. Formula: vₑ = √(2gR) = √(2GM/R) .
- For Earth, escape velocity is **11.2 km/s**. For Moon, it is **2.38 km/s** (low gravity, hence no atmosphere on Moon).

## High-Yield Formulas & Facts
- Work: W = F·s·cosθ
- KE = 0.5mv² = p²/2m
- Power: P = W/t = F·v
- 1 HP = 746 Watts
- g = GM/R²
- Escape Velocity: vₑ = √(2gR) ≈ 11.2 km/s

## 1. Matrix Classifications
- Symmetric Matrix : Aᵀ = A. Diagonal elements can be anything.
- Skew-Symmetric Matrix : Aᵀ = -A. Diagonal elements must be zero . (aᵢᵢ = -aᵢᵢ ⇒ 2aᵢᵢ = 0 ⇒ aᵢᵢ = 0).
- Orthogonal Matrix : A · Aᵀ = I. Also, det(A) = ±1.
- Idempotent Matrix : A² = A.
- Involutory Matrix : A² = I.
- Nilpotent Matrix : Aᵏ = O (where k is the index of nilpotency).

## 2. Determinant Laws
- det(Aᵀ) = det(A)
- det(AB) = det(A) · det(B)
- det(kA) = kⁿ det(A) (for a matrix of order n × n).
- det(A⁻¹) = 1 / det(A)
- If any two rows/columns are interchanged, the sign of the determinant changes.
- If all elements of a row/column are zero, the determinant is zero .

## 3. Adjoint & Inverse Relations
- A · adj(A) = adj(A) · A = |A| · I
- |adj A| = |A|ⁿ⁻¹ (order n)
- |adj(adj A)| = |A|^( (n-1)² )
- adj(AB) = adj(B) · adj(A) (Reversal law)
- A⁻¹ = adj(A) / |A| (only if |A| ≠ 0; non-singular matrix).
- (Aᵀ)⁻¹ = (A⁻¹)ᵀ

--- Ratios, Proportions & Percentages ---
Subject: Mathematics (NDA/CDS)
Chapter: Numerical Aptitude

## 1. Ratios and Proportions
- Ratio: A comparison of two quantities: a/b or a : b.
- Proportion: Equality of two ratios: a : b :: c : d ⇒ a / b = c / d . Product of extremes = Product of means (ad = bc).
- Mean Proportional : Between a and b is **x = √(ab)**.
- Third Proportional : To a and b is **x = b² / a**.
- Fourth Proportional : To a, b, c is **x = bc / a**.

## 2. Percentages
- Percentage to Fraction: Divide by 100 (e.g., 20% = 20/100 = 1/5).
- Fraction to Percentage: Multiply by 100 (e.g., 3/4 = 3/4 × 100 = 75%).
- Percentage Increase/Decrease = [ (New Value - Original) / Original ] × 100

## 3. Profit, Loss, and Discount
- Gain = Selling Price (SP) - Cost Price (CP) (if SP > CP)
- Loss = Cost Price (CP) - Selling Price (SP) (if CP > SP)
- Gain % = (Gain / CP) × 100 (Profit and Loss are always calculated on CP unless stated otherwise).
- Loss % = (Loss / CP) × 100
- Assent Formulas: SP = CP × (100 + Gain%) / 100
- SP = CP × (100 - Loss%) / 100
- Discount : Calculated on Marked Price (MP). SP = MP - Discount.
- Discount % = (Discount / MP) × 100

## High-Yield Formulas & Facts
- Gain% = (Gain/CP) * 100
- Loss% = (Loss/CP) * 100
- Discount% = (Discount/MP) * 100
- Mean Proportional = √(ab)
- Third Proportional = b²/a


--- Time, Speed & Distance Formulas ---

## 1. General Speed, Time, and Distance Relationship
- Speed = Distance / Time ⇒ Time = Distance / Speed ; Distance = Speed × Time
- Unit Conversions: To convert from km/h to m/s: multiply by **5/18**. (e.g., 90 km/h = 90 × 5/18 = 25 m/s).
- To convert from m/s to km/h: multiply by **18/5**.
- Average Speed : Case A: A body covers two equal distances at speeds x km/h and y km/h. Average Speed = 2xy / (x + y) .
- Case B: A body covers three equal distances at speeds x, y, and z km/h. Average Speed = 3xyz / (xy + yz + zx) .
- General Case: **Total Distance Covered / Total Time Taken**.

## 2. Relative Speed
Speed of one body with respect to another moving body:
- If two bodies move in the **same direction** with speeds u and v (where u > v), Relative Speed = u - v .
- If two bodies move in **opposite directions**, Relative Speed = u + v .

## 3. Trains and Platforms Problems
- Train crossing a pole/man : The distance covered is equal to the length of the train itself. Time = Length of Train / Speed.
- Train crossing a bridge/platform/tunnel : The distance covered is equal to the sum of lengths of train and platform. Time = (Length of Train + Length of Platform) / Speed.
- Two trains crossing each other : The distance covered is always the sum of both lengths (L₁ + L₂). The speed used is the relative speed.

## High-Yield Formulas & Facts
- Speed = D/T
- 1 km/h = 5/18 m/s
- Average Speed = 2xy/(x+y)
- Same direction: Speed₁ - Speed₂
- Opposite direction: Speed₁ + Speed₂


--- Chemical Bonding & Periodic Table ---
Subject: Chemistry (NDA/CDS)
Chapter: Chemical Bonding

## 1. Types of Chemical Bonds
- Electrovalent/Ionic Bond : Formed by complete transfer of electrons from electropositive metal to electronegative non-metal (e.g., NaCl, CaCl₂). Properties: High melting/boiling points, soluble in water, conduct electricity in molten/solution state.
- Covalent Bond : Formed by equal sharing of electrons between non-metals (e.g., H₂, O₂, H₂O). Properties: Low melting/boiling points, insoluble in water (soluble in organic solvents), poor conductors.
- Coordinate/Dative Bond : Special covalent bond where shared pair is donated by one atom (donor) and accepted by another (acceptor) (e.g., NH₄⁺, H₃O⁺).
- Hydrogen Bond : Electrostatic force of attraction between hydrogen atom bonded to a highly electronegative atom (F, O, N) and another electronegative atom. Intermolecular H-bonding: Between different molecules (e.g., H₂O, HF). Explains why H₂O is liquid while H₂S is gas.
- Intramolecular H-bonding: Within the same molecule (e.g., o-nitrophenol).

## 2. Modern Periodic Table & Periodic Trends
Developed by Henry Moseley, based on **Atomic Number**. Has 18 groups and 7 periods.
- Atomic Radius : Across Period (Left to Right): **Decreases** due to increase in effective nuclear charge (pulls electrons closer).
- Down Group (Top to Bottom): **Increases** due to addition of new electron shells.
- Ionization Energy (IE) : Energy required to remove the outermost electron. Across Period: **Increases** (atomic size decreases, nuclear pull increases).
- Down Group: **Decreases** (atomic size increases, easier to remove outer electron).
- Electronegativity : Tendency of an atom to attract shared electron pair. Across Period: **Increases** (Fluorine is most electronegative).
- Down Group: **Decreases**.
- Metallic Character (Electropositivity) : Across Period: **Decreases**.
- Down Group: **Increases** (Francium/Cesium are highly metallic).

## High-Yield Formulas & Facts
- Ionic Bond: Electron transfer
- Covalent Bond: Electron sharing
- H-Bonding: Strongest intermolecular attraction
- Electronegativity: F > O > N > Cl


--- Probability Theory & Bayes Theorem ---
Subject: Mathematics (NDA/CDS)
Chapter: Probability & Statistics

## 1. Basic Laws of Probability
- Addition Rule: P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A ∩ B)
- If events are mutually exclusive: P(A ∩ B) = 0 ⇒ P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B)
- Conditional Probability: P(A|B) = P(A ∩ B) / P(B) (where P(B) > 0)
- Independent Events: P(A ∩ B) = P(A) · P(B) ⇒ P(A|B) = P(A)

## 2. Bayes' Theorem
Used to calculate posterior probability when partition events E₁, E₂...Eₙ are given:
- P(Eᵢ|A) = [ P(Eᵢ) · P(A|Eᵢ) ] / [ Σ[j=1 to n] P(Eⱼ) · P(A|Eⱼ) ]

## 3. Statistics & Measures of Central Tendency
- Mean (x̄) : Average value. x̄ = (Σ xᵢ)/n.
- Median : Middle value. If n is odd: ((n+1)/2)th term. If n is even: Mean of (n/2)th and (n/2 + 1)th terms.
- Mode : Element with highest frequency.
- Empirical Relation : Mode = 3 Median - 2 Mean

## 4. Measures of Dispersion
- Variance (σ²) : σ² = Σ(xᵢ - x̄)² / n = (Σ xᵢ² / n) - (x̄)²
- Standard Deviation (σ) : σ = √Variance
- Coefficient of Variation (CV) : CV = (σ / x̄) * 100 (measures relative consistency).

## High-Yield Formulas & Facts
- P(A|B) = P(A ∩ B) / P(B)
- Mode = 3Median - 2Mean
- Variance(σ²) = (Σx²/n) - (x̄)²
- SD(σ) = √Variance
- CV = (σ/x̄)·100

--- Trigonometric Identities & Values ---
Subject: Mathematics (NDA/CDS)
Chapter: Trigonometry

## 1. Core Trigonometric Identities
Pythagorean identities form the bedrock of trigonometric simplification:
- sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 ⇒ sin²θ = 1 - cos²θ ; cos²θ = 1 - sin²θ
- sec²θ - tan²θ = 1 (θ ≠ (2n+1)π/2) ⇒ sec²θ = 1 + tan²θ ; tan²θ = sec²θ - 1
- cosec²θ - cot²θ = 1 (θ ≠ nπ) ⇒ cosec²θ = 1 + cot²θ ; cot²θ = cosec²θ - 1

## 2. Sum & Difference Formulas
Used to find values of angles like 15°, 75°, 105°, etc.:
- sin(A ± B) = sin A cos B ± cos A sin B
- cos(A ± B) = cos A cos B ∓ sin A sin B
- tan(A ± B) = (tan A ± tan B) / (1 ∓ tan A tan B)
- cot(A ± B) = (cot A cot B ∓ 1) / (cot B ± cot A)

## 3. Multiple Angle Identities
Essential for reducing powers in calculus integrations:
- sin 2A = 2 sin A cos A = 2tan A / (1 + tan² A)
- cos 2A = cos² A - sin² A = 2cos² A - 1 = 1 - 2sin² A = (1 - tan² A)/(1 + tan² A)
- tan 2A = 2tan A / (1 - tan² A)
- sin 3A = 3sin A - 4sin³ A
- cos 3A = 4cos³ A - 3cos A
- tan 3A = (3tan A - tan³ A) / (1 - 3tan² A)

## 4. Product-to-Sum & Sum-to-Product
Key transformations for calculus and equations:
- 2 sin A cos B = sin(A + B) + sin(A - B)
- 2 cos A sin B = sin(A + B) - sin(A - B)
- 2 cos A cos B = cos(A + B) + cos(A - B)
- 2 sin A sin B = cos(A - B) - cos(A + B)
- sin C + sin D = 2 sin((C+D)/2) cos((C-D)/2)
- sin C - sin D = 2 cos((C+D)/2) sin((C-D)/2)
- cos C + cos D = 2 cos((C+D)/2) cos((C-D)/2)
- cos C - cos D = -2 sin((C+D)/2) sin((C-D)/2)

## High-Yield Formulas & Facts
- sin(A+B) = sinA·cosB + cosA·sinB
- cos(2A) = 2cos²A - 1 = 1 - 2sin²A
- tan(2A) = 2tanA / (1 - tan²A)
- sin(3A) = 3sinA - 4sin³A
- cos(3A) = 4cos³A - 3cosA
- 2sinA·cosB = sin(A+B) + sin(A-B)
- sinC + sinD = 2sin((C+D)/2)cos((C-D)/2)

--- Vedic Age ---
Subject: History (CDS/NDA)
Chapter: 3. Ancient India

## 🌾 The Vedic Age
Reconstructed using the Vedas. Divided into Early Vedic (1500-1000 BCE) and Later Vedic (1000-600 BCE) periods.

## 1. Early Vedic Period (1500 - 1000 BCE)
- Geography: Settled in the **Sapta-Sindhu** region (Land of 7 Rivers: Indus, Saraswati, and 5 rivers of Punjab).
- Polity: Tribal structure. King (Rajan) was not absolute, advised by assemblies: **Sabha** (council of elders), **Samiti** (general tribal assembly), and **Vidatha** (oldest folk assembly). Women attended Sabha/Vidatha.
- Society & Economy: Pastoral economy. Cattle (Gau) was the main form of wealth (wars fought for cows called Gavisthi ). Society was egalitarian; caste system (Varna) was flexible, based on occupation, not birth.

## 2. Later Vedic Period (1000 - 600 BCE)
- Geography: Shifted eastward to the Gangetic Valley (Aryavarta). Use of iron (called Shyama Ayas ) cleared thick forests.
- Polity: Territorial kingdoms (Janapadas) emerged. Royal power increased. Sabha and Samiti lost power; Vidatha was abolished. Women were no longer allowed to attend assemblies.
- Society & Economy: Transition to an agrarian economy. Caste system became rigid and hereditary, split into 4 Varnas (Brahmins, Kshatriyas, Vaishyas, Shudras). 4 Ashramas (stages of life) emerged.

## 3. Vedic Literature
- Four Vedas: Rigveda (hymns), Samaveda (music/chants), Yajurveda (rituals), Atharvaveda (magic/spells).
- Brahmanas: Prose texts explaining the rituals of the Vedas.
- Aranyakas: Forest books dealing with mysticism and philosophy.
- Upanishads: Philosophical texts (Vedanta). Total 108. **"Satyameva Jayate"** is in **Mundaka Upanishad**.

## High-Yield Formulas & Facts
- Rigveda oldest | Mundaka Upanishad: Satyameva Jayate | Early Vedic: pastoral | Later Vedic: iron farming

--- What is History? ---
Subject: History (CDS/NDA)
Chapter: 1. Historiography & Historical Foundations

## 📚 Meaning and Scope of History
History is the systematic analysis of past human events, cultures, and structures. It is not a static list of dates, but a dynamic, interpretative science that examines cause-and-effect relationships over time.

## 1. Core Foundations
- Definition: The word comes from Greek Historia (inquiry/knowledge acquired by investigation). E.H. Carr defined it as "an unending dialogue between the present and the past."
- Importance of Chronology: Establishes the exact sequence of events, helping to avoid anachronisms and trace the evolutionary progression of human society.
- Cause and Effect: Every historical event has roots in economic, social, political, or geographical causes, and produces consequences that shape subsequent eras.
- Periodisation of Indian History: Originally divided by colonial historians (like James Mill) into Hindu, Muslim, and British periods (a communal division). Modern historians classify it scientifically into Ancient, Medieval, and Modern periods.
- Social, Economic & Cultural History: Shift from dynastic history (kings and wars) to social structures (caste, gender), economic patterns (trade routes, land revenue), and cultural expressions (art, religion).

## High-Yield Formulas & Facts
- Primary Sources: contemporary | Secondary Sources: later analysis

--- World War I ---
Subject: History (CDS/NDA)
Chapter: 6. World History

## 💥 World War I (1914 - 1918)
A global war fought mainly in Europe, changing map boundaries and destroying empires.

## 1. Causes and Blocs
- Causes: Imperialist competition, secret alliances, arms race, militarism, and rise of nationalism.
- Immediate Cause: Assassination of **Archduke Franz Ferdinand** (heir to the Austro-Hungarian throne) in Sarajevo (Bosnia) on 28 June 1914 by Serbian nationalist Gavrilo Princip.
- Alliances: - *Allied Powers:* Britain, France, Russia, Italy, Japan, and USA (entered 1917 after German submarines sank US ships). - *Central Powers:* Germany, Austria-Hungary, Ottoman Empire (Turkey), Bulgaria.

## 2. Outcomes
- Ended with Germany's surrender on 11 Nov 1918. Signed the **Treaty of Versailles (1919)**, placing the guilt of war on Germany, stripping its colonies, and imposing massive reparations.
- Collpase of major empires: German, Austro-Hungarian, Russian (became USSR), and Ottoman (led to modern Turkey).
- Created the **League of Nations (1920)** to prevent future wars (failed due to US absence and lack of military power).

--- World War II ---
Subject: History (CDS/NDA)
Chapter: 6. World History

## 💣 World War II (1939 - 1945)
The most destructive conflict in human history, involving the majority of the world's nations.

## 1. Causes and Outbreak
- Causes: Failure of the League of Nations, rise of fascism/nazism, policy of appeasement (Britain/France letting Hitler annex territory), and German dissatisfaction with Versailles.
- Immediate Cause: Germany's invasion of **Poland** on 1 September 1939. Britain and France declared war on Germany.
- Alliances: - *Axis Powers:* Germany (Hitler), Italy (Mussolini), Japan (Tojo). - *Allied Powers:* Britain (Churchill), USSR (Stalin), USA (Roosevelt / Truman), China.

## 2. Decisive Events & End
- **Pearl Harbor (7 Dec 1941):** Japan attacked US naval base in Hawaii. Prompted USA to enter the war.
- **Battle of Stalingrad (1942-43):** Turning point in Europe. Soviet Red Army defeated German forces.
- **D-Day (6 June 1944):** Allied invasion of Normandy (France), liberating Western Europe.
- **The Holocaust:** Systematic, state-sponsored genocide of 6 million Jews by Nazi Germany.
- **End:** Germany surrendered in May 1945 (Hitler committed suicide). Japan surrendered after USA dropped atomic bombs: **"Little Boy"** on **Hiroshima** (6 August 1945) and **"Fat Man"** on **Nagasaki** (9 August 1945).

## High-Yield Formulas & Facts
- WWII: 1939-1945 | Trigger: Poland Invasion | Atomic Bombs: Hiroshima (Aug 6), Nagasaki (Aug 9, 1945)

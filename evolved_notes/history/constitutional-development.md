# Constitutional Development under British Rule

## 1. Company Rule (1773 – 1858)

### Regulating Act of 1773
*   **Significance:** It was the first step taken by the [[British Parliament]] to control and regulate the affairs of the [[East India Company]] in India.
*   **Key Provisions:**
    *   Designated the [[Governor]] of Bengal as the **'[[Governor]]-General of Bengal'** (Warren Hastings was the first).
    *   Created an Executive Council of four members to assist him.
    *   Made the Governors of Bombay and Madras presidencies subordinate to the [[Governor]]-General of Bengal.
    *   Established the **[[Supreme Court]] at Calcutta** (1774) with one Chief [[Justice]] (Sir Elijah Impey) and three other judges.

### Pitt's India Act of 1784
*   **Significance:** It distinguished between the commercial and political functions of the Company for the first time.
*   **Key Provisions:**
    *   **Double Government:** Established a **Board of Control** (in London) to manage political affairs, while the **Court of Directors** managed commercial affairs.
    *   Reduced the [[Governor]]-General's Executive Council from four to three members.
    *   Company territories in India were called "British possessions in India" for the first time.

### Charter Act of 1813
*   **Key Provisions:**
    *   **Abolished the trade monopoly** of the EIC in India, except for trade in **tea** and trade with **China**.
    *   Allowed Christian missionaries to enter India for religious proselytization.
    *   Allocated Rs. 1 Lakh annually for the promotion of education among Indians.

### Charter Act of 1833
*   **Significance:** The final step towards the centralization of British India.
*   **Key Provisions:**
    *   Made the [[Governor]]-General of Bengal the **'[[Governor]]-General of India'** (Lord William Bentinck was the first).
    *   Deprived the Governors of Bombay and Madras of their legislative powers.
    *   Completely ended the commercial activities of the EIC; it became a purely administrative body.
    *   Added a fourth member (Law Member - Lord Macaulay) to the Executive Council.

### Charter Act of 1853
*   **Key Provisions:**
    *   Separated the legislative and executive functions of the [[Governor]]-General's council. It established a separate Indian (Central) Legislative Council (a mini-[[parliament]]).
    *   Introduced an **open competition system** for the selection of civil servants (throwing it open to Indians).

## 2. Crown Rule (1858 – 1947)

### Government of India Act of 1858
*   Passed in the wake of [[the Revolt of 1857]]. Known as the Act for the Good Government of India.
*   **Key Provisions:**
    *   Abolished the [[East India Company]] and transferred powers to the British Crown.
    *   Changed the designation of the [[Governor]]-General to the **Viceroy of India** (Lord Canning was the first).
    *   Abolished the Double Government (Board of Control and Court of Directors).
    *   Created a new office, the **Secretary of State for India**, based in London and assisted by a 15-member Council of India.

### Indian Councils Act of 1861
*   **Key Provisions:**
    *   Initiated the process of **decentralization** by restoring legislative powers to Bombay and Madras presidencies.
    *   Introduced the **Portfolio System** (initiated by Canning in 1859), where each member of the council was put in charge of specific departments.
    *   Empowered the Viceroy to issue **ordinances**.
    *   Indians were nominated for the first time to the legislative council as non-official members (Raja of Benaras, Maharaja of Patiala, Sir Dinkar Rao).

### Indian Councils Act of 1892
*   **Key Provisions:**
    *   Increased the number of non-official members in the Central and Provincial legislative councils.
    *   Introduced the principle of **indirect election** (nomination based on recommendations).
    *   Gave legislative councils the [[power]] to discuss the budget and ask questions to the executive (but no supplementary questions or voting rights).

### Indian Councils Act of 1909 (Morley-Minto Reforms)
*   **Significance:** Officially introduced communal representation in Indian politics.
*   **Key Provisions:**
    *   Introduced a system of **separate electorates for Muslims** (Lord Minto became known as the Father of Communal Electorate).
    *   Satyendra Prasad Sinha became the first Indian to join the Viceroy’s Executive Council (appointed as the Law Member).
    *   Members were allowed to ask supplementary questions and move resolutions on the budget.

### Government of India Act of 1919 (Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms)
*   **Significance:** Introduced responsible government in provinces.
*   **Key Provisions:**
    *   Introduced **Dyarchy** (double rule) in the provinces. Provincial subjects were divided into two parts:
        1.  *Transferred Subjects:* Administered by the [[Governor]] with the aid of ministers responsible to the legislative council.
        2.  *Reserved Subjects:* Administered by the [[Governor]] and his executive council without any responsibility to the legislature (Police, [[Justice]], Revenue).
    *   Introduced **Bicameralism** (a two-house legislature: Council of State and Legislative Assembly) and direct elections at the Center.
    *   Extended separate electorates to Sikhs, Indian Christians, Anglo-Indians, and Europeans.
    *   Provided for the establishment of a Public Service Commission (set up in 1926).

### Government of India Act of 1935
*   **Significance:** A lengthy document that formed the blueprint for the [[Constitution]] of independent India.
*   **Key Provisions:**
    *   Provided for the establishment of an **All-India Federation** consisting of provinces and princely states as units (this never materialized as princely states did not join).
    *   Abolished Dyarchy in the provinces and introduced **Provincial Autonomy**.
    *   Introduced **Dyarchy at the Center** (Federal subjects divided into reserved and transferred).
    *   Introduced bicameralism in 6 out of 11 provinces.
    *   Established a **Federal Court** (set up in 1937) and a Reserve Bank of India.
    *   Extended separate electorates further to depressed classes (Scheduled Castes), women, and labor.

### Indian Independence Act of 1947
*   Based on the [[Mountbatten Plan]] (June 3 Plan).
*   **Key Provisions:**
    *   Ended British rule in India and declared India an independent and [[sovereign]] state from August 15, 1947.
    *   Provided for the partition of India into two independent dominions: India and Pakistan.
    *   Abolished the office of Viceroy; provided a [[Governor]]-General for each dominion.
    *   Empowered the Constituent Assemblies of the two dominions to frame their own constitutions.

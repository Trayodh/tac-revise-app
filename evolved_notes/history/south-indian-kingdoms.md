# South Indian Kingdoms: Sangam Age to Chola Empire

## 1. The Sangam Age (300 BCE – 300 CE)
The Sangam Age refers to the period in the history of ancient Tamil Nadu, Kerala, and parts of Sri Lanka (known as Tamilakam) when classical Tamil [[literature]] (Sangam [[literature]]) was composed. The word 'Sangam' means an assembly or college of Tamil poets, patronized by the Pandyan kings of Madurai.

### Sangam Literature
*   **Tolkappiyam:** Authored by Tolkappiyar. It is the earliest available Tamil [[work]], primarily a treatise on Tamil grammar and poetics.
*   **Silappadikaram (The Jeweled Anklet):** Authored by Ilango Adigal. An epic detailing the tragic story of Kovalan and Kannagi.
*   **Manimekalai:** Authored by Sattanar. A Buddhist epic continuing the story of Kovalan's daughter.

## 2. The Pallavas of Kanchi (3rd Century CE – 9th Century CE)
Following the Kalabhra interregnum (a dark age after the Sangam period), the [[Pallavas]] emerged as a dominant [[power]] in northern Tamil Nadu, establishing their capital at **Kanchipuram**.

*   **Mahendravarman I (600–630 CE):** Originally a Jain, converted to Shaivism by Appar. He was defeated by the Chalukyan king Pulakesin II. He authored the Sanskrit play *Mattavilasa Prahasana* and initiated rock-cut temple [[architecture]] in South India.
*   **Narasimhavarman I (Mahamalla) (630–668 CE):** The greatest Pallava ruler. He defeated and killed Pulakesin II, capturing the Chalukyan capital Vatapi (assuming the title *Vatapikonda*). 
    *   **[[Architecture]]:** Founded the city of Mamallapuram (Mahabalipuram). Commissioned the famous monolithic **Ratha temples (Pancha Rathas)**.
*   **Narasimhavarman II (Rajasimha) (695–722 CE):** His reign was peaceful and marked by a surge in temple building. He built the famous **Shore Temple at Mahabalipuram** and the **Kailasanatha Temple at Kanchipuram** (structural temples).

## 3. The Chalukyas of Badami (Vatapi) (6th Century CE – 8th Century CE)
Ruling from Vatapi (modern Badami in Karnataka), they dominated the Deccan plateau.

*   **Pulakesin II (609–642 CE):** The most famous Chalukyan king. 
    *   He famously **defeated Harshavardhana** on the banks of the [[Narmada]] river, halting [[Harsha]]'s southern expansion.
    *   This victory is recorded in the **Aihole Inscription**, composed by his court poet Ravikirti.
    *   He was later defeated and killed by Pallava king Narasimhavarman I.
*   **[[Architecture]]:** Known as the cradle of Indian temple [[architecture]]. Developed the **Vesara style** (a hybrid of Nagara and Dravida styles). Examples: Virupaksha Temple at Pattadakal (built by Queen Lokamahadevi to commemorate the victory over [[Pallavas]]), temples at Aihole and Badami.

## 4. The Imperial Cholas (9th Century CE – 13th Century CE)
The [[Cholas]] revived their [[power]] under **Vijayalaya** in the 9th century, with their capital at **Thanjavur (Tanjore)**. They became the greatest naval [[power]] in ancient/medieval India.

### Key Rulers
*   **Rajaraja Chola I (985–1014 CE):**
    *   A brilliant military strategist who conquered northern Sri Lanka, the Maldives, and parts of the Chera and Pandyan kingdoms.
    *   Built the magnificent **Brihadeshwara Temple (Rajarajeshwara Temple)** at Thanjavur, a masterpiece of Dravidian [[architecture]] (UNESCO World Heritage Site).
*   **Rajendra Chola I (1014–1044 CE):**
    *   Expanded the empire to its greatest extent. He conquered the whole of Sri Lanka.
    *   **Northern Expedition:** Marched his army to the river [[Ganga]], defeated the Pala king Mahipala, and assumed the title **Gangaikonda** (Victor of the Ganges). He founded a new capital city, **Gangaikondacholapuram**.
    *   **Naval Expedition:** Launched a massive naval campaign against the Srivijaya Empire (modern Indonesia/Malaysia) to secure the maritime trade routes to China.

### Chola Administration and Art
*   **Local Self-Government:** The most striking feature of Chola administration was their highly organized system of local village autonomy, famously detailed in the **Uttaramerur Inscription**.
*   **Bronze Sculpture:** The Chola period witnessed the pinnacle of bronze casting using the *cire-perdue* (lost wax) technique. The **Nataraja** (Shiva as the Lord of Dance) is the most famous example of Chola bronze sculpture.
*   **[[Literature]]:** Kamban composed the *Ramavataram* (Tamil Ramayana).

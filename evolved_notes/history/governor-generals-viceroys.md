## 1. Governors of Bengal (1757 – 1774)

*   **Robert Clive (1757–1760 & 1765–1767):**
    *   Victor of the **[[Battle of Plassey]] (1757)**, which established British political [[power]] in Bengal.
    *   Victor of the **[[Battle of Buxar]] (1764)** (fought by Hector Munro). Clive negotiated the **Treaty of Allahabad (1765)**, securing the *Diwani* rights (revenue collection) of Bengal, Bihar, and Orissa for the [[East India Company]].
    *   Established the **Dual System of Government** in Bengal (Company had revenue [[power]]; Nawab had administrative responsibility without [[resources]]).

## 2. Governor-Generals of Bengal (1774 – 1833)
The Regulating Act of 1773 elevated the [[Governor]] of Bengal to [[Governor]]-General of Bengal, with supervisory powers over the Bombay and Madras presidencies.

*   **Warren Hastings (1773–1785):**
    *   The **First [[Governor]]-General of Bengal**.
    *   Abolished Clive's Dual System.
    *   Founded the Asiatic Society of Bengal (with William Jones) in 1784.
    *   Started the *Diwani* and *Faujdari Adalats* at the district level.
    *   He was the only [[Governor]]-General to be impeached in the [[British Parliament]] (later acquitted).
*   **Lord Cornwallis (1786–1793):**
    *   Introduced the **[[Permanent Settlement]] (Zamindari System)** in Bengal and Bihar (1793).
    *   Known as the **Father of Civil Services in India**.
    *   Introduced the Cornwallis Code based on the separation of revenue and [[justice]] administration.
    *   Defeated Tipu Sultan in the Third Anglo-Mysore War.
*   **Lord Wellesley (1798–1805):**
    *   Introduced the **[[Subsidiary Alliance]] System** to bring Indian states under British control without direct annexation (Hyderabad was the first state to sign).
    *   Defeated and killed Tipu Sultan in the Fourth Anglo-Mysore War (1799).
    *   Founded Fort William College in Calcutta to train civil servants.
    *   He called himself the "Bengal Tiger."
*   **Lord Hastings (1813–1823):**
    *   Ended the policy of non-intervention.
    *   Fought the Gurkha War (Treaty of Sagauli).
    *   Destroyed the Pindaris and fought the Third Anglo-Maratha War, completely dissolving the Maratha confederacy.
    *   Introduction of the **[[Ryotwari]] System** in Madras by Thomas Munro.
*   **Lord William Bentinck (1828–1835):**
    *   Known as the most liberal and reformist [[Governor]]-General.
    *   **Abolished Sati (1829)** with the help of Raja Rammohan Roy.
    *   Suppressed the Thugs (a cult of highway robbers) under Colonel Sleeman.
    *   Made English the medium of higher education in India (Macaulay's Minute, 1835).
    *   The Charter Act of 1833 made him the **First [[Governor]]-General of India**.

## 3. Governor-Generals of India (1833 – 1858)

*   **Lord Dalhousie (1848–1856):**
    *   A fierce expansionist. He introduced the infamous **[[Doctrine of Lapse]]**, annexing states like Satara, Jhansi, and Nagpur when the ruler died without a natural heir.
    *   Annexed Awadh (Oudh) on grounds of "maladministration" (1856), a major cause of the 1857 Revolt.
    *   **The Maker of Modern India:** Introduced the first railway line (Bombay to Thane, 1853), the telegraph line, the modern postal system, and established the Public Works Department (PWD).
    *   Passed the Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act (1856) drafted by Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar.
    *   Wood's Despatch (1854) on education, called the "Magna Carta of English Education in India."

## 4. Viceroys of India (1858 – 1947)
Following [[the Revolt of 1857]], the Government of India Act 1858 transferred [[power]] from the EIC to the British Crown. The [[Governor]]-General now also received the title of Viceroy (representative of the Crown).

*   **Lord Canning (1856–1862):**
    *   The last [[Governor]]-General and the **First Viceroy of India**.
    *   Passed the Indian Councils Act of 1861, introducing the portfolio system.
    *   Introduced the Indian Penal Code (IPC) and established [[High Courts]] at Calcutta, Bombay, and Madras.
*   **Lord Lytton (1876–1880):**
    *   Highly unpopular Viceroy.
    *   Passed the **Vernacular Press Act (1878)** (the 'Gagging Act') to suppress Indian-language newspapers.
    *   Passed the **Arms Act (1878)**, prohibiting Indians from carrying arms without a license.
    *   Held the grand Delhi Durbar of 1877 while a devastating famine raged across the country.
*   **Lord Ripon (1880–1884):**
    *   The most popular Viceroy, known as "Ripon the Good."
    *   Repealed the Vernacular Press Act.
    *   Known as the **Father of Local Self-Government in India** (passed a resolution in 1882).
    *   Appointed the Hunter Commission for education reform.
    *   **The Ilbert [[Bill]] Controversy (1883):** Tried to allow Indian judges to try European offenders, leading to massive protests by Europeans in India.
*   **Lord Curzon (1899–1905):**
    *   Highly capable but deeply imperialistic.
    *   His most controversial act was the **[[Partition of Bengal]] (1905)**, which sparked the [[Swadeshi Movement]].
    *   Passed the Ancient Monuments Preservation Act and the Indian Universities Act.
*   **Lord Minto (1905–1910):**
    *   Surat split of the INC (1907). Foundation of the Muslim League (1906).
    *   Passed the Morley-Minto Reforms (1909) introducing separate electorates for Muslims.
*   **Lord Chelmsford (1916–1921):**
    *   Lucknow Pact (1916), [[Rowlatt Act]] (1919), [[Jallianwala Bagh]] Massacre (1919).
    *   [[Non-Cooperation Movement]] launched. Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms (1919).
*   **Lord Irwin (1926–1931):**
    *   [[Simon Commission]] visit (1928), [[Nehru Report]], Lahore Session (Purna Swaraj).
    *   [[Civil Disobedience Movement]] (Dandi March, 1930).
    *   First Round Table Conference and the **Gandhi-Irwin Pact (1931)**.
*   **Lord Linlithgow (1936–1943):**
    *   Longest-serving Viceroy.
    *   Outbreak of WWII. Resignation of Congress ministries.
    *   August Offer (1940), [[Cripps Mission]] (1942), and the **[[Quit India Movement]] (1942)**.
*   **Lord Mountbatten (1947–1948):**
    *   The last Viceroy of British India and the **First [[Governor]]-General of independent India**.
    *   Put forth the [[Mountbatten Plan]] (June 3 Plan) leading to the partition and independence of India and Pakistan.
*   **C. Rajagopalachari (1948–1950):** The only Indian to hold the post of [[Governor]]-General.

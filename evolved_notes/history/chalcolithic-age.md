# Chalcolithic Age (The Copper-Stone Age)

## 1. Introduction to the Chalcolithic Phase
The [[Chalcolithic Age]] (from the Greek words *khalkos* meaning 'copper' and *lithos* meaning 'stone') marks the transition from the pure [[stone age]] to the age of [[metals]]. It is the period when humans first began using metal—specifically copper—alongside stone tools. Culturally, the Chalcolithic period in India mostly succeeds the Neolithic age, though in some regions (like the [[Indus]] Valley) it evolved into the Bronze Age, while in central and southern India, Chalcolithic farming cultures continued simultaneously with or even after the [[Harappan]] civilization.

Chronologically, non-[[Harappan]] Chalcolithic cultures in India generally span from **2000 BCE to 700 BCE**, primarily localized in Western India, Central India, and the Deccan region.

## 2. Key Characteristics of Chalcolithic Cultures

### Technology and Economy
*   **[[Metallurgy]]:** Copper was the first metal to be smelted and used by humans. They manufactured copper tools like axes, chisels, and bangles, but stone tools (microliths and blade tools) remained dominant because copper was scarce and soft.
*   **[[Agriculture]] and Animal Husbandry:** Chalcolithic communities were primarily rural, farming communities. They cultivated a wide variety of [[crops]] including wheat, barley, lentils, bajra, jowar, and ragi. They domesticated cattle, sheep, goats, and pigs, using them for food rather than primarily for milk or dairy.
*   **Pottery:** A defining feature of Chalcolithic cultures is their distinct pottery. The most widespread and characteristic type is **Black-and-Red Ware (BRW)**, often painted with white linear designs. They were the first to use painted pottery extensively.

### Society and Settlements
*   **Settlement Patterns:** They lived in thatched houses made of mud, mud-bricks, and wattle-and-daub. Unlike the Harappans, they did not use burnt bricks extensively.
*   **Burial Practices:** The dead were buried under the floors of their houses. In Maharashtra (Jorwe culture), the dead were buried in a north-south orientation, while in South India, an east-west orientation was preferred.
*   **Social Inequalities:** Grave goods (pots and copper ornaments found in burials) suggest the beginnings of social stratification and inequality. A chief's house would be larger, rectangular, and centrally located, while commoners lived in smaller circular huts.

## 3. Major Chalcolithic Cultures and Sites
India hosted several distinct regional Chalcolithic cultures, mostly named after their type-sites.

### A. Ahar-Banas Culture (Rajasthan)
*   **Region:** Banas River valley in southeastern Rajasthan.
*   **Key Sites:** Ahar (ancient name *Tambavati*, meaning 'place of copper'), Gilund, and Balathal.
*   **Features:** Uniquely, the Ahar culture used virtually no microlithic stone tools; they relied almost entirely on copper tools. They used black-and-red ware painted with white designs.

### B. Kayatha Culture (Madhya Pradesh)
*   **Region:** Chambal River valley (Malwa region).
*   **Key Sites:** Kayatha and Eran.
*   **Features:** Known for its sturdy red-slipped pottery painted with dark brown designs. The site of Kayatha yielded a cache of copper axes and bangles, indicating significant metallurgical skill.

### C. Malwa Culture (Madhya Pradesh & Maharashtra)
*   **Region:** [[Narmada]] River valley.
*   **Key Sites:** Navdatoli (the largest Chalcolithic rural site excavated in India), Maheshwar, and Nagda.
*   **Features:** Malwa pottery is considered the finest of all Chalcolithic ceramics in India. Navdatoli provides evidence for the cultivation of the widest variety of agricultural [[crops]] during this period.

### D. Jorwe Culture (Maharashtra)
*   **Region:** [[Godavari]] and Pravara river valleys (Western Maharashtra).
*   **Key Sites:** Jorwe, Nevasa, Daimabad, and Inamgaon.
*   **Features:** The Jorwe culture represents the most extensive and intensively excavated Chalcolithic culture.
    *   **Daimabad:** The largest Jorwe site, famous for the discovery of a hoard of massive solid cast bronze objects (including a chariot driven by a man, an elephant, a rhino, and a buffalo), showing late [[Harappan]] influence.
    *   **Inamgaon:** A highly fortified settlement with a moat, providing clear evidence of social stratification, a chiefdom society, and granaries.

## 4. Limitations of the Chalcolithic Cultures
Despite their advances, the Chalcolithic cultures had notable limitations that prevented them from achieving full [[urbanization]] (unlike the Harappans):
1.  **Ignorance of Bronze:** They did not know how to mix tin with copper to make bronze (which is much harder and better suited for clearing dense [[forests]] or deep plowing).
2.  **No Writing:** They remained pre-literate and did not develop any script.
3.  **Rural Economy:** They could not produce enough agricultural surplus to support large urban centers, artisans, and trade networks.
4.  **High Infant Mortality:** Burial data from sites like Inamgaon indicates a very high infant mortality rate, suggesting poor health, nutrition, or frequent epidemics.

# Indian Dance and Music

## 1. Classical Dance Forms of India
The Sangeet Natak Akademi currently recognizes **eight** classical dance forms in India. The foundational text for all classical Indian dance is the **Natya Shastra**, written by Sage Bharata (c. 200 BCE – 200 CE).

### The Two Basic Aspects of Indian Classical Dance:
*   **Lasya:** Represents grace, Bhava (emotion), Rasa (flavor), and Abhinaya (expression). It is considered the feminine aspect of dance (attributed to Goddess Parvati).
*   **Tandava:** Represents rhythm, movement, and [[energy]]. It is considered the masculine, vigorous aspect of dance (attributed to Lord Shiva).

### The Eight Classical Dances:

| Dance Form | State of Origin | Key Features and Themes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bharatanatyam** | Tamil Nadu | Oldest classical dance. Originally performed by *Devadasis* in temples. Known as the 'fire dance' (movements resemble a dancing flame). Emphasizes strict *Lasya* and precise footwork. |
| **Kathakali** | Kerala | Means 'story play'. A highly dramatic dance-drama. Known for elaborate, heavy makeup and massive headgear. Colors denote character types (e.g., green for noble, black for evil). Themes usually from Ramayana/Mahabharata. |
| **Kathak** | Uttar Pradesh | Derived from *Kathakas* (storytellers). Greatly influenced by the Mughal courts (transitioned from temple to court dance). Known for intricate footwork (*Tatkar*), rapid spins (*Chakkars*), and Jugalbandi with the Tabla. |
| **Kuchipudi** | Andhra Pradesh | Originated in the Kuchipudi village. Blends [[elements]] of speech, acting, and dance. Famous for the *Tarangam* sequence, where the dancer dances on the rim of a brass plate with a pitcher of water on their head. |
| **Odissi** | Odisha | Originates from the *Maharis* (temple dancers). Known as the 'water dance'. Characterized by the *Tribhanga* posture (a three-bend posture of the head, torso, and legs) and *Chowk* (a square stance). |
| **Manipuri** | Manipur | Deeply rooted in Vaishnavism (themes of Radha and [[Krishna]]'s Raas Leela). Very gentle, graceful, and gliding movements (*Lasya* dominant); faces remain mostly expressionless. Dancers wear a unique stiff, barrel-shaped skirt. |
| **Sattriya** | Assam | Introduced by the 15th-century Bhakti saint **Srimanta Sankardeva**. Originally performed only by male monks (*Bhokots*) in monasteries (*Sattras*). Themes revolve around [[Krishna]]. |
| **Mohiniyattam** | Kerala | Means 'Dance of the Enchantress' (referring to Vishnu's Mohini avatar). A solo dance performed exclusively by women. Extremely graceful, soft, and fluid movements. Dancers traditionally wear white/off-white sarees with gold brocade (Kasavu). |

## 2. Indian Classical Music
Indian classical music is broadly divided into two major traditions, which diverged around the 13th-14th centuries due to Persian/Islamic influences in the North.

### A. Hindustani Classical Music (North India)
*   **Influences:** A highly syncretic style blending ancient Hindu traditions with Persian and Arab musical concepts, heavily patronized by the [[Delhi Sultanate]] and Mughal courts.
*   **Key Concepts:** 
    *   Emphasis is more on improvisation and the exploration of the *Raga* (melodic framework).
    *   Strict adherence to the time of day and season for singing specific Ragas.
*   **Major Vocal Forms:**
    *   **Dhrupad:** The oldest and most austere form. Very structured, focusing on the purity of the Raga. Traditionally performed in temples. (e.g., Swami Haridas, Tansen).
    *   **Khyal:** Means 'imagination' in Persian. It is more free-flowing, romantic, and heavily ornamented compared to Dhrupad. It is the most popular form today.
    *   **Thumri:** A semi-classical, romantic, and highly emotional vocal form, often associated with [[the Bhakti movement]] ([[Krishna]] and Radha).
    *   **Tappa:** Originating from the folk songs of camel riders in Punjab. Known for rapid, complex, and rolling rhythmic patterns.
    *   **Ghazal:** A poetic form consisting of rhyming couplets, expressing the pain of loss or the beauty of love.
*   **Gharana System:** A unique feature of Hindustani music. A *Gharana* is a lineage or school of music (e.g., Gwalior, Agra, Jaipur-Atrauli, Kirana) that passes down a specific style of singing from guru to disciple.

### B. Carnatic Classical Music (South India)
*   **Influences:** Relatively uninfluenced by Persian/Islamic traditions, maintaining a purer, more indigenous form.
*   **Key Concepts:**
    *   Emphasis is on structured, composed pieces (Kritis) rather than pure improvisation. 
    *   The voice is often used as a musical instrument, closely matching the accompaniment.
*   **The Trinity of Carnatic Music:** The foundational composers who standardized the art form in the 18th century:
    1.  **Tyagaraja:** Composed primarily in Telugu in praise of Lord Rama.
    2.  **Muthuswami Dikshitar:** Composed primarily in Sanskrit.
    3.  **Syama Sastri:** Composed primarily in Telugu in praise of Goddess Kamakshi.
*   **Key Components:**
    *   *Shruti* (musical [[pitch]]).
    *   *Swara* (the actual musical note).
    *   *Raga* (the melodic formula).
    *   *Tala* (the rhythmic cycle).

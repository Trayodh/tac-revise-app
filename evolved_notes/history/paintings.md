# Indian Paintings

## 1. Mural Paintings
Mural [[paintings]] are large works executed directly on solid walls or ceilings. They are the earliest evidence of painting in India.

*   **Ajanta Caves (Maharashtra):** 
    *   The most famous murals in India, primarily depicting themes from **[[Buddhism]]** (the life of Buddha and Jataka tales).
    *   They were executed in the *fresco-secco* technique (painted on dry plaster).
    *   Key [[paintings]]: The **Padmapani** (Lotus Bearer) and Vajrapani Bodhisattvas, and the Dying Princess.
    *   Patronized heavily by the Vakataka rulers (especially Harishena).
*   **Ellora Caves (Maharashtra):** Contain murals related to all three major religions: Hinduism, [[Buddhism]], and [[Jainism]].
*   **Bagh Caves (Madhya Pradesh):** Similar to Ajanta but more [[secular]] in nature, portraying contemporary life.
*   **Sittanavasal (Tamil Nadu):** Famous for **Jain** mural [[paintings]] from the Pandya period.
*   **Lepakshi (Andhra Pradesh):** Excellent examples of Vijayanagara mural [[paintings]] (mostly Shiva themes).

## 2. Miniature Paintings
Miniature [[paintings]] are small, highly detailed [[paintings]] executed on perishable materials like palm leaves, paper, or cloth. They require intense focus and a magnifying [[glass]] to appreciate the fine details.

### A. Early Miniatures (Pala and Jaina Schools)
*   **Pala School (Eastern India):** Flourished in Bengal and Bihar (8th-11th century). Painted on palm leaves, primarily depicting **Buddhist** themes (Vajrayana [[Buddhism]]).
*   **Apabhramsa / Jaina School (Western India):** Flourished in Gujarat and Rajasthan (11th-15th century). Primarily depicted **Jain** texts like the *Kalpasutra*. Known for angular faces, prominent pointed noses, and bulging eyes extending beyond the face boundary.

### B. Mughal Miniatures
The Mughal period saw the zenith of miniature painting, blending Persian techniques with Indian themes.

*   **Characteristics:** Focus on [[secular]] themes (court scenes, hunting, portraits, flora and fauna) rather than religious themes. Extensive use of brilliant colors and gold.
*   **[[Humayun]]:** Brought Persian painters Mir Sayyid Ali and Abd-us-Samad to India.
*   **[[Akbar]]:** Established a formal *Karkhana* (workshop) for painters. Initiated massive illustrated manuscripts like the *Tutinama* and the *Hamzanama*. He encouraged the synthesis of Persian and Indian styles.
*   **[[Jahangir]]:** The **Golden Age** of Mughal painting. [[Jahangir]] was a profound connoisseur of art. The focus shifted from illustrated manuscripts to single-page portraits and incredibly detailed natural history [[paintings]] (birds, animals, flowers). Famous painters: Ustad Mansur (expert in flora/fauna) and Bishandas (expert in portraits).
*   **[[Shah Jahan]]:** Painting lost some of its vitality; the focus shifted more toward [[architecture]]. [[Paintings]] became overly rigid and formal, emphasizing the emperor's divine right.
*   **[[Aurangzeb]]:** Disliked painting and withdrew royal patronage. Painters migrated to regional courts (Rajputana and the Pahari region).

### C. Rajput (Rajasthani) School
*   **Origin:** Flourished in the princely courts of Rajasthan as an indigenous reaction against the Mughal style.
*   **Themes:** Deeply religious and romantic. Heavily inspired by the **[[Bhakti movement]]**, particularly the *[[Krishna]] Leela* (life of [[Krishna]] and Radha), the Ramayana, and the *Ragamala* (visual depictions of musical ragas).
*   **Sub-schools:**
    *   **Mewar (Udaipur):** Known for bold colors and emotional intensity (e.g., Sahibdin).
    *   **Kishangarh:** The most famous sub-school, known for the masterpiece **Bani Thani** (often called the Mona Lisa of India), painted by Nihal Chand, depicting Radha with a highly stylized, elongated face, arched eyebrows, and a sharp nose.
    *   **Bundi and Kota:** Famous for depictions of lush [[vegetation]] and hunting scenes.

### D. Pahari School
*   **Origin:** Flourished in the sub-Himalayan hill states (Himachal Pradesh, Jammu, Uttarakhand) in the 17th-19th centuries, largely developed by painters fleeing [[Aurangzeb]]'s court.
*   **Sub-schools:**
    *   **Basohli School:** The earliest Pahari style, known for its bold, intense colors and the unique use of small shiny green beetle wings to depict jewelry.
    *   **Kangra School:** The most refined Pahari style. Known for lyrical grace, delicate lines, and serene, romantic depictions of Radha and [[Krishna]] in lush green landscapes.

## 3. Modern Indian Painting
*   **Company Style (Kampani Kalam):** A hybrid Indo-European style that emerged in the 18th/19th century as Indian artists adapted their techniques (using water colors and perspective) to cater to the tastes of British [[East India Company]] officials.
*   **Raja Ravi Varma:** A pioneer of modern Indian art from Travancore. He famously blended Western techniques (oil painting, realism) with purely Indian mythological themes (Mahabharata, Ramayana). He started a lithographic press, making his prints accessible to the masses.
*   **Bengal School of Art:** An avant-garde nationalist movement reacting against Raja Ravi Varma's Western academic style. Led by **Abanindranath Tagore**, it sought to revive indigenous spiritual values in art, drawing inspiration from Ajanta and Mughal/Rajput miniatures. Abanindranath's painting of *Bharat Mata* is iconic.

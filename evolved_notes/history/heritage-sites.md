## 1. Introduction
A World Heritage Site is a landmark or area with legal protection by an international convention administered by the United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization (UNESCO). They are designated for having outstanding universal value to humanity.

India currently has **42** UNESCO World [[Heritage Sites]] (as of late 2023), making it the country with the sixth-largest number of sites in the world. These are categorized into:
*   **Cultural Sites (34)**
*   **Natural Sites (7)**
*   **Mixed Site (1)**

## 2. Key Cultural Heritage Sites (Selected)

### A. Ancient Monuments and Caves
*   **Ajanta Caves (Maharashtra):** Rock-cut Buddhist cave monuments (2nd century BCE to 480 CE) featuring masterpieces of Buddhist religious art and frescoes.
*   **Ellora Caves (Maharashtra):** Monasteries and temples (Hindu, Buddhist, and Jain) carved side-by-side into the rock wall, demonstrating the religious harmony prevalent during this period (600-1000 CE). The centerpiece is the monolithic Kailasanatha Temple.
*   **Elephanta Caves (Maharashtra):** Located on an island near Mumbai. Contains rock-cut temples dedicated primarily to Lord Shiva, featuring the famous 20-foot-high *Trimurti* (three-faced) Shiva sculpture.
*   **Sanchi Stupa (Madhya Pradesh):** The oldest stone structure in India, originally commissioned by Emperor [[Ashoka]]. A magnificent example of early Buddhist [[architecture]].
*   **Rock Shelters of Bhimbetka (Madhya Pradesh):** Five clusters of natural rock shelters displaying continuous habitation from the Paleolithic period. Famous for incredible prehistoric cave [[paintings]].

### B. Temple Complexes
*   **Sun Temple, Konark (Odisha):** A monumental representation of the sun god Surya's chariot, with 24 elaborately carved wheels pulled by 7 horses. Built by King Narasimhadeva I of the Eastern [[Ganga]] Dynasty.
*   **Group of Monuments at Mahabalipuram (Tamil Nadu):** A complex of sanctuaries carved out of rock along the Coromandel coast in the 7th and 8th centuries by the Pallava kings. Includes the Shore Temple and the Pancha Rathas.
*   **Great Living Chola Temples (Tamil Nadu):** Built by the kings of the Chola Empire. Includes the Brihadisvara Temple at Thanjavur, the Temple of Gangaikondacholapuram, and the Airavatesvara Temple at Darasuram. Masterpieces of Dravidian [[architecture]].
*   **Group of Monuments at Hampi (Karnataka):** The austere and grandiose site of the last capital of the great Hindu Kingdom of Vijayanagara. 
*   **Khajuraho Group of Monuments (Madhya Pradesh):** Built by the Chandella dynasty. Famous for their Nagara-style architectural symbolism and their erotic sculptures.

### C. Indo-Islamic Architecture
*   **Taj Mahal (Agra, UP):** An immense mausoleum of white marble, built between 1631 and 1648 by [[Shah Jahan]] in memory of his favorite wife, Mumtaz Mahal. The jewel of Muslim art in India.
*   **Agra Fort (UP):** A massive 16th-century Mughal red sandstone fortress enclosing palaces built by [[Akbar]] and [[Shah Jahan]].
*   **Fatehpur Sikri (UP):** Built by Emperor [[Akbar]], it served as the capital of the [[Mughal Empire]] for 10 years before being abandoned due to lack of water.
*   **Qutb Minar and its Monuments (Delhi):** Built in the early 13th century in red sandstone, the tower is 72.5m high. The complex includes the Quwwat-ul-Islam Mosque and the rust-resistant Iron Pillar of [[Chandragupta]] II.
*   **[[Humayun]]'s Tomb (Delhi):** Built in 1570, it is of particular cultural significance as it was the first garden-tomb on the Indian subcontinent, inspiring the Taj Mahal.

### D. Recent Additions
*   **Dholavira: a [[Harappan]] City (Gujarat) (2021):** One of the five largest [[Indus Valley Civilization]] sites, known for its sophisticated water management system.
*   **Kakatiya Rudreshwara (Ramappa) Temple (Telangana) (2021):** Known for its floating bricks and intricate carvings.
*   **Santiniketan (West Bengal) (2023):** Founded by Rabindranath Tagore as an ashram and later a university, blending Indian and global traditions.
*   **Sacred Ensembles of the Hoysalas (Karnataka) (2023):** Includes the spectacular star-shaped temples at Belur, Halebidu, and Somanathapura.

## 3. Key Natural Heritage Sites
*   **Kaziranga National Park (Assam):** Famous for holding two-thirds of the world's Great One-horned Rhinoceroses.
*   **Manas Wildlife Sanctuary (Assam):** A [[biodiversity]] hotspot known for rare and endangered endemic wildlife such as the Assam roofed turtle, hispid hare, golden langur, and pygmy hog.
*   **Keoladeo National Park (Rajasthan):** Formerly known as the Bharatpur Bird Sanctuary. A major wintering area for large numbers of aquatic birds from Afghanistan, Turkmenistan, China, and Siberia.
*   **Sundarbans National Park (West Bengal):** The largest mangrove forest in the world, covering the Ganges-[[Brahmaputra]] delta. Famous for the Royal Bengal Tiger.
*   **Western Ghats (Multiple States):** Older than the Himalaya mountains, it is recognized as one of the world's eight "hottest hotspots" of biological diversity.
*   **Nanda Devi and Valley of Flowers National Parks (Uttarakhand):** Set in the high western [[Himalayas]], known for spectacular landscapes and diverse alpine flora.
*   **Great Himalayan National Park [[Conservation]] Area (Himachal Pradesh):** Known for its pristine [[forests]] and snow-leopard habitat.

## 4. The Mixed Heritage Site
*   **Khangchendzonga National Park (Sikkim):** The only mixed (both cultural and natural significance) heritage site in India. It includes Mount Khangchendzonga, the third highest peak in the world, and holds immense cultural and religious significance for the indigenous people of Sikkim.

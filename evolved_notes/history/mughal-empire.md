## 1. Establishment and Early Rulers

### Babur (1526 – 1530)
*   **Foundation:** Zahir-ud-din Muhammad [[Babur]], a descendant of Timur (paternal) and Genghis Khan (maternal), invaded India from Kabul at the invitation of Daulat Khan Lodi.
*   **First Battle of Panipat (1526):** Defeated Ibrahim Lodi. This marked the end of the [[Delhi Sultanate]] and the establishment of the [[Mughal Empire]]. [[Babur]]'s victory was largely due to his effective use of **artillery (cannons)** and the *Tulughma* flanking tactic.
*   **Battle of Khanwa (1527):** Defeated Rana Sanga of Mewar, securing Mughal dominance in North India.
*   **[[Literature]]:** Wrote his autobiography, **Tuzuk-i-Baburi (Baburnama)**, in Chagatai Turkic (later translated into Persian by Abdur Rahim Khan-i-Khana).

### Humayun (1530 – 1540 & 1555 – 1556)
*   His reign was marked by instability due to his ambitious brothers and the rising [[power]] of the Afghans in the east.
*   **Defeat:** Decisively defeated by the Afghan leader **Sher Shah Suri** at the Battle of Chausa (1539) and the Battle of Kannauj/Bilgram (1540), forcing him into a 15-year exile in Persia.
*   **Restoration:** Reclaimed the throne in 1555 with Persian help but died shortly after by falling from the stairs of his library (*Sher Mandal*).
*   **[[Literature]]:** His biography, **[[Humayun]]-nama**, was written by his half-sister, Gulbadan Begum.

## 2. The Sur Interregnum (1540 – 1555)
*   **Sher Shah Suri:** A brilliant administrator and general who temporarily ousted the Mughals.
*   **Reforms:** 
    *   Introduced a silver coin called **Rupia**, which became the precursor to the modern Rupee.
    *   Built the **Grand Trunk Road** (Sadak-i-Azam) connecting Sonargaon (Bengal) to Peshawar.
    *   Standardized weights and measures and established a highly efficient postal system using horse relays.
*   **Tomb:** Built his own magnificent octagonal tomb at **Sasaram** (Bihar).

## 3. Akbar the Great (1556 – 1605)
Considered the greatest Mughal emperor due to his vast conquests, administrative reforms, and policy of religious tolerance.

*   **Second Battle of Panipat (1556):** His regent, Bairam Khan, defeated Hemu (the Hindu general of the Sur dynasty).
*   **Rajput Policy:** Forged strong alliances with the [[Rajputs]] through matrimonial ties (e.g., married Harkha Bai / Jodha Bai of Amber) and appointed [[Rajputs]] (like Raja Man Singh) to high military and administrative posts. Only Mewar (Maharana Pratap) offered stubborn [[resistance]] (Battle of Haldighati, 1576).
*   **Religious Policy:**
    *   Abolished the **Jizya** (poll tax on non-Muslims) and the pilgrimage tax.
    *   Built the **Ibadat Khana** (House of Worship) at Fatehpur Sikri to host religious and philosophical debates among scholars of all faiths (Islam, Hinduism, [[Jainism]], Zoroastrianism, Christianity).
    *   Promulgated a new syncretic faith called **Din-i-Ilahi** (Divine Faith), which focused on universal tolerance (*Sulh-i-Kul*), though it gained very few followers (Birbal was the only Hindu to join).
*   **Navaratnas (Nine Jewels):** His court included luminaries like Abul Fazl (author of *Akbarnama*), Todar Mal (Finance Minister), Tansen (musician), and Birbal.

## 4. Jahangir and Shah Jahan

### Jahangir (1605 – 1627)
*   His reign is noted for the zenith of **Mughal painting**, which shifted focus from manuscript illustration to portraiture and natural history (flora and fauna).
*   **Nur Jahan:** His wife, who became the de facto ruler and wielded immense political [[power]] behind the throne.
*   **Captain William Hawkins** and **Sir Thomas Roe** (ambassador of King James I) visited his court to secure trading rights for the English [[East India Company]].
*   He ordered the execution of the 5th Sikh Guru, Guru Arjan Dev, turning the peaceful Sikh community against the Mughals.

### Shah Jahan (1628 – 1658)
*   His reign is considered the **Golden Age of Mughal [[Architecture]]**.
*   Built the **Taj Mahal** (in memory of his wife Mumtaz Mahal), the **Red Fort** (Lal Qila) in Delhi, the **Jama Masjid** in Delhi, and commissioned the legendary **Peacock Throne**.
*   Shifted the capital from Agra to his newly built city, **Shahjahanabad** (Old Delhi).
*   His reign ended tragically when he was imprisoned in the Agra Fort by his son, [[Aurangzeb]], after a bloody war of succession among his four sons (Dara Shikoh, Shuja, [[Aurangzeb]], and Murad).

## 5. Aurangzeb (1658 – 1707)
The last of the "Great Mughals." His reign marked the territorial zenith of the empire but sowed the seeds of its destruction.

*   **Titles:** Assumed the title *Alamgir* (Conqueror of the World) and was known as a *Zinda Pir* (Living Saint) for his austere, orthodox Sunni lifestyle.
*   **Reversals of [[Akbar]]'s Policies:** 
    *   Reimposed the **Jizya** on Hindus.
    *   Banned music at the royal court, discontinued the practice of *Jharokha Darshan* (public appearance), and destroyed several Hindu temples.
*   **Deccan Ulcer:** He spent the last 25 years of his life in the Deccan fighting the [[Marathas]] (led by [[Shivaji]]) and the Sultanates of Bijapur and Golconda. This prolonged war drained the imperial treasury, ruined the economy, and fatally weakened the central administration in the North.
*   Executed the 9th Sikh Guru, Guru Tegh Bahadur, leading to the militarization of the Sikhs under Guru Gobind Singh (creation of the Khalsa).

## 6. Administration and Revenue System

### The Mansabdari System
Introduced by [[Akbar]], this was the unique military and civil administrative system of the Mughals.

![Mansabdari System in the Mughal Empire](/assets/diagrams/history_4_medieval_india_mughal_empire_mansabdari_system_mughal.png)

*   Every officer was assigned a **Mansab** (rank), which determined his salary and status.
*   The rank had two components:
    *   **Zat:** Indicated the personal status and salary of the noble.
    *   **Sawar:** Indicated the number of cavalrymen (horses) he was required to maintain for the emperor.
*   The system was non-hereditary, and officers were paid either in cash (*Naqdi*) or through assignments of land revenue (*Jagir*).

### Revenue System (Zabti / Dahsala System)
Developed by [[Akbar]]'s finance minister, **Raja Todar Mal**.
*   Land was accurately measured using a standard bamboo system with iron rings.
*   Revenue was fixed based on a 10-year average of the crop yield and prices (Dahsala system).
*   The state's share was generally fixed at one-third of the average produce.

### Central Administration
![Mughal Administration Overview](/assets/diagrams/history_4_medieval_india_mughal_empire_mughal_administration_chart.png)

*   **Wazir (Diwan):** Head of the revenue and finance department.
*   **Mir Bakhshi:** Head of the military department and intelligence (responsible for the Mansabdari system).
*   **Mir Saman:** In charge of the imperial household and royal workshops (*Karkhanas*).
*   **Sadr-us-Sudur:** Head of religious affairs and charitable grants.
*   **Qazi-ul-Quzat:** Chief [[Justice]].

## 7. Decline of the Empire
After [[Aurangzeb]]'s death in 1707, a series of weak "Later Mughals" ruled. The empire rapidly disintegrated due to:
*   Wars of succession and weak, incompetent successors.
*   The rise of powerful regional states (Awadh, Bengal, Hyderabad).
*   The devastating invasions of **Nadir Shah** (1739 - who looted the Peacock Throne and Koh-i-Noor diamond) and **Ahmad Shah Abdali**.
*   The rise of the Maratha [[power]].
*   The arrival and expansion of the British [[East India Company]]. The empire formally ended in 1857 when the British deposed Bahadur Shah Zafar.

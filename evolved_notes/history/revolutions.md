## 1. The American Revolution (1765 – 1783)
The American Revolution was a political and ideological revolution where the Thirteen American Colonies rejected the British monarchy and aristocracy, overthrew the authority of Great Britain, and founded the United States of America.

*   **Causes:**
    *   **"No Taxation Without Representation":** The [[British Parliament]] passed the Stamp Act and Townshend Acts to tax the colonies to pay for the Seven Years' War, without giving the colonists representation in [[Parliament]].
    *   **The Boston Tea Party (1773):** Colonists dumped British tea into the harbor to protest the Tea Act. Britain responded with the Intolerable Acts, closing the port of Boston.
    *   Influence of Enlightenment thinkers (John Locke, Thomas Paine's *Common Sense*).
*   **Key Events:**
    *   **Declaration of Independence (July 4, 1776):** Drafted primarily by **Thomas Jefferson**, asserting that all men are created equal and have unalienable rights to "Life, [[Liberty]] and the pursuit of Happiness."
    *   **Battles:** The Battles of Lexington and Concord (first shots), Saratoga (turning point, brought French support), and Yorktown (final British surrender).
    *   **Leadership:** George Washington commanded the Continental Army.
*   **Significance:** It was the first successful [[democratic]] revolution in modern history. It established a [[republic]] based on a written [[constitution]] and a [[bill]] of rights, deeply inspiring the French Revolution.

## 2. The French Revolution (1789 – 1799)
A watershed event in modern European history that sought to completely change the relationship between the rulers and those they governed and to redefine the nature of political [[power]].

*   **Causes:**
    *   **Social Inequality:** The archaic estate system. The 1st Estate (Clergy) and 2nd Estate (Nobility) enjoyed immense privileges and paid almost no taxes, while the 3rd Estate (Commoners, 98% of the [[population]]) bore the entire tax burden.
    *   **Economic Crisis:** France was practically bankrupt due to King Louis XVI's lavish spending (Palace of Versailles) and funding the American Revolution. Heavy bread shortages.
    *   **Enlightenment Ideas:** Philosophers like Rousseau (Social Contract), Voltaire, and Montesquieu challenged the absolute right to rule.
*   **Key Events:**
    *   **Fall of the Bastille (July 14, 1789):** An angry mob stormed the Bastille prison, a symbol of royal tyranny. This is celebrated as French National Day.
    *   **Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen:** Asserted that "men are born and remain free and equal in rights."
    *   **Reign of Terror (1793-1794):** Led by Maximilien Robespierre and the Jacobins. Thousands of "enemies of the revolution," including King Louis XVI and Marie Antoinette, were executed using the guillotine.
*   **Significance:** It popularized the ideals of **[[Liberty]], [[Equality]], and [[Fraternity]]**. It ended absolute monarchy and feudalism in France, paving the way for the rise of Napoleon Bonaparte.

## 3. The Industrial Revolution (c. 1760 – 1840)
While not a political revolution, this was a monumental shift in human history from agrarian, handicraft economies to industry and machine manufacturing.

*   **Origin:** Began in **Great Britain**. Why Britain? Abundant coal and iron, a stable government, colonial markets (like India) for raw materials, and the enclosure movement which created a pool of cheap labor.
*   **Key Innovations:**
    *   **Textiles:** The Flying Shuttle, Spinning Jenny, and [[Power]] Loom mechanized cotton spinning.
    *   **Steam [[Power]]:** James Watt perfected the steam engine, replacing water and muscle [[power]].
    *   **Transportation:** George Stephenson's steam locomotive (the railway) and Robert Fulton's steamboat.
*   **Impact:**
    *   **[[Urbanization]]:** Massive migration from villages to terribly overcrowded and unsanitary factory cities (slums).
    *   **Social Classes:** Created the industrial working class (proletariat) and the wealthy industrial middle class (bourgeoisie).
    *   **Child Labor:** Widespread exploitation of children and women in dangerous factory conditions.
    *   It led to the rise of new economic ideologies: **Capitalism** (Adam Smith) and eventually **Socialism/Communism** (Karl Marx) as a reaction to capitalist exploitation.

## 4. The Russian Revolution (1917)
The Russian Revolution dismantled the Tsarist autocracy and led to the creation of the Soviet Union, the world's first constitutionally [[socialist]] state.

*   **Causes:**
    *   **Autocracy:** The oppressive rule of Tsar Nicholas II (Romanov dynasty).
    *   **Economic Hardship:** Extreme poverty among peasants and horrible working conditions for the newly emerging industrial class.
    *   **[[World War I]]:** The war was an absolute disaster for Russia, leading to massive casualties, food shortages, and economic collapse.
*   **Two Phases:**
    1.  **The February Revolution (1917):** [[Mass]] protests in Petrograd led to the abdication of Tsar Nicholas II. A moderate Provisional Government was formed.
    2.  **The October Revolution (Bolshevik Revolution) (1917):** The Provisional Government failed to pull Russia out of WWI. **Vladimir Lenin** and his radical Bolshevik Party (Promising "Peace, Land, and Bread") staged a bloodless coup and seized [[power]].
*   **Aftermath:** The Bolsheviks pulled Russia out of WWI (Treaty of Brest-Litovsk), executed the royal family, and won a brutal Civil War against the anti-communist "White Army."
*   **Significance:** It established Communism as a major global political [[force]], leading directly to the [[Cold War]].

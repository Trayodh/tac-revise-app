# Geomorphology: Rocks, Plate Tectonics & Volcanism

## The Dynamic Earth: Shaping Our World

Geomorphology is the scientific study of landforms and the processes that create, modify, and destroy them. This module focuses on the fundamental building blocks of our planet – rocks – and the powerful internal forces of plate tectonics and volcanism that continuously shape Earth's surface. Understanding these concepts is crucial for Indian Defence Examinations like NDA, CDS, AFCAT, CAPF, and INET, as they form the basis of physical geography and environmental studies.

### 1. Earth's Interior: Unveiling the Depths

The Earth's interior is structured in concentric layers, each with distinct physical and chemical properties. Our understanding of these layers primarily comes from indirect sources like seismic waves.

#### 1.1. Sources of Information about Earth's Interior

*   **Direct Sources:**
    *   **Deep Mining:** Gold mines (e.g., Mponeng gold mine in South Africa, ~3-4 km deep) provide direct observation of rock types and conditions at shallow depths.
    *   **Volcanic Eruptions:** Bring molten material (magma/lava) from the upper mantle to the surface, offering clues about its composition.
    *   **Deep Ocean Drilling Projects:** Such as the Integrated Ocean Drilling Program (IODP), aim to extract core samples from the oceanic crust and mantle.
*   **Indirect Sources (Primary for deep interior):**
    *   **Seismic Waves (Earthquake Waves):** The most crucial source. Their velocity and path change as they pass through different materials, revealing discontinuities and properties of Earth's layers.
    *   **Gravitational Force:** Variations in gravity (gravity anomalies) indicate uneven distribution of mass within the Earth.
    *   **Magnetic Field:** The Earth's magnetic field is generated by the movement of molten iron in the outer core.
    *   **Pressure and Temperature:** Both increase with depth, influencing the state and density of materials.

#### 1.2. Layers of the Earth's Interior

The Earth's interior is broadly divided into three main layers:

1.  **Crust:**
    *   **Definition:** The outermost and thinnest solid layer.
    *   **Thickness:** Varies from ~5-70 km (oceanic crust: ~5-10 km, continental crust: ~30-70 km).
    *   **Composition:** Primarily lighter silicates (Silica and Aluminium - **SIAL**). Oceanic crust is denser, composed mainly of basalt and gabbro. Continental crust is less dense, composed mainly of granite.
    *   **Density:** Average ~2.7 g/cm³.
    *   **State:** Solid.
    *   **Mohorovičić Discontinuity (Moho):** Marks the boundary between the crust and the mantle.

2.  **Mantle:**
    *   **Definition:** The layer beneath the crust, extending to about 2,900 km depth.
    *   **Composition:** Denser silicates rich in Magnesium and Iron (**SIMA**).
    *   **Density:** Average ~3.3-5.7 g/cm³.
    *   **State:** Semi-solid (plastic, ductile) in the upper part (asthenosphere), more rigid in the lower part.
    *   **Sub-layers:**
        *   **Upper Mantle (Asthenosphere):** Extends from Moho to ~400 km. It is a highly viscous, mechanically weak, and ductile region where rocks are close to their melting point, allowing for plate movement.
        *   **Lower Mantle:** Extends from ~400 km to 2,900 km. It is more rigid due to immense pressure.
    *   **Repetti Discontinuity:** Separates the upper mantle from the lower mantle.
    *   **Gutenberg Discontinuity:** Marks the boundary between the mantle and the outer core.

3.  **Core:**
    *   **Definition:** The innermost and densest layer, extending to the Earth's center (~6,371 km).
    *   **Composition:** Primarily Nickel and Iron (**NIFE**).
    *   **Density:** Average ~10-13 g/cm³.
    *   **Sub-layers:**
        *   **Outer Core:** Extends from 2,900 km to 5,100 km. It is in a **liquid state**, responsible for generating Earth's magnetic field through convection currents.
        *   **Inner Core:** Extends from 5,100 km to the Earth's center. It is in a **solid state** due to extreme pressure, despite very high temperatures.
    *   **Lehmann Discontinuity:** Separates the outer core from the inner core.

#### 1.3. Discontinuities of the Earth

These are zones where seismic wave velocities change abruptly, indicating a change in material properties.

*   **Conrad Discontinuity:** Between upper and lower continental crust.
*   **Mohorovičić (Moho) Discontinuity:** Between crust and mantle.
*   **Repetti Discontinuity:** Between upper mantle and lower mantle.
*   **Gutenberg Discontinuity:** Between mantle and outer core.
*   **Lehmann Discontinuity:** Between outer core and inner core.

### 2. Rocks: Earth's Building Blocks

Rocks are naturally occurring solid aggregates of one or more minerals. They are classified based on their mode of formation.

#### 2.1. Classification of Rocks

1.  **Igneous Rocks (Primary Rocks):**
    *   **Formation:** Formed from the cooling and solidification of molten magma (underground) or lava (on the surface). They are called primary rocks because all other rocks are derived from them.
    *   **Characteristics:** Hard, granular, crystalline, do not contain fossils, do not form strata (layers).
    *   **Types:**
        *   **Intrusive Igneous Rocks (Plutonic):** Formed when magma cools slowly beneath the Earth's surface, leading to large crystals.
            *   **Examples:** Granite, Diorite, Gabbro.
            *   **Forms:**
                *   **Batholiths:** Large, dome-shaped intrusive bodies, often granitic.
                *   **Laccoliths:** Large, dome-shaped masses connected by a pipe-like conduit from below.
                *   **Lopoliths:** Saucer-shaped, concave to the sky.
                *   **Phacoliths:** Lens-shaped bodies found at the crests and troughs of folds.
                *   **Sills:** Horizontal sheets of solidified magma between sedimentary layers.
                *   **Dykes:** Vertical or near-vertical walls of solidified magma cutting across existing rock layers.
        *   **Extrusive Igneous Rocks (Volcanic):** Formed when lava cools rapidly on the Earth's surface, resulting in fine-grained or glassy textures.
            *   **Examples:** Basalt, Obsidian, Andesite, Rhyolite.
    *   **Silica Content Classification:**
        *   **Acidic Igneous Rocks:** High silica content (e.g., Granite). Viscous, lighter in color, forms dome structures.
        *   **Basic Igneous Rocks:** Low silica content (e.g., Gabbro, Basalt). Less viscous, darker in color, forms flat-top structures.

2.  **Sedimentary Rocks (Secondary Rocks):**
    *   **Formation:** Formed from the accumulation, compaction, and cementation of sediments derived from the weathering and erosion of older rocks, plants, and animals.
    *   **Characteristics:** Often layered (strata), may contain fossils, relatively soft.
    *   **Types:**
        *   **Mechanically Formed:** Formed from fragments of other rocks.
            *   **Examples:** Sandstone, Conglomerate, Shale, Loess.
        *   **Chemically Formed:** Formed from precipitation of minerals from water.
            *   **Examples:** Gypsum, Rock Salt, Saltpetre.
        *   **Organically Formed:** Formed from the remains of plants and animals.
            *   **Examples:** Limestone, Dolomite, Coal, Peat, Chalk, Lignite.

3.  **Metamorphic Rocks (Changed Form Rocks):**
    *   **Formation:** Formed when existing igneous or sedimentary rocks undergo changes in physical or chemical composition due to intense heat, pressure, or chemically active fluids (metamorphism).
    *   **Characteristics:** Very hard, crystalline, often show foliation (layered texture) or lineation (linear arrangement of minerals), generally do not contain fossils (original fossils are destroyed).
    *   **Examples:**
        *   **From Igneous:** Granite → Gneiss; Basalt → Schist.
        *   **From Sedimentary:** Shale → Slate → Phyllite → Schist; Limestone → Marble; Sandstone → Quartzite; Coal → Graphite.

#### 2.2. Rock Cycle

The rock cycle describes the transitions through geological time among the three main rock types: igneous, sedimentary, and metamorphic.

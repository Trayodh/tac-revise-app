## 🌌 Part 1: The Universe and Earth's Structure

### 1. The Universe and Solar System

The Universe encompasses all that exists – physical matter, energy, space, and time. Its study is known as **Cosmology**.

#### 1.1 Theories of Origin

*   **Big Bang Theory (Georges Lemaître):** The most accepted theory. Proposes that the universe originated from an extremely hot, dense point approximately 13.8 billion years ago, expanding and cooling to form galaxies and stars.
*   **Steady State Theory (Bondi, Gold, Fred Hoyle):** Suggests that the universe is continuously expanding, but new matter is constantly created to maintain a constant average density.
*   **Pulsating Theory:** Hypothesizes that the universe undergoes alternating phases of expansion and contraction.

#### 1.2 Galaxies

Giant assemblies of stars, planets, gases, and dust, held together by gravity.

*   **Milky Way (Akash Ganga):** Our galaxy, containing about 300 billion stars, including our Sun.
*   **Andromeda:** Our nearest spiral galaxy.
*   **Types of Galaxies:**
    *   **Spiral:** Central nucleus with spiral arms (e.g., Milky Way, Andromeda).
    *   **Elliptical:** Lacking spiral arms, more spherical or elongated.
    *   **Irregular:** No definite shape.

#### 1.3 The Solar System

Comprises the Sun, 8 planets, their moons, and other non-stellar objects.

*   **Sun:** The central star, accounting for over 99% of the solar system's mass. Energy is generated by nuclear fusion (hydrogen to helium).
    *   **Solar Winds:** Streams of protons emitted from the Sun.
    *   **Solar Flares:** Bursts of incandescent material, posing a threat to satellite communication.
    *   **Aurora:** Solar wind particles trapped by Earth's magnetic field, creating light displays (Aurora Borealis in North, Australis in South).
    *   **Sunspots:** Cooler, darker regions on the Sun's surface with an 11-year periodicity, influencing global climate.
*   **Planets:** Heavenly bodies orbiting the Sun in elliptical paths.

### 2. Earth's Interior

The Earth's interior is studied through **direct sources** (deep mining, volcanic eruptions) and **indirect sources** (seismic waves, gravity anomaly, magnetic field). [Class Notes]

#### 2.1 Layers of the Earth

The Earth's interior is divided into three main concentric layers:

1.  **Crust:**
    *   **Thickness:** Outermost and thinnest layer (8-40 km). Varies from 5-30 km (oceanic) to 50-70 km (continental).
    *   **Composition:** Rich in lighter minerals like silica (Si) and aluminium (Al). Hence, called **Sial**.
    *   **Density:** Least dense, about 2.7 gm/cm³.
    *   **Nature:** Brittle.
    *   **Lithosphere:** Comprises the crust and the uppermost part of the mantle. [Class Notes]

2.  **Mantle:**
    *   **Thickness:** About 2900 km.
    *   **Composition:** Rich in silica (Si) and magnesium (Mg). Hence, called **Sima**.
    *   **Density:** About 5.68 gm/cm³.
    *   **Nature:** Semi-solid state.
    *   **Upper Mantle (Asthenosphere):** About 250 km thick, where magma originates.

3.  **Core:**
    *   **Thickness:** Innermost layer, about 3500 km radius.
    *   **Composition:** Large concentration of nickel (Ni) and iron (Fe). Hence, called **Nife**.
    *   **Density:** Highest, about 17.2 gm/cm³ (average Earth density 5.53 gm/cm³).
    *   **Nature:** Outer core is liquid, inner core is solid.

#### 2.2 Discontinuities

These are transitional zones where seismic wave velocities change abruptly, indicating a change in material properties.

*   **Conrad Discontinuity:** Between Upper and Lower Crust.
*   **Mohorovičić (Moho) Discontinuity:** Between Crust and Mantle.
*   **Repetti Discontinuity:** Between Upper and Lower Mantle.
*   **Gutenberg Discontinuity:** Between Mantle and Core.
*   **Lehmann Discontinuity:** Between Outer and Inner Core.

#### 2.3 Composition of Whole Earth & Crust

*   **Whole Earth:** Iron (35%), Oxygen (30%), Silicon (15%), Magnesium (13%), Nickel (2.4%), Sulphur (1.9%).
*   **Earth's Crust:** Oxygen (46%), Silicon (28%), Aluminium (8%), Iron (6%), Magnesium (4%), Calcium (2.4%), Potassium (2.3%), Sodium (2.1%).

### 3. Forces Affecting Earth's Crust

These forces cause movements and modifications of landforms.

#### 3.1 Endogenetic Forces (Internal)

Originating from within the Earth (heat, radioactivity).

*   **Sudden/Catastrophic Forces:** Act swiftly and unpredictably.
    *   **Earthquakes:** Vibrations caused by sudden release of energy.
    *   **Volcanoes:** Eruption of molten rock and gases.
*   **Slow/Diastrophic Forces:** Act imperceptibly over millions of years, causing large-scale deformation.
    *   **Orogeny:** Mountain building (e.g., Himalayas, Andes).
    *   **Epeirogeny:** Continent building (uplift/subsidence of large landmasses).
    *   **Faulting:** Fractures in the crust.
    *   **Folding:** Bending of rock layers.

#### 3.2 Exogenetic Forces (External)

Operating on the Earth's surface (solar energy, gravity, wind, water, ice).

*   **Weathering:** Disintegration or decomposition of rocks *in situ* (static process).
    *   **Physical Weathering:** Mechanical breakdown (e.g., frost wedging, thermal expansion, abrasion).
    *   **Chemical Weathering:** Decomposition by chemical changes (e.g., solution, oxidation, carbonation, hydration).
    *   **Biological Weathering:** Action of plants, animals, and humans (e.g., root wedging, burrowing).
*   **Erosion:** Removal and transportation of weathered material by mobile agents (wind, water, ice, gravity).

### 4. Plate Tectonics Theory

The lithosphere is divided into several rigid segments called **plates** that move over the semi-molten asthenosphere.

*   **Major Plates:** Eurasia, Antarctica, North America, South America, Pacific, African, Indian.
*   **Plate Boundaries:**
    1.  **Divergent Plate Margin (Constructive):** Plates move apart, creating new crustal material (e.g., Mid-Atlantic Ridge, Surtsey Island).
    2.  **Convergent Plate Margin (Destructive):** Plates collide, with one subducting under the other, leading to crustal destruction, volcanic activity, and mountain building (e.g., Himalayas, Pacific Ring of Fire, deep oceanic trenches).
    3.  **Transform Fault Boundary (Conservative):** Plates slide horizontally past each other, neither creating nor destroying crust (e.g., San Andreas Fault).

### 5. Earthquakes

Vibrations of the Earth's surface caused by the sudden release of energy.

*   **Focus (Hypocenter):** Place of origin of the earthquake within the Earth.
*   **Epicenter:** Point on the ground surface directly above the focus.
*   **Seismology:** Study of earthquakes.
*   **Seismic Waves:**
    1.  **Primary Waves (P-waves):** Longitudinal, fastest, travel through solids, liquids, and gases.
    2.  **Secondary Waves (S-waves):** Transverse, slower, travel only through solids (cannot pass through liquid outer core).
    3.  **Surface Waves (L-waves):** Long wavelength, confined to Earth's surface, most destructive.
    *   **Shadow Zones:** Areas where P and S waves are not recorded due to Earth's interior structure. S-wave shadow zone is larger (entire zone beyond 105° from epicenter).
*   **Measurement Scales:**
    *   **Richter Scale:** Measures magnitude (energy released).
    *   **Mercalli Scale:** Measures intensity (damage caused).
*   **Distribution:** Most earthquakes occur along plate boundaries:
    *   **Circum-Pacific Belt (Pacific Ring of Fire):** Most active.
    *   **Mid-Continental Belt:** Himalayas, Mediterranean Sea.
    *   **Mid-Atlantic Belt:** Divergent boundary.

### 6. Volcanism

Phenomena related to the movement of heated material (magma) from the interior to the surface.

*   **Volcano:** A vent through which magma, gases, and rock fragments are ejected.
*   **Causes:** Increase in temperature with depth, heat from radioactive elements, reduction in pressure due to fractures.
*   **Types by Eruption Mode:**
    *   **Central/Explosive Type:** Magma erupts with great force through a small vent (e.g., Hawaiian, Strombolian, Pelean, Vesuvius).
    *   **Fissure/Quiet Eruption:** Large quantities of lava flow quietly from fissures, forming lava plateaus (e.g., Deccan Plateau).
*   **Types by Periodicity:**
    *   **Active:** Erupts periodically (e.g., Etna, Stromboli, Mayon).
    *   **Dormant:** Quiet for some time, but may erupt again (e.g., Fujiyama, Krakatoa, Barren Island).
    *   **Extinct:** No indication of future eruption.
*   **Types by Shape/Lava:**
    *   **Shield Volcanoes:** Largest, made of basic (less viscous) lava, gentle slopes (e.g., Mauna Loa, Hawaii). [Class Notes]
    *   **Composite/Stratovolcanoes:** Cone-shaped, made of acidic (more viscous) lava, pyroclastic material, steep slopes (e.g., Vesuvius, Fujiyama). [Class Notes]
    *   **Caldera:** Large basin-shaped depression formed when a volcano collapses after an eruption. [Class Notes]
*   **Volcanic Belts:**
    *   **Circum-Pacific Belt (Pacific Ring of Fire):** Extends across Kamchatka, Kurile Islands, Japan, Philippines, New Guinea, New Zealand.
    *   **Mid-Continental Belt:** Alpine mountain chain, Mediterranean Sea, East African Rift Zone (e.g., Stromboli, Vesuvius, Etna, Kilimanjaro).
    *   **Mid-Atlantic Belt:** Fissure eruption type (e.g., Iceland, Canary Islands).

### 7. Landforms

Features on the Earth's surface (mountains, plateaus, plains).

#### 7.1 Orders of Relief

1.  **First Order:** Continents and Ocean Basins.
2.  **Second Order:** Mountains, Plateaus, Plains.
3.  **Third Order:** Valleys, Cliffs, Beaches, Sand Dunes, Caves.

#### 7.2 Major Landform Types

*   **Fluvial Landforms (by Rivers):**
    *   **Erosional:** V-shaped valleys, gorges, canyons, waterfalls, rapids, potholes, river terraces.
    *   **Depositional:** Alluvial fans, deltas, floodplains, natural levees, point bars.
*   **Karst Landforms (by Groundwater in Limestone/Dolomite):**
    *   **Erosional:** Sinkholes, lapies (limestone pavement), caves.
    *   **Depositional:** Stalactites (hang from ceiling), stalagmites (rise from floor), pillars (joined stalactites/stalagmites).
*   **Glacial Landforms (by Glaciers):**
    *   **Erosional:** Cirques (corries), U-shaped valleys, horns, arêtes (serrated ridges), glacial troughs, hanging valleys.
    *   **Depositional:** Moraines, eskers, outwash plains, drumlins, nunataks.
*   **Coastal Landforms (by Sea Waves):**
    *   **Erosional:** Cliffs, terraces, caves, stacks.
    *   **Depositional:** Beaches, dunes, bars, barriers, spits.
*   **Aeolian Landforms (by Wind in Deserts):**
    *   **Erosional:** Pediments, pediplains, playas, deflation hollows, mushroom rocks, table rocks.
    *   **Depositional:** Sand dunes (barchans, seifs), loess.

#### 7.3 Mountains

Based on formation:

1.  **Fold Mountains:** Formed by compressive forces (plate collision).
    *   **Young/New:** Himalayas, Andes, Rockies, Alps.
    *   **Old:** Aravallis (India), Appalachians (US), Pennines (Europe).
2.  **Block Mountains:** Formed by faulting (uplifted block = horst, subsided block = graben).
    *   Vindhya and Satpura (India), Vosges (France), Black Forest (Germany).
3.  **Volcanic Mountains:** Formed by accumulation of volcanic material (e.g., Mt. Fuji, Cotopaxi, Vesuvius, Etna, Mayon).
4.  **Residual/Dissected Mountains:** Formed by erosion of plateaus and high plains (e.g., Girnar, Rajmahal Hills in India, Catskill Mountains).

#### 7.4 Plateaus

Tabular uplands with relief > 500 feet (e.g., Tibetan Plateau - highest).

*   **Tectonic Plateaus:** Formed by Earth movements (uplift).
    *   **Intermontane Plateaus:** Enclosed by fold mountains (e.g., Tibetan Plateau, Bolivian Plateau).
*   **Volcanic Plateaus:** Formed by lava accumulation (e.g., Deccan Plateau).
*   **Dissected Plateaus:** Worn down by weathering/erosion (e.g., Scottish Highlands).

#### 7.5 Plains

Low-lying, flat land surfaces.

*   **Structural Plains:** Formed by uplift of sea floor (e.g., Great Plains of USA).
*   **Erosional Plains:** Formed by erosion of elevated land (e.g., Plains of North Canada).
*   **Depositional Plains:** Formed by sediment filling depressions (e.g., Indo-Ganga Plain).

#### 7.6 Islands

Land surrounded by water.

*   **Continental Islands:** Rest on continental shelf, close to continent (e.g., Greenland, Great Britain).
*   **Volcanic Islands:** Formed by submarine volcanoes rising above water.
*   **Coral Islands:** Formed by accumulation of coral skeletons (e.g., Lakshadweep).
*   **Alluvial Islands:** Formed by sediment deposition at river mouths (e.g., New Moore Island).
*   **Barrier Islands:** Formed by wave deposition of sand on shallow ocean bottom.
*   **Tectonic Islands:** Formed by tectonic activity, often in island arcs (e.g., Indonesia, Philippines, Japan).

#### 7.7 Lakes

Bodies of water surrounded by land.

*   **Tarn Lakes (Alpine Lakes):** Glacial origin, formed in cirques.
*   **Rift Valley Lakes:** Formed where rock pulls apart (e.g., Lake Nyasa).
*   **Crater Lakes:** Formed in volcanic peaks after collapse (e.g., Crater Lake, Oregon).
*   **Deflation Lakes:** Formed by wind erosion in deserts (e.g., Oases in Sahara).
*   **Oxbow Lakes:** Cut-off river meanders (e.g., Mississippi River).
*   **Artificial Lakes:** Formed by damming rivers (reservoirs).

## 8. The Atmosphere

The atmosphere is a thick gaseous envelope that surrounds the Earth, held in place by gravitational force.

### 8.1 Composition

The atmosphere is primarily composed of gases, water vapour, and particulates.

*   **Gases:**
    *   **Nitrogen (N₂):** 78.08% (most abundant).
    *   **Oxygen (O₂):** 20.95%.
    *   **Argon (Ar):** 0.93%.
    *   **Carbon Dioxide (CO₂):** 0.036% (a greenhouse gas).
    *   Trace gases: Neon, Helium, Ozone, Hydrogen.
*   **Water Vapour:** Varies from 0-4% by volume. Crucial for weather phenomena like condensation and precipitation.
*   **Particulates:** Dust particles, salt particles, smoke, pollen, etc., acting as hygroscopic nuclei for cloud formation.

#### Layers of the Earth

The Earth's interior is divided into three main concentric layers:

1.  **Crust:**
    *   **What:** The outermost and thinnest solid layer.
    *   **Thickness:** Varies from 5-30 km under oceans (oceanic crust) to 50-70 km under continents (continental crust). Average thickness is 8-40 km.
    *   **Composition:** Primarily composed of lighter silicates. Rich in silica (Si) and aluminium (Al), hence called **Sial**.
    *   **Density:** Lowest, averaging about 2.7 gm/cm³.
    *   **Nature:** Brittle and prone to fracturing.
    *   **Lithosphere:** Comprises the crust and the rigid uppermost part of the mantle. It is the layer involved in plate tectonics.

2.  **Mantle:**
    *   **What:** The layer beneath the crust.
    *   **Thickness:** Extends to about 2900 km depth.
    *   **Composition:** Rich in silica (Si) and magnesium (Mg), hence called **Sima**.
    *   **Density:** Averages about 5.68 gm/cm³.
    *   **Nature:** Primarily solid, but its upper part behaves plastically (semi-solid) due to high temperature and pressure.
    *   **Asthenosphere:** The upper part of the mantle (about 250 km thick) where magma is generated. It is a weak, ductile layer over which the lithospheric plates move.

3.  **Core:**
    *   **What:** The innermost layer of the Earth.
    *   **Thickness:** Has a radius of about 3500 km.
    *   **Composition:** Primarily composed of nickel (Ni) and iron (Fe), hence called **Nife**.
    *   **Density:** Highest, reaching up to 17.2 gm/cm³.
    *   **Nature:**
        *   **Outer Core:** Liquid state, responsible for generating Earth's magnetic field.
        *   **Inner Core:** Solid state, extremely hot and dense.

#### Discontinuities

These are zones of abrupt change in seismic wave velocity, marking boundaries between different layers or sub-layers.

*   **Conrad Discontinuity:** Separates the upper continental crust from the lower continental crust.
*   **Mohorovičić (Moho) Discontinuity:** Separates the crust from the mantle.
*   **Repetti Discontinuity:** Separates the upper mantle (asthenosphere) from the lower mantle.
*   **Gutenberg Discontinuity:** Separates the mantle from the core.
*   **Lehmann Discontinuity:** Separates the liquid outer core from the solid inner core.

> **Memory Trick (Discontinuities from surface to center):** "**C**an **M**y **R**eally **G**ood **L**earner" (Conrad, Moho, Repetti, Gutenberg, Lehmann).

*   **Earth's Crust (Major Elements):** Oxygen (46%), Silicon (28%), Aluminium (8%), Iron (6%), Magnesium (4%), Calcium (2.4%), Potassium (2.3%), Sodium (2.1%).

## 9. Oceanography (Brief Overview)

Oceanography is the study of the physical, chemical, biological, and geological properties of the oceans.

### 9.1 Salinity

**What:** The total amount of dissolved salts and minerals in 1 kg of seawater, expressed as parts per thousand (‰). Average oceanic salinity is about 35‰.

#### Factors Affecting Salinity

*   **Evaporation:** Higher evaporation leads to higher salinity.
*   **Precipitation:** Higher precipitation (freshwater input) leads to lower salinity.
*   **Influx of River Water:** Large rivers discharge freshwater, reducing salinity at river mouths.
*   **Atmospheric Pressure & Winds:** Anticyclonic conditions (stable air, high temperature) increase evaporation, thus increasing salinity.
*   **Ocean Currents:** Mix waters, affecting spatial distribution.
*   **Freezing/Melting of Ice:** Freezing removes freshwater, increasing salinity of surrounding water. Melting adds freshwater, decreasing salinity.

#### Distribution

*   Generally decreases from tropics towards poles.
*   Highest salinity is found near the tropics (around 20°N-30°N and 20°S-30°S) due to high evaporation and low precipitation.
*   Lowest salinity at poles (due to melting ice) and equator (due to heavy rainfall).
*   **Isohalines:** Lines connecting points of equal salinity on a map.

### 9.2 Ocean Currents

**What:** Continuous, directed movement of ocean water, generated by forces like winds, Coriolis effect, temperature and salinity differences, and coastline configuration.

## 9. Oceanography (Brief Overview)

Oceanography is the study of the physical, chemical, biological, and geological properties of the oceans.

### 9.1 Salinity

**What:** The total amount of dissolved salts and minerals in 1 kg of seawater, expressed as parts per thousand (‰). Average oceanic salinity is about 35‰.

#### Factors Affecting Salinity

*   **Evaporation:** Higher evaporation leads to higher salinity (e.g., in subtropical high-pressure zones).
*   **Precipitation:** Higher precipitation (freshwater input) leads to lower salinity (e.g., at the equator).
*   **Influx of River Water:** Large rivers discharge freshwater, significantly reducing salinity at river mouths (e.g., Bay of Bengal).
*   **Atmospheric Pressure & Winds:** High atmospheric pressure and stable air (anticyclonic conditions) increase evaporation, thus increasing salinity.
*   **Ocean Currents:** Mix waters, affecting spatial distribution of salinity.
*   **Freezing/Melting of Ice:** Freezing of seawater removes freshwater, increasing the salinity of the remaining water. Melting of ice adds freshwater, decreasing salinity (e.g., polar regions).

#### Distribution

*   Salinity generally decreases from the subtropical regions towards both the poles and the equator.
*   **Highest Salinity:** Found in subtropical high-pressure zones (around 20°N-30°N and 20°S-30°S) due to high evaporation and low precipitation. Examples include the Red Sea (highest oceanic salinity, ~40‰) and Persian Gulf.
*   **Lowest Salinity:** Found at the poles (due to melting ice and low evaporation) and at the equator (due to heavy rainfall and high precipitation). Examples include the Baltic Sea (very low salinity, ~10-15‰).
*   **Isohalines:** Imaginary lines connecting points of equal salinity on a map.

### 9.2 Ocean Currents

**What:** Continuous, directed movement of ocean water, generated by various forces.

#### Causes of Ocean Currents

*   **Planetary Winds:** Primary driving force (e.g., Trade Winds drive Equatorial Currents, Westerlies drive North Atlantic Drift).
*   **Coriolis Force:** Deflects currents to the right in the Northern Hemisphere and to the left in the Southern Hemisphere.
*   **Temperature Differences:** Warm water is lighter and flows towards colder regions; cold water is denser and sinks, flowing towards warmer regions.
*   **Salinity Differences:** Higher salinity water is denser and sinks; lower salinity water is lighter and rises.
*   **Configuration of Coastlines:** Shapes and deflects currents.

#### Classification

*   **Warm Ocean Currents:** Flow from lower (equatorial) to higher latitudes, carrying warm water (e.g., Gulf Stream, Kuroshio).
*   **Cold Ocean Currents:** Flow from higher (polar) to lower latitudes, carrying cold water (e.g., Labrador Current, Peruvian Current).

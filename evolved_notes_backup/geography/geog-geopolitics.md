## **1. The Geopolitics of India's Borders**

### **1.1. The Line of Actual Control (LAC)**
The LAC is the disputed boundary between India and China, approximately **3,488 km** long. It is divided into three sectors:
*   **Western Sector (Ladakh):** Includes regions like Aksai Chin, Pangong Tso, and the Galwan Valley. (Site of the major 2020 clash).
*   **Middle Sector (Himachal Pradesh & Uttarakhand):** The least disputed sector.
*   **Eastern Sector (Arunachal Pradesh & Sikkim):** Defined primarily by the **McMahon Line** (drawn in 1914 at the Simla Convention, which China refuses to recognize).

### **1.2. The Line of Control (LoC)**
The LoC is the military control line between the Indian and Pakistani-controlled parts of the former princely state of Jammu and Kashmir. It is a highly militarized and volatile border.

### **1.3. The Siliguri Corridor ("Chicken's Neck")**
*   **Location:** A narrow stretch of land (as narrow as **22 km**) in West Bengal.
*   **Strategic Importance:** It connects India's northeastern states to the rest of the country.
*   **Vulnerability:** Its proximity to the Chumbi Valley (Tibet, China) and Doklam makes it a critical geopolitical vulnerability. China's "String of Pearls" strategy and infrastructure buildup near Doklam directly threaten this corridor.

## **2. The Indo-Pacific Region**

The Indo-Pacific is the new center of global geopolitics, shifting focus from the Atlantic. It encompasses the Indian Ocean and the Pacific Ocean.

### **2.1. India's SAGAR Vision**
*   **SAGAR:** Security And Growth for All in the Region.
*   **Goal:** India's strategic vision for the Indian Ocean Region (IOR), focusing on cooperative security, sustainable development, and capacity building for maritime neighbors.

### **2.2. Key Strategic Alliances & Initiatives**
*   **QUAD (Quadrilateral Security Dialogue):** A strategic forum comprising **India, USA, Japan, and Australia**. Its objective is to ensure a "free, open, and prosperous" Indo-Pacific (a counter to China's assertiveness).
*   **AUKUS:** A trilateral security pact between **Australia, the UK, and the USA**. Its primary initiative is to help Australia acquire nuclear-powered submarines.

### **2.3. China's Strategic Posture**
*   **String of Pearls:** A geopolitical theory suggesting China is building a network of commercial and military facilities along its sea lines of communication, encircling India (e.g., Gwadar in Pakistan, Hambantota in Sri Lanka, Chittagong in Bangladesh, Kyaukpyu in Myanmar).
*   **Malacca Dilemma:** China's vulnerability; a significant portion of its [[energy]] imports passes through the narrow Strait of Malacca. India's presence in the Andaman and Nicobar [[Islands]] is a strategic counter to this.
*   **BRI (Belt and Road Initiative) & CPEC:** China's massive infrastructure project. The **China-Pakistan Economic Corridor (CPEC)** is a flagship project that passes through Pakistan-occupied Kashmir (PoK), which India strongly opposes on sovereignty grounds.

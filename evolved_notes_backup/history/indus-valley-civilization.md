## 1. Introduction and Geography
The [[Indus Valley Civilization]] (IVC), also known as the [[Harappan]] Civilization after its first discovered site, is one of the four earliest Bronze Age urban civilizations of the world (alongside Mesopotamia, Egypt, and China). It flourished between **2500 BCE and 1750 BCE** (mature phase, according to radiocarbon dating).
Geographically, it was the largest among its contemporaries, covering parts of modern-day Pakistan, Afghanistan, and northwestern India (Gujarat, Rajasthan, Haryana, Punjab).

**Geographical Extent:**
*   **Northernmost point:** Manda (Jammu & Kashmir) on the river Chenab.
*   **Southernmost point:** Daimabad (Maharashtra) on the river Pravara.
*   **Easternmost point:** Alamgirpur (Uttar Pradesh) on the river Hindon.
*   **Westernmost point:** Sutkagendor (Makran coast, Pakistan/Iran border) on the river Dasht.

## 2. Town Planning and Architecture
The most striking feature of the IVC was its advanced and standardized urban planning, which was unparalleled in the ancient world.

![Indus Valley Civilization - City Plan & Architecture](/assets/diagrams/history_3_ancient_india_indus_valley_civilization_indus_valley_city_plan.png)

*   **Grid System:** Streets and lanes cut across one another almost at right angles, dividing the city into several rectangular blocks.
*   **City Division:** Most cities were divided into two parts:
    1.  **The Citadel (Acropolis):** Built on a raised mud-brick platform to the west. It housed the ruling elite and contained important public buildings like granaries, assembly halls, and the Great Bath.
    2.  **The Lower Town:** Located to the east, inhabited by commoners, merchants, and artisans.
*   **[[Drainage System]]:** Every house, large or small, had its own courtyard and bathroom. Water flowed from houses to street drains. The street drains were covered with bricks or stone slabs and equipped with manholes for cleaning—a hallmark of [[Harappan]] hygiene.
*   **Building Materials:** The extensive use of **standardized burnt bricks** (in a strict 4:2:1 ratio of length:breadth:thickness) is a unique feature. Mesopotamia largely used sun-dried bricks.

## 3. Economy: Agriculture, Crafts, and Trade
### Agriculture
*   **[[Crops]]:** The Harappans grew wheat, barley, pulses, peas, and mustard. They were the **earliest people in the world to produce cotton** (called *Sindon* by the Greeks).
*   **Tools:** They used wooden ploughshares (evidence of a ploughed field found at Kalibangan) and stone sickles for harvesting. Evidence of canals is rare (except at Shortughai in Afghanistan), but reservoirs were used (Dholavira).

### Crafts and Metallurgy
*   They were a Bronze Age civilization. Bronze was made by mixing copper (brought from Khetri mines in Rajasthan) with tin (from Afghanistan).
*   **Bead-making:** A highly specialized craft. Steatite, agate, and carnelian were used. Bead-making factories have been found at Chanhudaro and Lothal.
*   **Seals:** Over 2000 seals have been discovered, mostly made of **steatite**. They are usually square with a carved animal and a short inscription. They were primarily used for commercial purposes (to stamp clay tags on goods).

### Trade
*   A flourishing internal and external trade existed based on the barter system (no metallic money was used).
*   **Mesopotamian Trade:** Mesopotamian texts refer to trade with **Meluhha** (the ancient name for the [[Indus]] region). Trade outposts like Lothal (which had an artificial brick dockyard) facilitated maritime trade.
*   **Weights and Measures:** A highly standardized system of cubical chert weights was used. The lower denominations were binary (1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32) and the higher ones were decimal.

## 4. Society and Religion
*   **Script:** The [[Harappan]] script is pictographic and remains **undeciphered**. It was generally written from right to left (boustrophedon style when multi-lined).
*   **Religion:** There are no temples found in the IVC. Religion was likely animistic and deeply connected to nature.
    *   **Mother Goddess:** Terracotta figurines of a female deity with a plant growing out of her womb suggest the worship of an earth/fertility goddess.
    *   **Pashupati Shiva:** A famous steatite seal from Mohenjo-daro depicts a three-faced male deity sitting in a yogic posture, surrounded by an elephant, tiger, rhino, and a buffalo, with two deer at his feet. Sir John Marshall identified him as proto-Shiva.
    *   **Animism:** They worshipped trees (specifically the Peepal tree) and animals (the humpless bull or 'unicorn' is the most frequently depicted animal on seals).

## 6. Decline
The IVC did not collapse suddenly but faded gradually around 1750 BCE. There is no single consensus on the cause. Various theories include:
*   Aryan Invasion (Wheeler) - largely discarded now.
*   Ecological imbalance and deforestation (Fairservis).
*   Tectonic shifts causing [[earthquakes]] and changes in the course of the [[Indus]] river (Dales and Raikes).
*   Hydrological changes, specifically the drying up of the Ghaggar-Hakra (Saraswati) river system.

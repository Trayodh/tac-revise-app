## 1. Establishment and Expansion
The Mauryan Empire marks a turning point in Indian history as the first pan-Indian empire. It was founded by [[Chandragupta]] Maurya, who overthrew the unpopular Nanda dynasty of Magadha with the strategic brilliance of his mentor, **Chanakya** (Kautilya/Vishnugupta).

![The Mauryan Empire Overview & Administration](/assets/diagrams/history_3_ancient_india_mauryan_period_mauryan_empire_infographic.png)

### Key Rulers
1.  **[[Chandragupta]] Maurya (322 – 298 BCE):**
    *   Defeated Seleucus Nicator (Alexander's general) in 305 BCE, securing the northwestern frontiers up to the Hindu Kush (including Balochistan and Afghanistan).
    *   Seleucus sent **Megasthenes** as an ambassador to the Mauryan court at Pataliputra. Megasthenes wrote the *Indica*, a detailed account of Mauryan administration.
    *   In his later life, [[Chandragupta]] embraced [[Jainism]] under the guidance of the monk Bhadrabahu, migrated to Shravanabelagola (Karnataka), and starved himself to death (a practice called *Sallekhana*).
2.  **Bindusara (298 – 273 BCE):**
    *   Son of [[Chandragupta]], known to the Greeks as *Amitrochates* (Slayer of Foes).
    *   He patronized the **Ajivika sect** (a deterministic, ascetic philosophy).
3.  **[[Ashoka]] the Great (268 – 232 BCE):**
    *   Considered one of the greatest emperors in history. He fought only one major war—the **Kalinga War (261 BCE)**. The massive bloodshed filled him with remorse, leading to his conversion to [[Buddhism]] under the monk Upagupta.
    *   He replaced *Bherighosha* (the [[sound]] of the war drum) with *Dhammaghosha* (the [[sound]] of peace/Dharma).

## 2. Ashoka's Dhamma and Edicts
[[Ashoka]]'s *Dhamma* was not exactly [[Buddhism]]; it was a broad, [[secular]] moral code intended to maintain social harmony in a diverse empire. It emphasized obedience to parents, respect for teachers and Brahmanas, and compassion towards slaves and animals.

He propagated his Dhamma through royal edicts carved on [[rocks]] and highly polished sandstone pillars placed across the empire.

![Ashokan Pillar Anatomy and Inscriptions](/assets/diagrams/history_3_ancient_india_mauryan_period_ashokan_pillar_edicts.png)

### Key Ashokan Edicts (Highly Tested)
*   **Major Rock Edict XIII:** Mentions the Kalinga War and [[Ashoka]]'s subsequent change of [[heart]]. Also mentions five contemporary Greek kings (including Antiochus and Ptolemy).
*   **Bhabru Edict:** Confirms [[Ashoka]]'s conversion to [[Buddhism]] (he expresses faith in the Buddha, Dhamma, and Sangha).
*   **Lumbini Pillar Edict:** Commemorates his visit to the birthplace of Buddha and exempts the village from heavy taxation (reducing the land revenue to 1/8th).
*   **Language and Script:** Most edicts are in the **Prakrit language** written in the **Brahmi script** (deciphered by James Prinsep in 1837). Edicts in the northwest used Kharoshthi, Aramaic, and Greek.

## 3. Mauryan Administration
The Mauryan state, heavily influenced by the principles laid out in Chanakya's *Arthashastra*, was highly centralized and bureaucratic. It was a welfare state with a massive espionage system.

### Central Administration
*   **Tirthas:** The highest category of officials (18 in number). The most important were the *Mantri* (Chief Minister), *Purohita* (Chief Priest), *Senapati* (Commander), and *Yuvaraja* (Crown Prince).
*   **Adhyakshas:** Superintendents controlling various economic departments (e.g., *Sitadhyaksha* for crown lands, *Panyadhyaksha* for commerce).

### Provincial and Local Administration
*   The empire was divided into provinces ruled by royal princes (*Kumara* or *Aryaputra*).
*   **City Administration:** According to Megasthenes, Pataliputra was administered by a city council of 30 members divided into 6 committees (handling [[industries]], foreigners, births/deaths, trade, manufactured goods, and tax collection).
*   **[[Judiciary]]:** Two types of courts existed: *Dharmasthiya* (civil courts) and *Kantakashodhana* (criminal courts).

## 4. Economy and Society
*   **[[Agriculture]]:** It was the backbone of the economy. The state actively brought new lands under cultivation and built [[irrigation]] facilities (e.g., the Sudarshana Lake in Gujarat, built by Pushyagupta, [[Chandragupta]]'s [[governor]]).
*   **Taxation:** Land revenue (*Bhaga*) was the main source of income, usually 1/6th to 1/4th of the produce.
*   **Society:** Megasthenes noted the absence of slavery in India (though the *Arthashastra* mentions it). He famously divided Indian society into **7 classes** based on occupation (philosophers, farmers, herdsmen, artisans, soldiers, overseers, and councilors), mistakenly confusing occupation with the rigid Varna system.

## 5. Decline of the Empire
Following [[Ashoka]]'s death in 232 BCE, the empire rapidly disintegrated. 
*   **Causes:** Weak successors, the vastness of the empire making central control difficult, and economic stress from maintaining a huge army and bureaucracy.
*   **The End:** In 185 BCE, the last Mauryan king, **Brihadratha**, was assassinated by his own Brahmin commander-in-chief, **Pushyamitra Shunga**, who founded the Shunga dynasty, marking the beginning of the Post-[[Mauryan period]].

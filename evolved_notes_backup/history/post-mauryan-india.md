# The Post-Mauryan Period (200 BCE - 300 CE)

## 1. Political Fragmentation and Invasions
The decline of the Mauryan Empire in 185 BCE left a political vacuum in the Indian subcontinent. Without a strong central [[power]], the subcontinent fragmented into several regional kingdoms. Simultaneously, a series of foreign invasions from Central Asia began through the northwestern passes. 

This period is broadly divided into two major political streams:
1.  **Native Successors in the East, Center, and South:** Shungas, Kanvas, and Satavahanas.
2.  **Foreign Invaders in the Northwest:** Indo-Greeks, Shakas, Parthians, and Kushans.

## 2. Native Dynasties

### The Shunga Dynasty (185 BCE – 73 BCE)
*   **Pushyamitra Shunga:** A Brahmana commander-in-chief who assassinated the last Mauryan king, Brihadratha. He was a staunch supporter of orthodox Brahmanism and performed two Ashvamedha sacrifices (officiated by the grammarian **Patanjali**). 
*   Despite being anti-Buddhist, the famous Buddhist stupas at Sanchi and Bharhut were heavily renovated and expanded with stone railings during the Shunga period.
*   **Agnimitra:** Pushyamitra's son and the hero of Kalidasa's famous play, *Malavikagnimitram*.

### The Satavahana Dynasty (c. 1st Century BCE – 3rd Century CE)
*   **Region:** The Deccan plateau (capital at Pratishthana/Paithan in Maharashtra). They served as a cultural bridge between North and South India.
*   **Key Rulers:** 
    *   **Simuka:** The founder.
    *   **Gautamiputra Satakarni:** The greatest ruler, who defeated the Shakas (Nahapana) and claimed to be the unique Brahmana (*Eka Brahmana*).
*   **Distinctive Features:** 
    *   They were the first Indian rulers to issue **coins of lead**.
    *   They were the first to start the practice of making **tax-free land grants to Brahmanas and Buddhist monks**, laying the foundation for feudalism in India.
    *   They had a matronymic naming system (naming kings after their mothers, e.g., Gautamiputra), although the succession was patriarchal.
    *   They patronized Prakrit [[literature]] (King Hala wrote the *Gathasaptashati*).

## 3. Foreign Invasions

### The Indo-Greeks (Bactrian Greeks)
*   They were the first to cross the Hindu Kush into India after the Mauryas.
*   **Menander (Milinda):** The most famous Indo-Greek ruler. His capital was at Sakala (Sialkot). He was converted to [[Buddhism]] by the monk Nagasena. Their philosophical dialogues are recorded in the famous Pali text, **Milinda Panho** (Questions of Milinda).
*   **Legacy:** They were the **first to issue coins attributing them to specific kings**, and the **first to issue gold coins in India**. They introduced Hellenistic (Greek) art features, which profoundly influenced the Gandhara School of Art.

### The Shakas (Scythians)
*   They replaced the Indo-Greeks in the northwest and western India. They were divided into five branches.
*   **Rudradaman I (130 – 150 CE):** The most famous Shaka ruler of Ujjain. He is celebrated for repairing the **Sudarshana Lake** in Gujarat (originally built by the Mauryas). He issued the **Junagadh Rock Inscription**, which is the **first long inscription written in pure Sanskrit**.
*   **Vikrama Era (58 BCE):** Traditionally believed to have been started by a king of Ujjain, Vikramaditya, to commemorate his victory over the Shakas.

### The Kushan Empire
*   Originating from the nomadic Yueh-chi tribe in Central Asia, they displaced the Shakas and Parthians.
*   **Kanishka (c. 78 CE – 144 CE):** The greatest Kushan emperor. His empire extended from Central Asia to Pataliputra.
    *   **Shaka Era (78 CE):** The official calendar of the Government of India, started by Kanishka upon his accession.
    *   **[[Buddhism]]:** He was a great patron of Mahayana [[Buddhism]] and convened the **Fourth Buddhist Council in Kashmir**.
    *   **Silk Route:** The Kushans controlled the famous Silk Route, generating immense wealth, which is why they issued the **largest number of pure gold coins** in ancient India.

## 4. Art and Architecture
The Post-[[Mauryan period]] was a golden age for sculptural art, leading to the emergence of three distinct schools:

| Feature | Gandhara School | Mathura School | Amaravati School |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Patrons** | Kushans | Kushans | Satavahanas / Ikshvakus |
| **Influence** | Strong Greek/Hellenistic influence (Apollo-like Buddha). | Purely indigenous Indian style. | Purely indigenous. |
| **Material** | Bluish-grey sandstone. | Spotted red sandstone. | White marble. |
| **Theme** | Primarily Buddhist. | Buddhist, Jain, and Hindu ([[Secular]] as well). | Primarily Buddhist (Jataka tales depicted in continuous narrative). |

## 5. Trade and Commerce
This period witnessed unprecedented economic prosperity due to thriving international trade.
*   **Indo-Roman Trade:** Flourished immensely. India exported spices (pepper was known as *Yavanapriya* or the 'passion of the Greeks'), pearls, ivory, and fine textiles. In return, massive amounts of Roman gold and silver coins poured into India.
*   **Ports:** *Bharuch (Barygaza)* and *Kalyan* on the west coast; *Arikamedu* and *Kaveripattinam* on the east coast.

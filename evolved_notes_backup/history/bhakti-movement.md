# The Bhakti Movement

## 1. Introduction
[[The Bhakti Movement]] was a significant religious and social reform movement in medieval India that emphasized a direct, personal, and emotional devotion (Bhakti) to a personal God as the ultimate path to salvation, bypassing complex rituals, sacrifices, and priestly intermediaries. 

*   **Origins:** The movement originated in South India (Tamil Nadu) between the 7th and 10th centuries and later spread to North India.
*   **Key Features:**
    *   Rejection of the rigid caste system, untouchability, and ritualism.
    *   Preaching in local vernacular languages (instead of Sanskrit), making religion accessible to the common masses.
    *   Concept of [[equality]] before God.
    *   Influence of Sufism (ideas of mystical love and brotherhood).

## 2. South Indian Origins
The movement began with the poetry and songs of two groups of Tamil saints who traveled from temple to temple:
*   **Alvars:** Twelve saints who were devoted to **Vishnu** (or his avatars).
*   **Nayanars:** Sixty-three saints who were devoted to **Shiva**.
*   **Philosophy:** These saints rejected the austerities preached by [[Jainism]] and [[Buddhism]] and championed a path of intense love and devotion. The hymns of the Alvars were compiled in the *Nalayira Divya Prabandham*.

### Key Philosophers
*   **Shankaracharya (8th Century):** Propounded the philosophy of **Advaita Vedanta** (Non-dualism) – the absolute identity of the individual soul (Atman) and the supreme reality (Brahman). He established four Mathas (monasteries) in the four corners of India.
*   **Ramanujacharya (11th Century):** Reacted against Shankara's absolute non-dualism. He propounded **Vishishtadvaita** (Qualified Non-dualism) – the soul is united with God but retains its distinct identity. He heavily influenced the North Indian [[Bhakti movement]].

## 3. The Two Streams of Bhakti
As the movement spread to North India, it bifurcated into two main ideological streams based on how they conceptualized God:

### A. Nirguna Bhakti (Formless God)
Believed that God is universal, formless, without attributes (*Nirguna*), and beyond human comprehension. They vehemently opposed idol worship and the caste system.

*   **Kabir (15th Century):** A weaver who was a disciple of Ramananda. He strongly rejected both Hindu and Islamic orthodoxies, rituals, and idol worship. He preached a religion of universal brotherhood. His verses (Dohas) are preserved in the *Bijak* and the *Guru Granth Sahib*.
*   **Guru Nanak (1469 – 1539):** The founder of Sikhism. He preached the worship of one formless God (*Akal Purakh*) and established the institution of Langar to promote [[equality]].

### B. Saguna Bhakti (God with Form)
Believed that God has distinct forms, attributes, and incarnations (usually Rama or [[Krishna]]). They worshiped idols and composed intensely emotional poetry directed at their chosen deity.

*   **Ramananda (14th Century):** The bridge between South and North Indian Bhakti. He was the first to preach in Hindi. His disciples included people from all castes (e.g., Kabir the weaver, Ravidas the cobbler).
*   **Tulsidas (16th Century):** A great devotee of Lord Rama. He wrote the **Ramcharitmanas** in Awadhi, making the Ramayana accessible to the masses.
*   **Surdas (16th Century):** A blind poet devoted to Lord [[Krishna]]. He composed the *Sursagar* in Braj Bhasha, vividly describing the childhood of [[Krishna]].
*   **Mirabai (16th Century):** A Rajput princess who became a passionate devotee of Lord [[Krishna]], whom she considered her divine lover. She composed numerous Bhajans expressing intense spiritual longing.
*   **Chaitanya Mahaprabhu (15th Century):** Popularized the worship of [[Krishna]] in Bengal. He introduced the practice of ecstatic group chanting and dancing (*Sankirtan*).

## 4. Regional Bhakti Saints
*   **Maharashtra:**
    *   **Jnaneshwar (Dnyaneshwar):** Wrote the *Jnaneshwari*, a commentary on the Bhagavad Gita in Marathi.
    *   **Namdev:** A tailor by profession; his verses are included in the Guru Granth Sahib.
    *   **Tukaram:** Composed devotional poetry (Abhangs) and was a contemporary of [[Shivaji]].
*   **Assam:**
    *   **Srimanta Sankardeva:** A polymath who preached Vaishnavism in Assam and introduced the *Sattriya* classical dance and the institution of *Sattras* (monasteries).

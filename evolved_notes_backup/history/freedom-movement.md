## 1. Formation of the Indian National Congress (1885)
The [[Indian National Congress]] (INC) was founded in 1885 by **A.O. Hume** (a retired British civil servant). 
*   **First Session:** Held in Bombay (1885), presided over by **Womesh Chandra Bonnerjee**.
*   **Safety Valve Theory:** A theory suggesting that Hume formed the INC to provide a safe, peaceful outlet for Indian grievances to prevent another violent uprising like 1857.

![Important Sessions of the Indian National Congress](/assets/diagrams/history_5_modern_india_highest_priority_freedom_movement_inc_sessions_map.png)

## 2. The Moderate Phase (1885 – 1905)
*   **Leaders:** Dadabhai Naoroji, Surendranath Banerjee, Pherozeshah Mehta, Gopal [[Krishna]] Gokhale.
*   **Methods:** Constitutional agitation, petitions, prayers, and public meetings. They believed in the innate sense of British [[justice]].
*   **Achievements:** Articulated the economic critique of colonialism (Drain Theory) and successfully pushed for the expansion of legislative councils (Indian Councils Act 1892).

## 3. The Extremist Phase and Partition of Bengal (1905 – 1918)
*   **Leaders:** The Lal-Bal-Pal trio (**Lala Lajpat Rai, Bal Gangadhar Tilak, Bipin Chandra Pal**) and Aurobindo Ghosh.
*   **Ideology:** Believed in self-reliance (Atmashakti), boycott of foreign goods, and direct action. Tilak declared: *"Swaraj is my birthright, and I shall have it."*
*   **[[Partition of Bengal]] (1905):** Lord Curzon partitioned Bengal ostensibly for administrative efficiency, but the real motive was to divide the politically active Bengalis along religious lines (East Bengal for Muslims, West Bengal for Hindus).
*   **[[Swadeshi Movement]] (1905):** Launched in response to the partition. It involved a massive boycott of British goods, the burning of foreign cloth, and the promotion of indigenous [[industries]] and national education.
*   **Surat Split (1907):** The ideological clash between Moderates and Extremists culminated in a physical clash and a split in the INC at the Surat session. The British capitalized on this by suppressing the Extremists (Tilak was exiled to Mandalay).
*   **Home Rule Movement (1916):** Launched separately by **Bal Gangadhar Tilak** (in Maharashtra and Karnataka) and **Annie Besant** (rest of India) to demand self-government within the British Empire, on the lines of Irish Home Rule.
*   **Lucknow Pact (1916):** The INC and the Muslim League held a joint session and signed a pact, agreeing to demand self-government and accepting separate electorates for Muslims. The Moderates and Extremists also reunited.

## 4. The Gandhian Era (1915 – 1947)
Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi returned to India from South Africa in 1915, bringing with him his powerful weapon of **Satyagraha** (Truth-[[force]]/Non-violent [[resistance]]).

![The Gandhian Era Timeline](/assets/diagrams/history_5_modern_india_highest_priority_freedom_movement_gandhian_era_timeline.png)

### Early Local Satyagrahas
1.  **Champaran Satyagraha (1917):** First Civil Disobedience in India. Fought against the exploitative Tinkathia system imposed on indigo planters in Bihar.
2.  **Ahmedabad Mill Strike (1918):** First Hunger Strike. Intervened in a dispute between mill owners and workers over plague bonuses.
3.  **Kheda Satyagraha (1918):** First Non-Cooperation. Organized a no-tax campaign for peasants whose [[crops]] had failed due to drought.

### Rowlatt Act and Jallianwala Bagh (1919)
*   **[[Rowlatt Act]]:** Allowed the government to arrest and imprison anyone without trial for two years ("No Dalil, No Vakil, No Appeal").
*   **[[Jallianwala Bagh]] Massacre (April 13, 1919):** A peaceful crowd gathered in Amritsar to protest the arrest of their leaders (Dr. Saifuddin Kitchlew and Dr. Satyapal). General Dyer ordered his troops to fire on the unarmed crowd, killing hundreds. Rabindranath Tagore renounced his knighthood in protest.

### Non-Cooperation and Khilafat Movement (1920 – 1922)
*   **[[Khilafat Movement]]:** Launched by the Ali brothers (Shaukat Ali and Muhammad Ali) to protest the harsh treatment of the Ottoman Caliph (the spiritual head of Muslims) by the British after WWI. Gandhi supported it to unite Hindus and Muslims.
*   **[[Non-Cooperation Movement]] (NCM):** Launched by Gandhi demanding Swaraj. Involved boycotting government schools, colleges, courts, and foreign goods.
*   **Chauri Chaura Incident (1922):** A violent mob burnt down a police station in UP, killing 22 policemen. Deeply disturbed by the violence, Gandhi abruptly called off the NCM.

### Civil Disobedience Movement (1930 – 1934)
*   **Lahore Session (1929):** Presided over by Jawaharlal Nehru, the INC declared **Purna Swaraj** (Complete Independence) as its ultimate goal. January 26, 1930, was celebrated as the first Independence Day.
*   **Dandi March (March 1930):** Gandhi began the [[Civil Disobedience Movement]] (CDM) by marching 240 miles from Sabarmati Ashram to Dandi to break the salt law, protesting the oppressive salt tax. This sparked massive, nationwide civil disobedience.
*   **Gandhi-Irwin Pact (1931):** The movement was temporarily suspended. The government agreed to release political prisoners, and Gandhi agreed to attend the Second Round Table Conference in London (which ultimately failed to provide meaningful concessions).

### Quit India Movement (1942)
*   **Trigger:** The failure of the [[Cripps Mission]] (1942) and the advancing threat of a Japanese invasion of India during WWII.
*   **The Call:** Gandhi gave the famous mantra **"Do or Die"** (Karo ya Maro) at the Gowalia Tank Maidan in Bombay on August 8, 1942.
*   **Nature:** The British immediately arrested all top Congress leaders. The movement became a massive, leaderless, and often violent uprising. Parallel governments were established in places like Ballia, Tamluk, and Satara.

## 5. Revolutionary Nationalism
A parallel stream of the freedom struggle that believed in armed rebellion and assassinations of unpopular British officials.
*   **Phase I (Post-Swadeshi):** Anushilan Samiti and Jugantar in Bengal. The Ghadar Party (founded by Lala Hardayal in San Francisco) attempted an armed mutiny in India during WWI.
*   **Phase II (Post-Non-Cooperation):** 
    *   **HRA/HSRA:** Founded by Ram Prasad Bismil, Chandrashekhar Azad, and Bhagat Singh. They executed the Kakori train robbery (1925).
    *   **Bhagat Singh & Batukeshwar Dutt:** Threw harmless bombs in the Central Legislative Assembly (1929) to protest anti-labor bills. Bhagat Singh, Rajguru, and Sukhdev were hanged in 1931.
    *   **Surya Sen:** Led the daring Chittagong Armoury Raid (1930).

## 6. The Indian National Army (INA)
*   **Subhas Chandra Bose:** A radical leader who twice became INC [[President]] but resigned due to differences with Gandhi. He escaped house arrest, traveled to Germany, and then to Japan to seek armed help against the British.
*   **The INA (Azad Hind Fauj):** Originally formed by Mohan Singh. Bose revitalized it in Singapore in 1943. He gave the slogans *"Delhi Chalo"* and *"Give me [[blood]], and I shall give you freedom!"*
*   The INA fought alongside the Japanese army in Imphal and Kohima. Though defeated militarily, the INA trials at the Red Fort (1945-46) generated unprecedented patriotic fervor across India, sparking naval mutinies that convinced the British their hold over the Indian armed forces was broken.

## 1. Causes for the Rise of Sramana Movements
The 6th century BCE witnessed profound intellectual and religious ferment in the Gangetic valley. Numerous heterodox sects arose, the most prominent being [[Buddhism]] and [[Jainism]]. These were *Sramana* (ascetic) movements that directly challenged the orthodox Vedic religion.

**Key Causes:**
1.  **Reaction against Ritualism:** The later Vedic religion had become excessively ritualistic, expensive (requiring huge animal sacrifices), and dominated by the Brahmana priestly class.
2.  **Rigid Varna System:** The Kshatriyas (rulers/warriors) deeply resented the social supremacy claimed by the Brahmanas. Both Mahavira and Buddha were Kshatriya princes.
3.  **Rise of the Vaishyas:** The agricultural surplus and growth of trade led to the economic rise of the Vaishyas. However, their social status remained low. They patronized these new religions which preached social [[equality]] and non-violence (which protected their cattle wealth).
4.  **Language Barrier:** Vedic [[literature]] was in complex Sanskrit, which was not understood by the masses. [[Buddhism]] and [[Jainism]] used common languages (Pali and Prakrit) to preach.

## 2. Jainism
[[Jainism]] is considered much older than [[Buddhism]]. It believes in a succession of 24 **Tirthankaras** (ford-makers/teachers).
*   **1st Tirthankara:** Rishabhanatha (symbol: Bull) - mentioned in the Rigveda.
*   **23rd Tirthankara:** Parshvanatha (symbol: Serpent) - prince of Benaras.
*   **24th Tirthankara:** Vardhamana Mahavira (symbol: Lion).

### Vardhamana Mahavira (540 BCE – 468 BCE)
*   **Birth:** Kundagrama (near Vaishali, Bihar). He belonged to the Jnatrika Kshatriya clan.
*   **Kaivalya (Enlightenment):** Attained at the age of 42 under a Sal tree on the banks of the Rijupalika river near Jrimbhikagrama.
*   **Nirvana (Death):** Pavapuri (near Rajgir).

### Core Doctrines of Jainism
1.  **Ahimsa (Non-violence):** The supreme principle. Strict avoidance of injury to any living being (including microscopic life).
2.  **Anekantavada (Theory of Plurality):** The truth or reality is complex and has multiple aspects; no single perspective contains the absolute truth.
3.  **Syadvada:** The theory of conditioned predication (all judgments are conditional).
4.  **Triratna (Three Jewels):** Right Faith, Right Knowledge, Right Conduct.
5.  **Pancha Mahavratas (Five Great Vows):** Ahimsa (non-violence), Satya (truth), Asteya (non-stealing), Aparigraha (non-attachment), and Brahmacharya (chastity - added by Mahavira).

*Note: [[Jainism]] recognized the existence of gods but placed them lower than the Jina (conqueror). It did not condemn the Varna system entirely, believing a person's Varna is determined by karma from their past life.*

## 3. Buddhism
[[Buddhism]] was founded by Siddhartha Gautama, aiming to find the root cause of human suffering.

![Buddhism: The Path of Enlightenment](/assets/diagrams/history_3_ancient_india_buddhism_jainism_buddhism_teachings_origins.png)

### Gautama Buddha (563 BCE – 483 BCE)
*   **Birth:** Lumbini (Nepal). He belonged to the Shakya Kshatriya clan of Kapilavastu (hence called *Shakyamuni*).
*   **The Four Sights:** An old man, a sick man, a corpse, and a serene ascetic. These sights prompted his renunciation (Mahabhinishkramana).
*   **Enlightenment (Nirvana):** Attained at the age of 35 under a Peepal (Bodhi) tree on the banks of the Niranjana river in Bodh Gaya.
*   **First Sermon (Dharmachakra Pravartana):** Delivered to five disciples at the Deer Park in Sarnath.
*   **Mahaparinirvana (Death):** Kushinagar (capital of the Mallas).

### Core Doctrines of Buddhism
1.  **The Four Noble Truths (Arya Satyas):**
    *   The world is full of suffering (*Dukkha*).
    *   The cause of suffering is desire (*Trishna*).
    *   Suffering can be stopped if desire is conquered.
    *   The path to end desire is the Eightfold Path.
2.  **The Noble Eightfold Path (Ashtangika Marg):** Also known as the Middle Path (*Madhyama Pratipada*), avoiding the extremes of severe asceticism and excessive indulgence. It comprises Right View, Right Intention, Right Speech, Right Action, Right Livelihood, Right Effort, Right Mindfulness, and Right Concentration.
3.  **Anatta & Anicca:** [[Buddhism]] explicitly denies the existence of an eternal soul (Anatta) and believes everything is impermanent (Anicca).

## 4. Buddhist Councils (Sangitis)
The teachings of Buddha were compiled during four major councils held after his death.

| Council | Year/Location | Patron King | Presided By | Key Outcome |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **First** | 483 BCE / Rajagriha | Ajatashatru (Haryanka) | Mahakashyapa | Compilation of *Sutta Pitaka* (teachings) and *Vinaya Pitaka* (rules for monks). |
| **Second**| 383 BCE / Vaishali | Kalasoka (Shishunaga) | Sabakami | First split in the Sangha (Sthaviras vs. Mahasanghikas). |
| **Third** | 250 BCE / Pataliputra| [[Ashoka]] (Maurya) | Moggaliputta Tissa | Compilation of *Abhidhamma Pitaka* (philosophy). |
| **Fourth**| 72 CE / Kashmir | Kanishka (Kushan) | Vasumitra | **Major Split:** [[Buddhism]] formally divided into **Mahayana** (idol worship) and **Hinayana** (original teachings). |

## 5. Comparison: Buddhism vs. Jainism
*   **Similarities:** Both were founded by Kshatriyas, rejected the authority of the [[Vedas]], opposed animal sacrifices, believed in Karma and rebirth, and used common languages (Pali/Prakrit).
*   **Differences:** 
    *   [[Jainism]] practiced extreme asceticism and penance; [[Buddhism]] preached the "Middle Path."
    *   [[Jainism]] believes in a soul (jiva) in all objects; [[Buddhism]] denies an eternal soul (Anatta).
    *   [[Buddhism]] spread widely across Asia, while [[Jainism]] remained largely confined to India.

## 1. Introduction and The Tripartite Struggle
The period between the decline of [[Harsha]]'s empire (7th century) and the establishment of the [[Delhi Sultanate]] (13th century) is termed the Early Medieval Period. This era was characterized by political fragmentation, the rise of regional kingdoms, and a decentralized feudal structure. 

The most defining political event of this period in North India was the **Tripartite Struggle (or Kannauj Triangle)**. For nearly two centuries, three great powers constantly fought for the control of **Kannauj** (in modern Uttar Pradesh), which had replaced Pataliputra as the symbol of imperial [[power]] in North India due to its strategic location on the fertile Gangetic plain and trade routes.

The three participants were:
1.  **The Palas** (Eastern India / Bengal)
2.  **The Pratiharas / Gurjara-Pratiharas** (Western India / Rajputana & Malwa)
3.  **The [[Rashtrakutas]]** (Deccan / Southern India)

## 2. The Three Great Powers

### The Pala Empire
*   **Founder:** **Gopala** (elected by the people in 750 CE to end a period of anarchy).
*   **Key Rulers:**
    *   **Dharmapala:** He revived the Nalanda University and founded the famous **Vikramashila University** in Bihar.
    *   **Devapala:** Expanded the empire to Assam and Odisha. He had close diplomatic ties with the Shailendra dynasty of Southeast Asia.
*   **Religion:** The Palas were staunch patrons of **Mahayana and Tantric [[Buddhism]]**. During their rule, [[Buddhism]] spread extensively to Tibet. They were the last major royal patrons of [[Buddhism]] in India.
*   **Art:** Patronized the Eastern School of Bronze sculpture (influenced by Nalanda artists Dhiman and Bitpalo).

### The Gurjara-Pratiharas
*   **Origin:** Believed to be of Rajput origin (Agnikula lineage). They ruled over Gujarat and Rajasthan.
*   **Key Rulers:**
    *   **Nagabhata I:** Successfully defended western India against Arab incursions from Sindh.
    *   **Mihira Bhoja:** The greatest ruler of the dynasty. A devotee of Vishnu, he adopted the title *Adivaraha*.
*   **Significance:** They acted as a strong bulwark against Islamic invasions into India for several centuries.

### The Rashtrakutas
*   **Founder:** **Dantidurga** (who overthrew the Chalukyas of Badami). Their capital was Manyakheta (Malkhed).
*   **Key Rulers:**
    *   **[[Krishna]] I:** Built the magnificent rock-cut monolithic **Kailasanatha Temple at Ellora**.
    *   **Amoghavarsha:** A great patron of [[Jainism]] and [[literature]]. He wrote *Kavirajamarga*, the earliest Kannada [[work]] on poetics.
*   **Significance:** They were the most powerful of the three empires. They constantly raided North India (participating in the Tripartite Struggle) while simultaneously fighting the [[Cholas]] in the South. Arab travelers (like Al-Masudi) considered the Rashtrakuta king (Balhara) one of the four greatest monarchs of the world.

## 3. The Rise of the Rajputs
Following the decline of the Pratiharas in the 10th century, North India disintegrated into several independent Rajput states. The [[Rajputs]] were fierce warriors known for their chivalry, but their constant internecine warfare left India vulnerable to foreign invasions.

### Important Rajput Dynasties
*   **The Chauhans (Chahamanas) of Delhi and Ajmer:**
    *   The most famous ruler was **Prithviraj Chauhan**.
    *   Fought the **Battles of Tarain** against Muhammad Ghori (won the first in 1191, lost the second in 1192).
*   **The Chandellas of Bundelkhand:**
    *   Famous for building the world-renowned **Khajuraho temples** (a UNESCO World Heritage site known for intricate sculptures and Panchayatana style).
*   **The Paramaras of Malwa:**
    *   King **Bhoja** was a legendary polymath, warrior, and patron of arts who founded the city of Bhopal.
*   **The Solankis (Chalukyas of Gujarat):**
    *   Patronized [[Jainism]] and built the spectacular **Modhera Sun Temple** and the **Dilwara Jain Temples** at Mount Abu.

## 4. Foreign Invasions: Arab and Turkic Incursions

### The Arab Conquest of Sindh (712 CE)
*   Led by **Muhammad bin Qasim**, the Arabs conquered Sindh (defeating King Dahir). 
*   This was the first successful Islamic invasion of India, though it remained localized to Sindh and did not expand further into the mainland due to [[resistance]] from the Pratiharas.

### Mahmud of Ghazni (Early 11th Century)
*   A Turkic conqueror from Afghanistan, Mahmud launched **17 devastating raids** into North India between 1000 and 1027 CE.
*   **Motive:** His primary motive was to plunder the immense wealth of Indian temples to finance his Central Asian campaigns, rather than to establish a permanent empire in India.
*   **Famous Raids:** Plundered Nagarkot, Thanesar, Mathura, Kannauj, and most notoriously, the **Somnath Temple** in Gujarat (1025 CE).
*   **Scholars:** He patronized scholars like **Al-Biruni** (who wrote the seminal *Kitab-ul-Hind*, a detailed account of Indian society and sciences) and **Firdausi** (author of the *Shahnameh*).

### Muhammad Ghori (Late 12th Century)
*   Unlike Mahmud of Ghazni, Ghori aimed to establish a **permanent Islamic empire in India**.
*   **First Battle of Tarain (1191):** Ghori was defeated by Prithviraj Chauhan.
*   **Second Battle of Tarain (1192):** Ghori returned with a better-prepared army and decisively defeated Prithviraj Chauhan. **This battle laid the foundation of Muslim rule in India.**
*   He left his trusted slave-general, **Qutb-ud-din Aibak**, in charge of his Indian conquests, which eventually led to the establishment of the [[Delhi Sultanate]] in 1206.

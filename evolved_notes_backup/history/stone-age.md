# Stone Age

## 1. Introduction to Prehistoric India
The [[Stone Age]] marks the earliest period of human [[evolution]], a time when hominids and early humans relied primarily on stone tools for survival. Because this period predates the invention of writing, it falls under the category of **Prehistory**. Our understanding of the [[Stone Age]] in India is entirely dependent on archaeological evidence—specifically, lithic (stone) tools, cave [[paintings]], and skeletal remains.

The [[Stone Age]] in India is broadly classified into three major phases based on the geological age, the type and technology of stone tools used, and the nature of subsistence:
1.  **Paleolithic Age** (Old [[Stone Age]])
2.  **Mesolithic Age** (Middle [[Stone Age]])
3.  **Neolithic Age** (New [[Stone Age]])

## 2. Paleolithic Age (Old Stone Age: 2,000,000 BCE – 10,000 BCE)
The Paleolithic Age corresponds to the Pleistocene epoch (the Ice Age). During this era, humans were essentially nomadic hunter-gatherers. They had no knowledge of [[agriculture]], fire-making, or pottery.

### Key Characteristics
*   **Tools:** Humans used unpolished, rough stone tools. In India, they primarily used **Quartzite**, a hard rock, which is why Paleolithic men in India are often referred to as "Quartzite Men".
*   **Subsistence:** Hunting wild animals and gathering wild fruits and roots.
*   **Phases:** Based on the [[evolution]] of tool types, the Indian Paleolithic is divided into:
    *   **Lower Paleolithic:** Characterized by hand axes and cleavers. Key sites include the Soan Valley (Punjab, now in Pakistan) and the Belan Valley (UP).
    *   **Middle Paleolithic:** Characterized by flake tools (scrapers, borers). Key sites are found in the [[Narmada]] and Tungabhadra river valleys.
    *   **Upper Paleolithic:** Characterized by blade tools and burins. This phase coincides with the last phase of the Ice Age and the emergence of *Homo sapiens*.

### Major Paleolithic Sites in India
*   **Bhimbetka (Madhya Pradesh):** A UNESCO World Heritage site famous for its prehistoric rock shelters and cave [[paintings]]. These [[paintings]] provide incredible insights into the social life, hunting practices, and fauna of the Paleolithic and Mesolithic periods.
*   **Attirampakkam (Tamil Nadu):** One of the oldest Paleolithic sites in India, famous for early Acheulean stone tools.
*   **Hathnora (Madhya Pradesh):** Located in the [[Narmada]] Valley, it is famous for the discovery of the earliest hominid fossil in India (the [[Narmada]] Man).

## 3. Mesolithic Age (Middle Stone Age: 10,000 BCE – 4,000 BCE)
The Mesolithic Age represents a transitional phase between the Paleolithic and Neolithic ages. It coincides with the onset of the Holocene epoch—the climate became warmer and drier, leading to changes in flora and fauna.

### Key Characteristics
*   **Tools (Microliths):** The hallmark of the Mesolithic period is the use of **Microliths**—miniature stone tools (usually 1 to 5 cm in length) such as points, crescents, and triangles. These were often hafted onto bone or wooden handles to make composite tools like arrows and spears.
*   **Subsistence:** While hunting and gathering continued, there was a shift towards hunting smaller game, fishing, and fowling. Crucially, the Mesolithic period shows the very first preliminary evidence of the **domestication of animals** (dogs, cattle, sheep, goats).

### Major Mesolithic Sites in India
*   **Bagor (Rajasthan):** Located on the river Kothari, Bagor is one of the largest and best-documented Mesolithic sites in India. It provides the earliest evidence of animal domestication.
*   **Adamgarh (Madhya Pradesh):** Also provides early evidence for the domestication of animals.
*   **Langhnaj (Gujarat):** Important for the discovery of microliths alongside animal bones and human burials.

## 4. Neolithic Age (New Stone Age: 4,000 BCE – 1,000 BCE)
The Neolithic Age marked a revolutionary turning point in human history, often termed the "Neolithic Revolution." It saw the transition from a nomadic hunting-gathering lifestyle to a settled, food-producing economy.

### Key Characteristics
*   **[[Agriculture]] and Domestication:** Humans began cultivating plants (wheat, barley, rice) and fully domesticated animals for milk, meat, and labor.
*   **Tools:** The defining technological shift was the use of **polished and ground stone tools**, particularly stone axes (celts), which were highly effective for clearing [[forests]] for [[agriculture]].
*   **Pottery:** With the advent of [[agriculture]], there was a need to store surplus grain and cook food, leading to the invention of pottery (initially hand-made, later wheel-turned).
*   **Settled Life:** People began living in sedentary communities, building circular or rectangular houses made of mud and reed.

### Major Neolithic Sites in India
*   **Mehrgarh (Balochistan, Pakistan):** Often considered the precursor to the [[Indus Valley Civilization]], Mehrgarh is the oldest agricultural settlement in the Indian subcontinent. It provides the earliest evidence for the cultivation of wheat and cotton, and the domestication of animals.
*   **Burzahom and Gufkral (Jammu & Kashmir):** Famous for their **pit dwellings** (houses dug into the ground to survive the cold). Burzahom is uniquely known for the practice of **burying pet dogs with their masters**.
*   **Chirand (Bihar):** Notable for the abundant use of tools and weapons made entirely of bone (specifically deer antlers).
*   **Piklihal and Tekkalakota (Karnataka):** Important South Indian Neolithic sites known for ash mounds and cattle-herding communities.

# The Arrival of Europeans in India

## 1. Introduction
[[The arrival of Europeans in India]] marked the beginning of a new era that eventually led to colonial subjugation. The primary motivation for finding a new sea route to India was to bypass the Ottoman Empire's monopoly over the traditional land and Red Sea spice trade routes after the fall of Constantinople in 1453.

The sequence of [[European arrival]] in India was: **Portuguese ➔ Dutch ➔ English ➔ Danes ➔ French**.
*(Mnemonic: **P**e**D**a **E**nglish **D**efinitely **F**ry)*

## 2. The Portuguese (First to arrive, Last to leave)
*   **Vasco da Gama:** Discovered the sea route to India via the Cape of Good Hope. He arrived at **Calicut (Kozhikode)** on May 20, 1498, and was received by the local Hindu ruler, the Zamorin.
*   **Francisco de Almeida (1505-1509):** The first Portuguese [[Governor]]. He initiated the **'Blue Water Policy'** (Cartaz system) to establish Portuguese mastery over the Indian Ocean rather than building territorial empires on land.
*   **Afonso de Albuquerque (1509-1515):** The real founder of Portuguese [[power]] in the East.
    *   He captured **Goa** from the Sultan of Bijapur in 1510 (making it the first bit of Indian territory under European control since Alexander the Great).
    *   He abolished Sati in his territories and encouraged Portuguese men to marry Indian women to build a [[permanent settlement]].
*   **Decline:** The Portuguese monopoly was eventually broken by the Dutch and English. They lost Hugli to the Mughals ([[Shah Jahan]]) and Bombay to the English (as dowry for Charles II's marriage to Catherine of Braganza). They were ultimately restricted to Goa, Daman, and Diu, which they held until **1961** (Operation Vijay).

## 3. The Dutch (Netherlands)
*   The Dutch [[East India Company]] (VOC) was formed in 1602.
*   **Headquarters:** Initially Pulicat (until 1690), later shifted to **Nagapattinam**.
*   **Focus:** Their primary interest was not India, but the **Spice [[Islands]] (Indonesia)**. They used India mainly as a trading post to buy cotton textiles to exchange for spices in Indonesia.
*   **Battle of Colachel (1741):** The Dutch suffered a humiliating defeat at the hands of Marthanda Varma of Travancore.
*   **Battle of Bedara (1759):** The English decisively defeated the Dutch in Bengal, effectively ending their ambitions in India. They eventually traded all their Indian settlements to the British for total control over Indonesia.

## 4. The English (British East India Company)
*   **Foundation:** The English [[East India Company]] (EIC) was granted a Royal Charter by Queen Elizabeth I on December 31, 1600, giving it a monopoly on trade in the East.
*   **Early Embassies:**
    *   **Captain William Hawkins** arrived at the court of [[Jahangir]] in 1609 but failed to get permission to build a factory due to Portuguese [[pressure]].
    *   **Sir Thomas Roe** (Ambassador of King James I) visited [[Jahangir]] in 1615 and successfully obtained an imperial *farman* to establish factories in various parts of the [[Mughal Empire]].
*   **Key Settlements:**
    *   **West:** Surat (first factory in 1613), Bombay (acquired in 1668).
    *   **South:** Masulipatnam (1611), Madras (Fort St. George, founded in 1639).
    *   **East:** Calcutta (Fort William, founded in 1690 by Job Charnock).
*   **The Farman of 1717:** Mughal Emperor **Farrukhsiyar** issued a *farman* (called the Magna Carta of the Company) granting the EIC duty-free trade rights in Bengal, Gujarat, and Hyderabad, which gave them a massive advantage over their rivals.

## 5. The Danes (Denmark)
*   The Danish [[East India Company]] was established in 1616.
*   **Settlements:** Tranquebar (Tamil Nadu) and Serampore (Bengal) - their headquarters.
*   They were never a major political or commercial threat. They are best known for their **missionary activities** (the Serampore Mission Press). They sold all their settlements to the British in 1845.

## 6. The French (The Last Arrivers)
*   The French [[East India Company]] (Compagnie des Indes Orientales) was formed in 1664 by Colbert (minister to King Louis XIV). Unlike the British EIC, it was heavily controlled and funded by the French government.
*   **Headquarters:** **Pondicherry** (founded by Francois Martin in 1674). Other key settlements were Chandernagore (Bengal), Mahe, and Karaikal.
*   **Joseph Francois Dupleix:** A brilliant French [[Governor]] who initiated the strategy of interfering in the internal succession disputes of Indian princes to gain political and territorial advantages.

## 7. The Carnatic Wars (Anglo-French Rivalry)
The struggle for supremacy between the British and the French in India was fought out in the Carnatic (Coromandel coast) region through three wars.

1.  **First Carnatic War (1746-1748):** An extension of the War of the Austrian Succession in Europe. Ended with the **Treaty of Aix-la-Chapelle**. It proved the superiority of disciplined European infantry over massive Indian armies (Battle of St. Thome).
2.  **Second Carnatic War (1749-1754):** Fought over succession disputes in Hyderabad and the Carnatic. Robert Clive emerged as a brilliant British commander (Siege of Arcot). Ended with the **Treaty of Pondicherry** (Dupleix was recalled to France).
3.  **Third Carnatic War (1758-1763):** An extension of the Seven Years' War in Europe. 
    *   **Battle of Wandiwash (1760):** The British General Sir Eyre Coote decisively defeated the French General Count de Lally. 
    *   Ended with the **Treaty of Paris (1763)**. The French were allowed to keep their factories but were forbidden from fortifying them or maintaining an army. This established the British as the undisputed European masters of India.

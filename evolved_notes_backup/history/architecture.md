# Indian Architecture: Ancient to Medieval

## 1. Indus Valley Architecture
The [[Indus Valley Civilization]] (IVC) is renowned for its exceptional urban planning, which was vastly superior to contemporary civilizations in Egypt and Mesopotamia.

![Indus Valley Civilization - City Plan & Architecture](/assets/diagrams/history_1_ancient_india_indus_valley_civilization_ivc_city_plan.png)

*   **Grid System:** Cities were laid out in a grid pattern, with main streets intersecting at right angles.
*   **Town Planning:** The city was usually divided into two parts:
    *   **The Citadel (Acropolis):** Built on a raised mud-brick platform. It housed important public buildings (like the Great Bath and Granaries) and was likely occupied by the ruling class.
    *   **The Lower Town:** Where the common people lived.
*   **[[Drainage System]]:** The most striking feature. Every house had its own bathroom connected to street drains, which were covered with bricks or stone slabs and equipped with manholes for cleaning.
*   **Building Material:** Extensive use of standardized **burnt bricks** (unlike Egypt, which used dried bricks). No stone was used in construction. 
*   **Key Structures:** The **Great Bath** at Mohenjo-Daro (used for ritual bathing) and large **Granaries** (found at Harappa and Mohenjo-Daro) with raised platforms for ventilation.

## 2. Buddhist Architecture
Buddhist [[architecture]] evolved significantly during the Mauryan and Post-Mauryan periods, primarily featuring three structures:

### A. Stupas
*   **Purpose:** A solid hemispherical dome used to house relics (hair, teeth, bones) of the Buddha or prominent Buddhist monks.
*   **Key Components:**
    *   *Anda:* The hemispherical mound.
    *   *Harmika:* A square balcony-like structure on top of the dome, representing the abode of the gods.
    *   *Chhatra:* A central pillar supporting three umbrellas (representing the Three Jewels of [[Buddhism]]).
    *   *Pradakshina Patha:* The circular circumambulatory pathway around the stupa.
    *   *Torana:* Beautifully carved gateways at the four cardinal points.
*   **Famous Example:** The **Sanchi Stupa** (Madhya Pradesh), originally commissioned by [[Ashoka]] and later expanded by the Sungas and Satavahanas.

### B. Chaityas and Viharas
*   **Chaitya:** A Buddhist prayer hall or shrine. It usually has a stupa at one end. Famous examples include the Chaitya at Karle (Maharashtra).
*   **Vihara:** A residential monastery for Buddhist monks. They consist of a central courtyard surrounded by small cells.
*   **Ajanta and Ellora:** The rock-cut caves of Ajanta and Ellora (Maharashtra) contain some of the finest examples of Chaityas and Viharas, heavily patronized by the Vakatakas and [[Rashtrakutas]].

## 3. Hindu Temple Architecture
Hindu temple [[architecture]] began in the [[Gupta period]] (as simple, flat-roofed structures like the Dashavatara Temple at Deogarh) and evolved into three distinct styles across India.

### A. Nagara Style (North India)
*   **Region:** Primarily found between the [[Himalayas]] and the Vindhyas.
*   **Features:**
    *   Temples are generally built on a high stone platform.
    *   **Shikhara:** A tall, curving, beehive-shaped tower over the main sanctum.
    *   **Amalaka:** A large fluted disc located at the top of the Shikhara.
    *   **Kalasha:** A pot-like finial placed above the Amalaka.
    *   Absence of elaborate boundary walls or imposing gateways.
*   **Famous Examples:** The **Khajuraho temples** (built by the Chandellas, featuring the Kandariya Mahadeva temple), the **Sun Temple at Konark** (Odisha), and the **Lingaraja Temple** (Bhubaneswar).

### B. Dravida Style (South India)
*   **Region:** Found between the [[Krishna]] river and Kanyakumari. Reached its zenith under the [[Cholas]].
*   **Features:**
    *   Temples are enclosed within massive boundary walls.
    *   **Gopuram:** Lofty, pyramidal gateway towers at the entrance of the temple complex.
    *   **Vimana:** The main tower over the sanctum. Unlike the curved Nagara Shikhara, the Vimana is a straight-edged step pyramid.
    *   **Shikhara:** In Dravida style, the Shikhara refers only to the octagonal or dome-shaped crowning element at the very top (unlike the entire tower in the North).
    *   Presence of a large water tank (Kalyani) within the temple enclosure.
*   **Famous Examples:** The **Brihadeshwara Temple at Thanjavur** (built by Rajaraja Chola), the Shore Temple at Mahabalipuram ([[Pallavas]]), and the Meenakshi Temple at Madurai.

### C. Vesara Style (Deccan)
*   **Region:** The Deccan region, between the Vindhyas and the [[Krishna]] river.
*   **Features:** A hybrid style that combines the features of both Nagara and Dravida styles. It features the Vimana of the South and the Shikhara shape of the North.
*   **Famous Examples:** The temples built by the Chalukyas of Badami and the Hoysalas. The **Hoysaleswara Temple at Halebid** (known for its intricate, star-shaped plan) and the Virupaksha Temple at Pattadakal.

## 4. Indo-Islamic Architecture
The arrival of Islamic rulers brought new architectural [[elements]] (the arcuate style) that blended with indigenous Indian [[elements]] (the trabeate style), creating the Indo-Islamic style.

### Key New Features Introduced:
*   Use of **Arches and Domes** (eliminating the need for multiple pillars to support the roof).
*   Use of **Mortar** (especially limestone) as a cementing agent.
*   **Minarets** (tall towers) used for the call to prayer (*Azaan*).
*   Avoidance of human and animal figures in decoration. Instead, they used **Calligraphy, Arabesque (geometric patterns), and Pietra Dura** (inlay of semi-precious stones).

### Evolution:
*   **[[Delhi Sultanate]]:** 
    *   *Slave Dynasty:* The Qutb Minar complex and Quwwat-ul-Islam mosque (built using materials from demolished Hindu/Jain temples).
    *   *Khalji Dynasty:* Prominent use of red sandstone and the true arch (e.g., Alai Darwaza).
    *   *Tughlaq Dynasty:* Stark, massive, and unornamented structures with sloping walls (*Batter* effect), e.g., Tughlaqabad Fort.
*   **Mughal [[Architecture]]:** The zenith of Indo-Islamic [[architecture]].
    *   *[[Humayun]]'s Tomb:* Built during [[Akbar]]'s reign, it was the first garden-tomb (Charbagh style) on the Indian subcontinent and a precursor to the Taj Mahal.
    *   *[[Akbar]]:* Built Fatehpur Sikri (Buland Darwaza, Panch Mahal) using mostly red sandstone.
    *   *[[Shah Jahan]]:* Replaced red sandstone with pristine white marble. Reached the absolute peak with the **Taj Mahal**, the Jama Masjid, and the Red Fort in Delhi.

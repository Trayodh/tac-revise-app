## 1. Introduction
[[The Revolt of 1857]], also known as the Sepoy Mutiny or the First War of Indian Independence (a term popularized by V.D. Savarkar), was a massive, though ultimately unsuccessful, uprising against the British [[East India Company]]'s rule in India.

## 2. Causes of the Revolt
The rebellion was not a sudden occurrence but the culmination of a century of accumulated grievances.

*   **Political Causes:** Lord Dalhousie's aggressive expansionist policies, particularly the **[[Doctrine of Lapse]]**, disinherited many native rulers (Satara, Jhansi, Nagpur). The annexation of Awadh (1856) on grounds of "maladministration" deeply angered the sepoys, a large percentage of whom came from Awadh.
*   **Economic Causes:** The heavy taxation under the Zamindari and [[Ryotwari]] systems ruined the peasantry. The influx of cheap British machine-made goods destroyed traditional Indian handicrafts, leading to massive de-industrialization and unemployment.
*   **Socio-Religious Causes:** Activities of Christian missionaries created fears of [[mass]] conversion. Reforms like the Abolition of Sati (1829), the Widow Remarriage Act (1856), and the Religious Disabilities Act (which allowed converts to inherit ancestral property) were viewed by orthodox Indians as an attack on their religion.
*   **Military Causes:** Indian sepoys faced severe discrimination in salary and promotion compared to British soldiers. The **General Service Enlistment Act (1856)** mandated that future recruits must be ready to serve overseas; crossing the sea was considered a loss of caste for high-caste Hindus.
*   **Immediate Cause:** The introduction of the new **Enfield Rifle**. A rumor spread that the paper cartridges (which had to be bitten off before loading) were greased with beef and pig fat, deeply offending the religious sentiments of both Hindu and Muslim sepoys.

## 3. Outbreak and Spread
*   **Mangal Pandey (March 29, 1857):** The first spark. A sepoy of the 34th Native Infantry at Barrackpore (Bengal) refused to use the greased cartridges and fired at his European officers. He was hanged on April 8.
*   **The Mutiny Begins (May 10, 1857):** The revolt officially broke out in **Meerut**. The sepoys broke open the jail, released their comrades, killed British officers, and marched to Delhi.
*   **Delhi (May 11):** The mutineers captured Delhi and proclaimed the aging Mughal Emperor **Bahadur Shah Zafar** as the Emperor of India (*Shahenshah-e-Hind*).

## 4. Causes of Failure
Despite its massive scale, the revolt failed due to several critical weaknesses:
1.  **Lack of Unity and All-India Participation:** The revolt was largely confined to North and Central India. Large parts of the country, including the Madras and Bombay presidencies, Punjab, and Bengal, remained quiet or loyal to the British.
2.  **Lack of Effective Leadership and Coordination:** The rebels lacked a unified command structure, a common ideology, and a forward-looking vision. Leaders were fighting primarily for their own local grievances (e.g., Rani of Jhansi for her state, Nana Saheb for his pension).
3.  **Absence of Support from Educated Indians and Princes:** Most ruling chiefs (like the Scindias of Gwalior and Holkars of Indore) actively helped the British. The educated middle class did not support the revolt, viewing the British as agents of modernization and the rebels as backward-looking feudal [[elements]].
4.  **Superior British [[Resources]]:** The British had superior weapons (Enfield rifles vs. swords/muskets), better generals (Lawrence, Campbell, Havelock), and control over the telegraph system, allowing for swift communication and troop movement.

## 5. Impact and Significance (The Queen's Proclamation)
The revolt was a turning point in Indian history. It ended the rule of the [[East India Company]].

*   **Government of India Act 1858:** [[Power]] was formally transferred from the EIC to the British Crown. The [[Governor]]-General acquired the title of **Viceroy** (Lord Canning).
*   **Queen Victoria's Proclamation (November 1, 1858):** Read out by Lord Canning at the Allahabad Durbar. It promised:
    *   No further territorial expansion (abandonment of the [[Doctrine of Lapse]]).
    *   Respect for the rights, dignity, and honor of native princes.
    *   Non-[[interference]] in the religious beliefs and customs of Indians.
    *   Equal protection under the law.
*   **Military Reorganization:** The ratio of British to Indian soldiers was significantly increased (1:2 in Bengal, 1:3 in Madras/Bombay). Artillery was kept exclusively in European hands. The concept of "martial races" (Sikhs, Gurkhas, Pathans) was developed to divide and rule the army.

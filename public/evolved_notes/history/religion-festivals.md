## 1. Major Religions Originating in India

### A. Hinduism
*   **Nature:** It is the oldest and most widely practiced religion in India. It is a complex synthesis of various beliefs and philosophies (Sanatana Dharma), lacking a single founder, a single unified text, or a central religious authority.
*   **Key Concepts:** *Brahman* (the ultimate, formless reality), *Atman* (the individual soul), *Karma* (the law of cause and effect), *Samsara* (the cycle of rebirth), and *Moksha* (liberation from the cycle).
*   **Major Deities:** The Trimurti (Brahma the Creator, Vishnu the Preserver, Shiva the Destroyer) and the Tridevi (Saraswati, Lakshmi, Parvati/Durga).
*   **Sacred Texts:** The Shruti ([[Vedas]], [[Upanishads]]) and the Smriti (Epics like Ramayana and Mahabharata, Puranas, Dharmashastras).

### B. Buddhism
*   **Founder:** Siddhartha Gautama (The Buddha), born in Lumbini (Nepal) in the 6th century BCE.
*   **Core Philosophy:** The **Four Noble Truths** (Life is suffering; desire is the cause of suffering; suffering can be ended; the path to end suffering is the Noble Eightfold Path).
*   **Ultimate Goal:** *Nirvana* (the blowing out of the flames of desire and ignorance).
*   **Major Sects:** 
    *   **Theravada (Hinayana):** The 'Lesser Vehicle'. Focuses on individual enlightenment and strictly follows the original teachings of the Buddha. Does not worship idols.
    *   **Mahayana:** The 'Greater Vehicle'. Focuses on the salvation of all beings through the help of *Bodhisattvas* (enlightened beings who delay Nirvana to help others). Worships the Buddha as a deity in idol form.
    *   **Vajrayana:** Tantric [[Buddhism]] (prominent in Tibet).

### C. Jainism
*   **Founders:** The 24 *Tirthankaras* (ford-makers). The first was Rishabhanatha, and the 24th and most prominent historical figure was **Vardhamana Mahavira** (a contemporary of Buddha).
*   **Core Philosophy:** Extreme emphasis on **Ahimsa** (non-violence) to all living beings. Jains believe that everything, even inanimate objects, has a soul.
*   **Three Jewels (Triratna):** Right Faith, Right Knowledge, Right Conduct.
*   **Major Sects:**
    *   **Digambara:** 'Sky-clad'. Monks do not wear clothes, believing that attachment to clothing is a hindrance to salvation. Women cannot achieve salvation in this sect.
    *   **Svetambara:** 'White-clad'. Monks and nuns wear simple white clothes. They believe women can achieve salvation.

### D. Sikhism
*   **Founder:** **Guru Nanak Dev** (15th century CE) in the Punjab region.
*   **Core Philosophy:** Monotheism (belief in one Formless God, *Ik Onkar*). Emphasizes [[equality]] of all humans, rejection of the caste system, and honest labor (*Kirat Karo*).
*   **Sacred Text:** The **Guru Granth Sahib**, which is considered the eternal, living Guru.
*   **Key Practices:** *Langar* (free community kitchen) and the maintenance of the Five Ks (Kesh, Kanga, Kara, Kachera, Kirpan) by the initiated Khalsa.

## 2. Major Festivals of India

India's incredible diversity is reflected in its vast array of festivals, which are often tied to the agricultural calendar, religious mythology, or seasonal changes.

### A. Hindu Festivals
*   **Diwali (Deepavali):** The Festival of Lights. Celebrates the return of Lord Rama to Ayodhya after 14 years of exile and his victory over Ravana. It symbolizes the spiritual victory of [[light]] over darkness and good over evil.
*   **Holi:** The Festival of Colors. A spring festival celebrating the eternal and divine love of Radha [[Krishna]], and the victory of Lord Vishnu (as Narasimha) over the demon king Hiranyakashipu (and his sister Holika).
*   **Dussehra (Vijayadashami):** Marks the end of Navaratri (the nine-day worship of Goddess Durga). Celebrates the victory of Durga over the buffalo demon Mahishasura, and Rama's victory over Ravana.
*   **Maha Shivaratri:** The 'Great Night of Shiva', celebrating the marriage of Shiva and Parvati. Devotees fast and keep vigil all night.
*   **Ganesh Chaturthi:** A massive 10-day festival (especially prominent in Maharashtra) celebrating the birth of Lord Ganesha, the remover of obstacles. Concludes with the immersion (*Visarjan*) of Ganesha idols in water.
*   **Kumbh Mela:** The largest peaceful gathering in the world. It rotates between four pilgrimage places on four sacred [[rivers]]: Haridwar ([[Ganga]]), Ujjain (Shipra), Nashik ([[Godavari]]), and Prayagraj (Confluence of [[Ganga]], Yamuna, and Saraswati). Held every 12 years at each location.

### B. Islamic Festivals
*   **Eid-ul-Fitr:** The 'Festival of Breaking the Fast'. Marks the end of **Ramadan**, the holy month of fasting.
*   **Eid-ul-Adha:** The 'Festival of Sacrifice'. Commemorates the willingness of Ibrahim (Abraham) to sacrifice his son as an act of obedience to God.
*   **Muharram:** The first month of the Islamic calendar. The 10th day (Ashura) is a day of intense mourning for Shia Muslims, commemorating the martyrdom of Imam Hussain (grandson of the Prophet Muhammad) at the Battle of Karbala.

### C. Other Important Festivals
*   **Baisakhi (Sikh/Hindu):** A spring harvest festival in Punjab. For Sikhs, it commemorates the formation of the **Khalsa Panth** by Guru Gobind Singh in 1699.
*   **Buddha Purnima (Buddhist):** Celebrates the birth, enlightenment (Nirvana), and death (Parinirvana) of Gautama Buddha, all of which conventionally fall on the same full moon day.
*   **Mahavir Janma Kalyanak (Jain):** Celebrates the birth of Lord Mahavira, the 24th Tirthankara.
*   **Christmas (Christian):** Celebrates the birth of Jesus Christ (December 25).
*   **Navroz (Zoroastrian/Parsi):** The Iranian and Parsi New Year, celebrated on the vernal equinox (first day of spring).

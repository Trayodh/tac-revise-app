## 1. Introduction and Causes
[[World War II]] was the deadliest and most widespread conflict in human history, involving the vast majority of the world's nations.

**Key Causes:**
1.  **The Flawed Treaty of Versailles:** Crippling reparations and territorial losses bred intense German resentment and a desire for revenge.
2.  **Rise of Fascism and Totalitarianism:**
    *   **Germany:** Adolf Hitler and the Nazi Party rose to [[power]], preaching Aryan supremacy and demanding *Lebensraum* (living space) in Eastern Europe.
    *   **Italy:** Benito Mussolini established a fascist dictatorship aiming to recreate the Roman Empire.
    *   **Japan:** Extreme militarism took over, driving an aggressive imperial expansion into China and the Pacific for [[resources]].
3.  **Failure of the League of Nations:** The League proved utterly powerless to stop aggression by Japan (invasion of Manchuria, 1931), Italy (invasion of Ethiopia, 1935), and Germany.
4.  **Policy of Appeasement:** Britain and France repeatedly gave in to Hitler's territorial demands (e.g., the Munich Agreement of 1938, giving Germany the Sudetenland) in a desperate, failed attempt to avoid another war.

**The Spark:** On **September 1, 1939, Germany invaded Poland**. Two days later, Britain and France declared war on Germany, officially beginning WWII.

## 3. The European Theatre
*   **Blitzkrieg (Lightning War):** Germany's highly effective military tactic using rapid, coordinated attacks by tanks (Panzers) and air [[power]] (Luftwaffe). They rapidly conquered Poland, Denmark, Norway, Belgium, and France (which fell in just six weeks in 1940).
*   **Battle of Britain (1940):** The first major campaign fought entirely by air forces. The British RAF successfully defended the UK against the German Luftwaffe, preventing a German invasion.
*   **Operation Barbarossa (1941):** Hitler's massive, surprise invasion of the Soviet Union. It was the largest military operation in history. 
*   **Battle of Stalingrad (1942-43):** The turning point of the war in Europe. The Soviet Red Army decisively defeated the German army in brutal urban combat, halting the German advance and beginning their long push back to Berlin.
*   **D-Day (June 6, 1944):** The massive Allied amphibious invasion of Normandy, France (Operation Overlord), which opened a second major front in Europe and began the liberation of Western Europe.
*   **Fall of Berlin (1945):** Soviet forces captured Berlin. Hitler committed suicide on April 30. Germany formally surrendered on May 8, 1945 (V-E Day).

## 4. The Pacific Theatre
*   **Pearl Harbor (December 7, 1941):** Japan launched a surprise naval and air attack on the US Pacific Fleet in Hawaii. This brought the United States into the war on both fronts.
*   **Battle of Midway (1942):** The turning point in the Pacific. The US Navy decisively defeated the Japanese fleet, destroying four of their aircraft carriers and halting Japanese expansion.
*   **Island Hopping:** The US strategy of capturing strategically important [[islands]] across the Pacific while bypassing heavily fortified ones, slowly closing in on the Japanese home [[islands]].
*   **The Atomic Bombs:** To [[force]] a swift Japanese surrender and avoid a horrific land invasion of Japan, US [[President]] Harry Truman ordered the dropping of atomic bombs on **Hiroshima (August 6, 1945)** and **Nagasaki (August 9, 1945)**. 
*   Japan formally surrendered on September 2, 1945 (V-J Day), aboard the USS Missouri, ending the war.

## 5. The Holocaust
The systematic, state-sponsored genocide orchestrated by Nazi Germany.
*   Hitler's "Final Solution" aimed to exterminate the entire Jewish [[population]] of Europe.
*   Six million Jews (two-thirds of Europe's Jewish [[population]]) were murdered in extermination camps (like Auschwitz and Treblinka) using gas chambers, alongside millions of others including Romani people, Soviet POWs, disabled individuals, and political dissidents.

## 6. Global Consequences
*   **The [[Cold War]]:** The alliance between the USA and the USSR immediately collapsed, splitting the world into two ideological superpowers (Capitalism/Democracy vs. Communism).
*   **Creation of the United Nations (1945):** Established to replace the failed League of Nations, with a stronger mandate and structure (including the Security Council) to prevent future global conflicts.
*   **Decolonization:** The war exhausted the European imperial powers (Britain, France, Netherlands). This catalyzed a massive [[wave]] of independence movements across Asia and Africa, leading to the dismantling of colonial empires (including India's independence in 1947).

## 1. Introduction and Rise of the Guptas
Following the disintegration of the Kushan and Satavahana empires, the Gupta dynasty emerged in Magadha and reunified a large part of Northern India. Though their empire was not as vast as the Mauryas, the [[Gupta period]] is widely considered the **"Golden Age" of ancient India** due to unprecedented advancements in art, [[literature]], science, [[metallurgy]], and mathematics.

![The Gupta Empire: The Golden Age of India](/assets/diagrams/history_3_ancient_india_gupta_period_gupta_empire_infographic.png)

### Key Rulers
1.  **Sri Gupta & Ghatotkacha:** The early, relatively obscure founders of the dynasty. They used the simple title *Maharaja*.
2.  **[[Chandragupta]] I (319 – 334 CE):**
    *   The first powerful ruler, he assumed the title *Maharajadhiraja* (King of Kings).
    *   Started the **Gupta Era (319-320 CE)** to commemorate his accession.
    *   Married a Licchavi princess, Kumaradevi, which greatly elevated the political prestige of the Guptas. He issued gold coins jointly with his queen.
3.  **[[Samudragupta]] (335 – 380 CE):**
    *   Known as the **"Napoleon of India"** (a title given by V.A. Smith) for his relentless military conquests.
    *   His court poet, **Harisena**, composed the **Prayag Prashasti (Allahabad Pillar Inscription)**, detailing his victories across North and South India.
    *   Unlike the Mauryas who annexed territory, [[Samudragupta]] followed a policy of *Grahana-Moksha-Anugraha* (capture, liberate, and reinstate) towards the southern kings, making them tributaries.
    *   He was a great patron of arts and is depicted on his coins playing the Veena.
4.  **[[Chandragupta]] II / Vikramaditya (380 – 415 CE):**
    *   The empire reached its peak of territorial expansion and cultural zenith during his reign.
    *   He defeated the Shaka Kshatrapas of Ujjain, annexing western India (Gujarat/Malwa) and giving the Guptas access to the lucrative western seaports. To celebrate this, he issued silver coins (the first Gupta king to do so) and took the title *Vikramaditya* and *Sakari* (Destroyer of Shakas).
    *   The Chinese pilgrim **Fa-Hien** visited India during his reign.
    *   His court at Ujjain was adorned by the **Navaratnas (Nine Gems)**, including Kalidasa and Amarasimha.
5.  **Kumaragupta I (415 – 455 CE):**
    *   Founded the world-renowned **Nalanda University** in Bihar.
    *   Faced the first waves of the Huna invasions but repulsed them.
6.  **Skandagupta (455 – 467 CE):**
    *   The last great Gupta king. He successfully defeated the ferocious Hunas (White Huns) who invaded from Central Asia, saving the empire from immediate collapse.

## 2. Administration and Society
Unlike the highly centralized Mauryan state, the Gupta administration was highly decentralized and feudal in character.
*   **Decentralization:** [[Power]] was delegated to provincial governors (*Uparikas*) and district heads (*Vishayapatis*). The practice of granting tax-free land to Brahmanas and temples (*Agrahara* grants) accelerated, creating a class of powerful local landlords and weakening central authority.
*   **Society:** The Varna system became highly rigid. The condition of women deteriorated further (first epigraphic evidence of the *Sati* system is found in the **Eran Inscription of 510 CE** during the reign of Bhanugupta). However, untouchability increased, and Fa-Hien notes that the Chandalas had to live outside the city and strike a piece of wood when entering to warn others of their polluting presence.

## 3. Cultural and Scientific Zenith

### Literature (The Age of Sanskrit)
Sanskrit became the official court language, replacing Prakrit.
*   **Kalidasa:** The greatest Sanskrit poet and dramatist. Wrote plays like *Abhijnanasakuntalam*, *Malavikagnimitram*, and epics like *Raghuvamsha* and *Meghaduta*.
*   **Vishakhadatta:** Wrote *Mudrarakshasa* (details [[Chandragupta]] Maurya's rise) and *Devichandraguptam*.
*   **Vishnu Sharma:** Composed the *Panchatantra* (animal fables).
*   **Shudraka:** Wrote *Mrichchhakatika* (The Little Clay Cart).

### Science, Mathematics, and Astronomy
*   **Aryabhata:** Wrote the *Aryabhatiya*. He was the first to mathematically prove that the Earth rotates on its axis, scientifically explained the causes of solar and lunar eclipses, and calculated the value of Pi (π) with high precision.
*   **Varahamihira:** Wrote the *Brihat Samhita* (an encyclopedia of astronomy, botany, and geography) and *Panchasiddhantika* (five astronomical systems).
*   **Sushruta:** Wrote the *Sushruta Samhita*, a foundational text on Ayurveda and surgical procedures (including plastic surgery/rhinoplasty).
*   **Brahmagupta:** Outlined the rules for using Zero as a number and introduced negative numbers.

### Art, Architecture, and Metallurgy
*   **Temples:** The [[Gupta period]] marks the beginning of free-standing structural Hindu temples. Example: The **Dashavatara Temple at Deogarh** (UP), one of the earliest examples of the Panchayatana style (a central shrine surrounded by four smaller shrines).
*   **Caves:** The breathtaking rock-cut caves of **Ajanta** (famous for fresco [[paintings]] of Buddhist Jataka tales) and **Ellora** were significantly developed during this time.
*   **[[Metallurgy]]:** The **Mehrauli Iron Pillar** in Delhi (erected by a king named 'Chandra', likely [[Chandragupta]] II) is a marvel of ancient [[metallurgy]], as it has not rusted despite being exposed to the [[elements]] for over 1500 years. The massive Sultanganj copper statue of Buddha is another prime example.

## 4. Decline of the Guptas
The empire declined rapidly after Skandagupta due to repeated Huna invasions (led by Toramana and Mihirakula), weak successors, internal rebellions by feudal lords, and the economic strain of constant warfare. By 540 CE, the Gupta empire had ceased to exist.

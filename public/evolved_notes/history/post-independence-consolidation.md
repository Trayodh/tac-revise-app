# Post-Independence Consolidation (1947 - 1964)

## 1. Introduction
India achieved independence on August 15, 1947, but it was born amidst the trauma of Partition. The new nation faced monumental challenges: the integration of hundreds of princely states, the rehabilitation of millions of refugees, the drafting of a [[constitution]], and the massive task of economic development.

## 2. Integration of Princely States
At the time of independence, British India was divided into provinces directly ruled by the British and 565 Princely States ruled by native princes under British paramountcy. The Indian [[Independence Act]] allowed these states to join either India or Pakistan, or remain independent.

*   **The Architects:** The colossal task of integrating these states into the Indian Union was masterfully executed by **Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel** (the Iron Man of India and the first Deputy Prime Minister) and his able secretary, **V.P. Menon**.
*   **The Instrument of Accession:** Patel used a mix of persuasion, diplomacy, and the threat of [[force]] ("carrot and stick" policy) to convince the princes to sign the Instrument of Accession, surrendering Defense, External Affairs, and Communications to the Indian government. By August 15, 1947, almost all states contiguous to India had acceded.
*   **The Three Holdouts:**
    1.  **Junagadh:** The Nawab (a Muslim) wanted to join Pakistan, but the [[population]] (predominantly Hindu) revolted. The Nawab fled to Pakistan, and Junagadh was integrated into India following a plebiscite.
    2.  **Hyderabad:** The largest princely state, ruled by the Nizam. He wanted to remain independent and encouraged the violent Razakar militia. Patel ordered **Operation Polo** (a police action) in September 1948, forcing the Nizam to surrender and integrate with India.
    3.  **Kashmir:** The Hindu ruler (Maharaja Hari Singh) of a Muslim-majority state initially wanted to remain independent. When Pakistani tribal raiders invaded in October 1947, he signed the Instrument of Accession with India in exchange for military help, leading to the First Indo-Pak War (1947-48).

## 3. Linguistic Reorganization of States
The integration of states resulted in a chaotic map of India, categorized into Part A, B, C, and D states. There was a massive popular demand to reorganize states on linguistic (language) lines.

*   **Early Committees:** The Dhar Commission (1948) and the JVP Committee (Jawaharlal Nehru, Vallabhbhai Patel, Pattabhi Sitaramayya, 1948) both rejected language as the primary basis for reorganization, prioritizing national unity and security.
*   **Potti Sriramulu:** In 1952, a veteran Gandhian, Potti Sriramulu, undertook a fast unto death demanding a separate Telugu-speaking state out of the Madras presidency. His death after 56 days sparked massive riots.
*   **Creation of Andhra (1953):** Forced by the protests, the government created **Andhra State, the first linguistic state in India**.
*   **States Reorganisation Commission (SRC):** Set up in 1953 under Fazl Ali (along with K.M. Panikkar and H.N. Kunzru) to objectively look into the issue. It accepted language as the basis for reorganization.
*   **States Reorganisation Act (1956):** Abolished the Part A/B/C/D system and created **14 states and 6 [[Union Territories]]** primarily on linguistic lines.

## 4. Drafting the Constitution
*   **The [[Constituent Assembly]]:** Formed in 1946 under the [[Cabinet Mission Plan]]. It took 2 years, 11 months, and 18 days to frame the [[Constitution]].
*   **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar:** The [[Chairman]] of the crucial Drafting Committee. Known as the **Father of the Indian [[Constitution]]**. He ensured the inclusion of strong safeguards for the Depressed Classes (Dalits).
*   **Adoption:** The [[Constitution]] was adopted on **November 26, 1949** (celebrated as [[Constitution]] Day) and came into full effect on **January 26, 1950** ([[Republic]] Day, chosen to commemorate the 1930 Purna Swaraj declaration).

## 5. Economic Consolidation and Planning
Jawaharlal Nehru, deeply influenced by Fabian socialism and the Soviet model, believed that heavy industrialization and state-led planning were essential to lift India out of poverty.

*   **Planning Commission (1950):** Established as an extra-constitutional body, with the Prime Minister as its ex-officio [[chairman]], to formulate Five-Year Plans.
*   **First Five-Year Plan (1951-1956):** Based on the Harrod-Domar model. It prioritized **[[Agriculture]]** and [[irrigation]] (construction of huge dams like Bhakra Nangal and Hirakud, which Nehru called the "temples of modern India"). It was a major success.
*   **Second Five-Year Plan (1956-1961):** Based on the **Mahalanobis model**. It marked a major shift towards rapid industrialization, focusing heavily on **heavy [[industries]] and capital goods**. Steel plants at Bhilai, Durgapur, and Rourkela were set up with foreign collaboration.

## 6. Foreign Policy: The Non-Aligned Movement (NAM)
*   **Context:** The [[Cold War]] was dividing the world into two hostile blocs led by the USA and the USSR.
*   **Nehru's Vision:** He refused to join either military bloc, choosing to retain India's strategic autonomy. This policy of **Non-Alignment** became the cornerstone of India's foreign policy.
*   **Founders of NAM:** Nehru (India), Tito (Yugoslavia), Nasser (Egypt), Sukarno (Indonesia), and Nkrumah (Ghana). The first official NAM summit was held in Belgrade in 1961.
*   **Panchsheel (1954):** The Five Principles of Peaceful Coexistence signed between India (Nehru) and China (Zhou Enlai), forming the basis of India's idealistic diplomacy in the 1950s (which tragically ended with the 1962 Sino-Indian War).

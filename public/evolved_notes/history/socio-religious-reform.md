# Socio-Religious Reform Movements

## 1. Introduction
The 19th century witnessed a series of [[socio-religious reform movements]] in India, often referred to as the Indian Renaissance. These movements were a response to the ideological challenge posed by Western culture, colonial rule, and the spread of English education.

*   **Core Ideology:** The reformers sought to blend modern rationalism, scientific thinking, and humanism with the core philosophical values of ancient Indian texts (like the [[Upanishads]] and [[Vedas]]), while rejecting later degenerate practices (like Sati, untouchability, child marriage, and idol worship).

## 2. Major Hindu Reform Movements

### A. Brahmo Samaj (Bengal)
*   **Founder:** **Raja Rammohan Roy** (1828), known as the **Father of the Indian Renaissance**.
*   **Ideology:** Monotheism (belief in one God), rejection of idol worship and meaningless rituals, and strong opposition to the caste system.
*   **Social Reform:** He led a fierce crusade against **Sati**, which was finally abolished in 1829 by Lord William Bentinck. He also advocated for women's education and property rights.
*   **Journalism:** He started the newspapers *Sambad Kaumudi* (Bengali) and *Mirat-ul-[[Akbar]]* (Persian).
*   **Successors:** After his death, the movement was led by **Debendranath Tagore** (who founded the Tattvabodhini Sabha) and later by **Keshab Chandra Sen**, under whom the Samaj split.

### B. Arya Samaj (Punjab / North India)
*   **Founder:** **Swami Dayanand Saraswati** (1875) in Bombay (later shifted to Lahore).
*   **Ideology:** **"Go back to the [[Vedas]]."** He believed the [[Vedas]] were the ultimate, infallible source of truth and rejected all post-Vedic texts (Puranas) and practices like idol worship, untouchability, and child marriage.
*   **Key [[Work]]:** He authored **Satyarth Prakash** (The [[Light]] of Truth).
*   **Shuddhi Movement:** An aggressive program to reconvert individuals who had converted to Islam or Christianity back to the Hindu fold.
*   **Education:** Established a network of **D.A.V. (Dayanand Anglo-Vedic)** schools and colleges (Lala Hansraj) and traditional Gurukuls (Swami Shraddhanand).

### C. Ramakrishna Mission (Bengal)
*   **Founder:** **Swami Vivekananda** (1897) in the name of his guru, **Ramakrishna Paramahamsa**.
*   **Ideology:** Ramakrishna was a mystic who believed that all religions are different paths to the same God. Vivekananda popularized this philosophy, blending Vedanta with modern Western organizational methods.
*   **Core Principle:** *Daridra Narayana* (Service to humanity is service to God). The Mission heavily focused on social [[work]], running hospitals, schools, and undertaking disaster relief.
*   **Global Impact:** Vivekananda dramatically introduced Hinduism to the West at the **[[Parliament]] of Religions in Chicago (1893)**.

### D. Prarthana Samaj (Maharashtra)
*   **Founder:** Atmaram Pandurang (1867) in Bombay, heavily influenced by Keshab Chandra Sen.
*   **Key Leaders:** M.G. Ranade and R.G. Bhandarkar.
*   **Focus:** Unlike the Brahmo Samaj, they did not break completely from orthodox Hinduism but focused on practical social reforms: inter-dining, inter-marriage, widow remarriage, and the upliftment of women and depressed classes.

## 3. Muslim Reform Movements

### A. The Aligarh Movement
*   **Founder:** **Sir Syed Ahmad Khan**.
*   **Ideology:** After the 1857 revolt, Muslims were backward in modern education and government jobs. Sir Syed sought to reconcile Islam with modern scientific thought and Western education to uplift the Muslim community. He advised Muslims to stay away from nationalist politics (the INC) and remain loyal to the British to secure their interests.
*   **Institutions:** Founded the Mohammedan Anglo-Oriental (MAO) College at Aligarh in 1875, which later became the **Aligarh Muslim University (AMU)**.
*   **Journal:** He published the journal *Tahzib-ul-Akhlaq* (Social Reformer).

### B. The Deoband Movement
*   **Founders:** Muhammad Qasim Nanotavi and Rashid Ahmad Gangohi (1866) at Deoband (UP).
*   **Ideology:** It was an orthodox Islamic revivalist movement (in contrast to the reformist Aligarh movement). It aimed to purify Islam, propagate the true teachings of the Quran and Hadith, and issue *fatwas* against foreign influences. In politics, unlike the Aligarh school, the Deoband school generally supported the [[Indian National Congress]] against British rule.

## 4. Other Important Reformers and Movements
*   **Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar (Bengal):** A great scholar of Sanskrit. His life's mission was the upliftment of women. Due to his relentless efforts, the **Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act was passed in 1856**. He strongly advocated for female education (Bethune College).
*   **Jyotiba Phule (Maharashtra):** A pioneer of the lower-caste and anti-Brahmin movement. He founded the **[[Satyashodhak Samaj]]** (Truth Seekers' Society) in 1873 to liberate lower castes from Brahminical oppression. He wrote the influential book **Gulamgiri** (Slavery) and opened the first school for girls in Pune with his wife, Savitribai Phule.
*   **Young Bengal Movement:** Led by **Henry Vivian Derozio** (a teacher at Hindu College). He inspired his students to think rationally, question authority, and oppose social evils. They were radical free-thinkers inspired by the French Revolution.
*   **Theosophical Society:** Founded by Madam H.P. Blavatsky and Col. M.S. Olcott in New York (1875); headquarters later moved to Adyar (Madras). **Annie Besant** popularized it in India. It aimed to study ancient religions (Hinduism/[[Buddhism]]) and promote universal brotherhood.
*   **Sikh Reform:** The **Singh Sabha Movement** (1873) aimed to restore the purity of Sikhism and establish modern educational institutions (Khalsa Colleges). It led to the **Akali Movement (Gurdwara Reform Movement)** in the 1920s to liberate Sikh shrines from corrupt, British-backed Mahants, culminating in the passing of the Sikh Gurdwaras Act (1925).

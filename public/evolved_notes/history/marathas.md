## 1. Rise of the Maratha Power under Shivaji
The [[Marathas]] were a martial community in the Deccan who initially served in the armies of the Bahmani Sultanate and its successor states (Bijapur and Ahmadnagar). They rose to prominence as an independent [[sovereign]] [[power]] under the leadership of **Chhatrapati [[Shivaji]] Maharaj**.

![Rise and Fall of the Maratha Empire](/assets/diagrams/history_4_medieval_india_marathas_maratha_empire_infographic.png)

### Shivaji (1627 – 1680)
*   **Early Life:** Born at Shivneri Fort. His father, Shahaji Bhonsle, was a jagirdar in Bijapur, and his mother was Jijabai. He was deeply influenced by his mother and his guardian, Dadaji Kondadev, as well as Bhakti saints like Tukaram and Ramdas.
*   **Military Campaigns:**
    *   Using guerrilla warfare tactics (*Ganimi Kava*), he captured several hill forts from Bijapur (Torna, Raigad, Kondhana).
    *   **Afzal Khan Episode (1659):** The Bijapur Sultan sent Afzal Khan to capture [[Shivaji]]. [[Shivaji]] killed him with a concealed tiger claw (*Bagh Nakh*) at Pratapgarh.
    *   **Treaty of Purandar (1665):** [[Aurangzeb]] sent his best Rajput general, **Raja Jai Singh of Amber**, who besieged [[Shivaji]] at Purandar. [[Shivaji]] was forced to sign the treaty, surrendering 23 forts and agreeing to visit the Mughal court at Agra.
    *   **Escape from Agra (1666):** He was imprisoned at Agra but made a daring escape hidden in a basket of sweets.
*   **Coronation (1674):** He crowned himself an independent [[sovereign]] (*Chhatrapati*) at **Raigad**, officially founding the Maratha Empire.

## 2. Shivaji's Administration
[[Shivaji]] established a highly efficient administrative system inspired by the Deccan sultanates but rooted in Hindu traditions.

*   **Ashtapradhan:** A council of eight ministers to assist the King.
    1.  **Peshwa (Mukhya Pradhan):** Prime Minister, looked after general administration.
    2.  **Amatya (Mazumdar):** Finance Minister.
    3.  **Sachiv (Surunavis):** Secretary, managed royal correspondence.
    4.  **Mantri (Waqia-navis):** Interior Minister/Intelligence.
    5.  **Senapati (Sar-i-Naubat):** Commander-in-Chief.
    6.  **Sumant (Dabir):** Foreign Minister.
    7.  **Nyayadhish:** Chief [[Justice]].
    8.  **Pandit Rao:** Head of religious affairs.
*   **Revenue System:**
    *   He abolished the Jagirdari system and paid officers in cash.
    *   **Chauth:** A tax equal to 1/4th (25%) of the standard land revenue exacted from neighboring territories not directly under Maratha control, in exchange for protection from Maratha raids.
    *   **Sardeshmukhi:** An additional 1/10th (10%) tax collected by [[Shivaji]] asserting his position as the supreme head (Sardeshmukh) of Maharashtra.
*   **Military:** Maintained a disciplined standing army. The cavalry was divided into *Bargirs* (provided horses by the state) and *Siledars* (brought their own horses).

## 3. The Peshwa Era (1713 – 1818)
After [[Shivaji]]'s death, the empire faced a war of succession and a prolonged 27-year war of independence against [[Aurangzeb]]. In 1713, under Shahu ([[Shivaji]]'s grandson), the position of **Peshwa** became hereditary and the de facto ruler of the empire, reducing the Chhatrapati to a titular head at Satara.

### Key Peshwas
1.  **Balaji Vishwanath (1713 – 1720):**
    *   The first powerful Peshwa. He secured from the Mughal emperor the *Farman* (royal decree) granting the [[Marathas]] the right to collect Chauth and Sardeshmukhi in the Deccan.
2.  **Baji Rao I (1720 – 1740):**
    *   Considered the greatest cavalry general of his time. He propounded the policy of "striking at the trunk of the withering tree" (the [[Mughal Empire]]).
    *   He vastly expanded the empire into Malwa, Gujarat, and Bundelkhand, making the [[Marathas]] the supreme [[power]] in India.
    *   Initiated the **Maratha Confederacy** system, assigning territories to prominent Maratha families (Scindias of Gwalior, Holkars of Indore, Gaekwads of Baroda, Bhonsles of Nagpur).
3.  **Balaji Baji Rao (Nana Saheb) (1740 – 1761):**
    *   Under him, the Maratha empire reached its maximum territorial limits, from Peshawar (in modern Pakistan) to Thanjavur in the south.
    *   **Third Battle of Panipat (1761):** The Maratha army, led by Sadashivrao Bhau, suffered a catastrophic defeat at the hands of the Afghan invader **Ahmad Shah Abdali**. This battle shattered the Maratha dream of replacing the Mughals as the central [[power]] of India.

## 4. Decline of the Marathas
Following the disaster at Panipat, the Maratha Confederacy became loosely held together.
*   **Madhav Rao I (1761-1772)** temporarily restored Maratha glory.
*   However, intense internal infighting among the confederacy chiefs (Scindia vs. Holkar) and the [[Peshwas]] allowed the British [[East India Company]] to intervene.
*   The [[Marathas]] fought three **Anglo-Maratha Wars** against the British. 
*   **Third Anglo-Maratha War (1817-1818):** The British decisively defeated the Peshwa (Baji Rao II). The Peshwaship was abolished, and the Maratha empire was absorbed into British India.

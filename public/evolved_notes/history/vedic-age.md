## 1. Introduction and Origins
The [[Vedic Age]] is the period in Indian history during which the [[Vedas]], the oldest scriptures of Hinduism, were composed. This era marks the arrival and settlement of the Indo-[[Aryans]] in the Indian subcontinent. The term "Arya" is a linguistic term meaning "noble" or "free-born" in Sanskrit, referring to speakers of the Indo-Aryan branch of the Indo-European language family.

**The Aryan Debate:** The most widely accepted theory regarding the origin of the [[Aryans]] is the **Central Asian theory** (proposed by Max Muller), which suggests they migrated from the steppes of Central Asia around 1500 BCE in multiple waves. They initially settled in the *Sapta Sindhu* region (the land of the seven [[rivers]]: [[Indus]] and its five tributaries + the Saraswati) in northwestern India.

The [[Vedic Age]] is historically divided into two distinct phases based on geographic expansion, technological shifts, and societal changes:
1.  **Early Vedic (Rigvedic) Period:** 1500 BCE - 1000 BCE
2.  **Later Vedic Period:** 1000 BCE - 600 BCE

## 2. Vedic Literature (Shruti and Smriti)
The word 'Veda' comes from the root *vid*, meaning "to know", signifying supreme knowledge. Vedic [[literature]] is traditionally classified into *Shruti* (that which has been heard/revealed) and *Smriti* (that which is remembered/traditional).

### The Four Vedas (Shruti)
1.  **Rigveda:** The oldest religious text in the world. It is a collection of 1028 hymns (Suktas) divided into 10 Mandalas. 
    *   *Mandala III* contains the famous **Gayatri Mantra** (dedicated to Savitri, the solar deity), composed by Vishvamitra.
    *   *Mandala X* contains the **Purusha Sukta**, which explains the origin of the four Varnas (social classes) from the cosmic man.
2.  **Samaveda:** The Veda of melodies and chants. It is the root of Indian classical music. Most of its hymns are derived from the Rigveda, modified to be sung.
3.  **Yajurveda:** The Veda of sacrificial formulas and rituals. It contains the procedures for performing sacrifices. It is divided into Shukla (White) and [[Krishna]] (Black) Yajurveda.
4.  **Atharvaveda:** The latest Veda, completely different from the other three. It contains charms, magical spells to ward off [[diseases]] and evil spirits, and is a vital source for understanding the non-Aryan/popular beliefs of the time.

### Other Vedic Texts
*   **Brahmanas:** Prose commentaries attached to the [[Vedas]] explaining the hidden meanings of the rituals. (e.g., Shatapatha Brahmana is the most important).
*   **Aranyakas:** "Forest books" meant for hermits. They form the concluding parts of the Brahmanas and mark a shift from ritualism to philosophy.
*   **[[Upanishads]]:** Also known as *Vedanta* (the end of the [[Vedas]]). There are 108 [[Upanishads]]. They are highly philosophical texts dealing with the concepts of *Atman* (soul) and *Brahman* (ultimate reality). 
    *   *Trivia:* The national motto **"Satyameva Jayate"** (Truth alone triumphs) is taken from the **Mundaka Upanishad**.

## 3. Early Vedic (Rigvedic) Period (1500 - 1000 BCE)

### Polity and Society
*   **Polity:** The political structure was tribal. The tribe was called *Jana*, and the king was the *Rajan*. The king's [[power]] was not absolute; it was restricted by tribal assemblies like the **Sabha** (council of elders) and **Samiti** (general assembly of the tribe). Women actively participated in these assemblies.
*   **Society:** Society was patriarchal, but women held a respectable position. They received education (scholars like Gargi and Maitreyi) and could choose their husbands (Swayamvara). There was no child marriage, and widow remarriage was allowed.
*   **Economy:** It was a predominantly **pastoral economy**. Cattle (*Gau*) was the main measure of wealth, and most wars (*Gavishthi*) were fought for cows. [[Agriculture]] was secondary. There was no regular taxation; voluntary offerings (*Bali*) were made to the king.
*   **Religion:** They worshipped personified forces of nature. There were no temples or idol worship. The central rituals were sacrifices (*Yajnas*).
    *   **Indra:** The most important god (destroyer of forts, *Purandara*).
    *   **Agni:** The fire god (intermediary between gods and men).
    *   **Varuna:** The upholder of cosmic order (*Rta*).

## 4. Later Vedic Period (1000 - 600 BCE)

### Transition and Expansion
The Later Vedic period is marked by the discovery and widespread use of **Iron** (called *Shyama Ayas* or *[[Krishna]] Ayas*). Iron axes allowed the [[Aryans]] to clear the dense [[forests]] of the Gangetic plain, leading to a geographical shift from the [[Indus]] region eastward to the Kuru-Panchala region (western UP) and further to Videha (Bihar).

### Polity and Economy
*   **Polity:** The tribal assemblies lost their [[power]]. The king's authority became absolute and hereditary. He legitimized his [[power]] through massive sacrifices like the *Rajasuya* (conferring supreme [[power]]) and *Ashvamedha* (horse sacrifice for territorial expansion). *Janas* merged to form large territorial states called *Janapadas*.
*   **Economy:** The economy shifted from pastoral to **predominantly agricultural**. Iron ploughshares enabled deep plowing, producing a massive agricultural surplus. This surplus allowed for the rise of a non-producing class (priests, kings, artisans), laying the groundwork for the Second [[Urbanization]] in India. Regular taxation systems (collected by the *Bhagadugha*) were established.

### Social Changes and the Varna System
Society became rigidly stratified based on birth rather than occupation.

![Vedic Society Hierarchy & Varna System](/assets/diagrams/history_3_ancient_india_vedic_age_vedic_social_hierarchy.png)

*   **Varna System:** The fourfold division solidified:
    1.  **Brahmanas:** Priests and teachers.
    2.  **Kshatriyas:** Rulers and warriors.
    3.  **Vaishyas:** Farmers, traders, and artisans (the only tax-paying class).
    4.  **Shudras:** Laborers and servants.
*   **Position of Women:** The status of women declined drastically. They lost the right to participate in assemblies, education was restricted, and child marriages became common.
*   **Ashrama System:** The concept of four stages of life emerged: *Brahmacharya* (student), *Grihastha* (householder), *Vanaprastha* (hermit), and *Sannyasa* (ascetic).

### Religion
The early Vedic nature gods (Indra, Agni) lost their importance. They were replaced by the Hindu trinity (Prajapati/Brahma, Vishnu, and Rudra/Shiva). Rituals became extremely elaborate, expensive, and dominated by the Brahmanas, leading to deep social discontent which eventually paved the way for the rise of [[Buddhism]] and [[Jainism]].

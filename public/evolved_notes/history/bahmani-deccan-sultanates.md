# The Bahmani Kingdom and Deccan Sultanates

## 1. The Bahmani Kingdom (1347 CE – 1527 CE)
The Bahmani Sultanate was the first independent Islamic kingdom in South India. It was founded as a revolt against the oppressive policies of the Delhi Sultan Muhammad bin Tughlaq.

*   **Founder:** **Alauddin Hasan Bahman Shah** (originally known as Hasan Gangu) in 1347 CE. He established his capital at **Gulbarga** (later moved to **Bidar** by Ahmad Shah Wali).
*   **Territory:** Spanned across the Deccan plateau, primarily between the [[Krishna]] and [[Narmada]] [[rivers]].
*   **Conflict with Vijayanagara:** The history of the Bahmani kingdom is dominated by its incessant wars with the [[Vijayanagara Empire]] to the south over the fertile **Raichur Doab** and the [[Krishna]]-[[Godavari]] basin.

### Key Figures
*   **Taj-ud-din Firuz Shah (1397 – 1422):** A learned ruler who patronized scholars and encouraged the induction of Hindus into the administration. He built an observatory near Daulatabad.
*   **Mahmud Gawan:** The most significant figure in the later Bahmani period was not a king, but a Prime Minister (Wakir-us-Sultanat). 
    *   An Iranian by birth, he was a brilliant military commander and administrator who expanded the empire to its greatest extent (conquering Goa and Dabhol).
    *   **Reforms:** He divided the empire into eight provinces (*Tarafs*) and strictly controlled the nobles. He built a magnificent **Madrasa at Bidar** (a center of Islamic learning).
    *   His execution by a suspicious Sultan (Muhammad Shah III) marked the beginning of the rapid decline of the Bahmani kingdom.

### Internal Factionalism
The Bahmani nobility was deeply divided into two hostile camps, leading to chronic instability:
1.  **Deccanis:** Indigenous Muslims (including converts) and Sunni Africans (Habshis).
2.  **Afaquis (Gharibs):** Foreign nobles of Iranian, Iraqi, and Turkish descent (mostly Shias).

## 2. The Five Deccan Sultanates
Following the execution of Mahmud Gawan and the weakening of central authority, the Bahmani Kingdom fractured into five independent Sultanates between 1490 and 1527 CE:

| Sultanate | Dynasty | Founder | Annexed By (Mughal Emperor) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bijapur** | Adil Shahi | Yusuf Adil Shah | [[Aurangzeb]] (1686) |
| **Ahmadnagar** | Nizam Shahi | Malik Ahmad | [[Shah Jahan]] (1636) |
| **Golconda** | Qutb Shahi | Quli Qutb Shah | [[Aurangzeb]] (1687) |
| **Bidar** | Barid Shahi | Ali Barid | Bijapur (1619) |
| **Berar** | Imad Shahi | Fathullah Imad-ul-Mulk | Ahmadnagar (1574) |

### Key Features of the Deccan Sultanates
*   **The Battle of Talikota (1565):** Four of the five sultanates (Bijapur, Ahmadnagar, Golconda, Bidar) formed a grand alliance to decisively defeat and destroy the [[Vijayanagara Empire]].
*   **[[Architecture]]:** 
    *   **Gol Gumbaz (Bijapur):** The mausoleum of Muhammad Adil Shah. It has the largest dome in India (and second largest in the world after St. Peter's Basilica) and is famous for its 'Whispering Gallery'.
    *   **Charminar (Hyderabad/Golconda):** Built by Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah to commemorate the end of a plague.
*   **Deccani Urdu:** A distinct regional language (a blend of Arabic, Persian, Marathi, Kannada, and Telugu) flourished under their patronage.
*   **Painting:** The Deccani schools of miniature painting (Bijapur, Golconda, Ahmadnagar styles) developed, known for their rich colors and Persian influence mixed with local traditions.

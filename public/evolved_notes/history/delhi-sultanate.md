## 1. Introduction
The period between 1206 CE and 1526 CE in Indian history is known as the [[Delhi Sultanate]] period. During these 320 years, five distinct Turkic and Afghan dynasties ruled from Delhi.

![Delhi Sultanate Timeline & Five Dynasties](/assets/diagrams/history_4_medieval_india_delhi_sultanate_delhi_sultanate_timeline.png)

The five dynasties of the [[Delhi Sultanate]] were:
1.  **Slave Dynasty (Mamluk Dynasty):** 1206 – 1290
2.  **Khalji Dynasty:** 1290 – 1320 (Shortest ruling dynasty)
3.  **Tughlaq Dynasty:** 1320 – 1414 (Longest ruling dynasty)
4.  **Sayyid Dynasty:** 1414 – 1451
5.  **Lodi Dynasty:** 1451 – 1526 (First Afghan dynasty)

## 2. The Slave Dynasty (1206 – 1290)
Called 'Slave' because its founders were military slaves (Mamluks) of Muhammad Ghori.

*   **Qutb-ud-din Aibak (1206 – 1210):** 
    *   Founder of the dynasty. Known as *Lakh Bakhsh* (Giver of Lakhs) for his generosity.
    *   He began the construction of the **Qutb Minar** (named after Sufi saint Qutbuddin Bakhtiyar Kaki) and built the **Quwwat-ul-Islam mosque** in Delhi and *Adhai Din Ka Jhonpra* in Ajmer.
    *   Died accidentally while playing *Chaugan* (polo).
*   **Iltutmish (1211 – 1236):**
    *   Considered the **real founder** of the [[Delhi Sultanate]]. He shifted the capital from Lahore to Delhi.
    *   He saved Delhi from the wrath of Genghis Khan by refusing asylum to the Khwarizmi Shah.
    *   Completed the Qutb Minar.
    *   Organized the *Turkan-i-Chahalgani* (The Corps of Forty), a powerful council of Turkic nobles.
    *   Introduced the Arabic coinage system in India: the silver *Tanka* and the copper *Jital*.
*   **Razia Sultana (1236 – 1240):** The first and **only female Muslim ruler** of medieval India. She was assassinated due to a conspiracy by the Chahalgani nobles who resented a female [[sovereign]].
*   **Ghiyasuddin Balban (1266 – 1287):**
    *   A stern ruler who broke the [[power]] of the *Chahalgani*.
    *   Propounded the **Theory of Divine Right of Kingship** (called himself *Zil-i-Ilahi*, shadow of God).
    *   Introduced Persian court customs like *Sijda* (prostration) and *Paibos* (kissing the monarch's feet) and the Persian festival of *Navroz*.
    *   Created a separate military department called **Diwan-i-Arz**.

## 3. The Khalji Dynasty (1290 – 1320)
*   **Jalaluddin Khalji:** Founder of the dynasty.
*   **Alauddin Khalji (1296 – 1316):** 
    *   The most powerful ruler of the [[Delhi Sultanate]]. He assassinated his uncle/father-in-law Jalaluddin to take the throne.
    *   **Military:** First Sultan to maintain a large permanent standing army. Introduced *Dagh* (branding of horses) and *Chehra* (descriptive roll of soldiers) to prevent corruption.
    *   **Market Reforms:** He is famous for his strict **Market Control Policy**. He set up three separate markets in Delhi (for food grains, costly cloth, and horses/slaves) and appointed officers called *Shahna-i-Mandi* to strictly regulate prices.
    *   **Conquests:** He conquered Gujarat, Ranthambore, Chittor (Padmavati episode), and Malwa. He was the first Sultan to expand south of the Vindhyas; his slave-general **Malik Kafur** led successful campaigns against the Yadavas, Kakatiyas, Hoysalas, and Pandyas.
    *   **[[Architecture]]:** Built the *Alai Darwaza* (entrance to Qutb complex), *Siri Fort*, and *Hauz Khas*.

## 4. The Tughlaq Dynasty (1320 – 1414)
*   **Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq:** Founder. Built the city of Tughlaqabad.
*   **Muhammad bin Tughlaq (1324 – 1351):** 
    *   Known as a 'brilliant but eccentric' scholar. His reign is famous for five ambitious but disastrous administrative experiments:
        1.  **Transfer of Capital** from Delhi to Devagiri (renamed Daulatabad) to control the south. The forced migration caused immense suffering, and he eventually had to move back to Delhi.
        2.  **Introduction of Token Currency** (copper/brass coins at par with silver). Failed because people began forging coins at home.
        3.  **Taxation in the Doab:** Increased taxes in the fertile [[Ganga]]-Yamuna doab during a severe famine, leading to massive peasant revolts.
        4.  **Khurasan Expedition:** Raised a huge standing army to conquer Central Asia but abandoned the plan after a year, bankrupting the treasury.
        5.  **Karachil Expedition:** A disastrous campaign in the Kumaon hills where the entire army perished.
    *   The Moroccan traveler **Ibn Battuta** visited India during his reign and wrote the book *Rihla*.
*   **Firoz Shah Tughlaq (1351 – 1388):**
    *   A reformer who built numerous canals for [[irrigation]], founded cities (Firozabad, Hisar, Jaunpur, Fatehabad), and established hospitals (*Dar-ul-Shifa*) and employment bureaus.
    *   He imposed *Jizya* purely as a religious tax (even on Brahmanas, who were previously exempt).

## 5. Sayyid and Lodi Dynasties
*   **Timur's Invasion (1398):** The Mongol conqueror Timur (Tamerlane) invaded and sacked Delhi, dealing a death blow to the Tughlaq dynasty.
*   **The Sayyid Dynasty (1414 – 1451):** Founded by Khizr Khan, who claimed descent from the Prophet Muhammad. They ruled as tributaries to Timur's successors.
*   **The Lodi Dynasty (1451 – 1526):**
    *   **Bahlul Lodi:** Founder; the first Afghan ruler of Delhi.
    *   **Sikandar Lodi:** The greatest Lodi king. He founded the city of **Agra** (1504) and introduced the *Gaj-i-Sikandari* (a unit for measuring land).
    *   **Ibrahim Lodi:** The last Sultan. His arrogant behavior alienated his Afghan nobles. Daulat Khan Lodi ([[governor]] of Punjab) and Rana Sanga of Mewar invited [[Babur]] (from Kabul) to invade India.
    *   **First Battle of Panipat (1526):** [[Babur]] decisively defeated and killed Ibrahim Lodi, bringing the [[Delhi Sultanate]] to an end and establishing the [[Mughal Empire]].

## 6. Administration of the Delhi Sultanate
*   **Sultan:** The absolute head of state, though legally acknowledging the supremacy of the Caliph.
*   **Key Departments:**
    *   *Diwan-i-Wizarat:* Department of finance, headed by the *Wazir* (Prime Minister).
    *   *Diwan-i-Arz:* Military department, headed by the *Ariz-i-Mamalik*.
    *   *Diwan-i-Insha:* Department of correspondence.
    *   *Diwan-i-Rasalat:* Department of religious affairs and appeals.
*   **Iqta System:** Introduced by Iltutmish. It was a unique land distribution system where military commanders (Iqtadars or Muqtis) were granted land (Iqta) in lieu of cash salary. They collected revenue from the land, maintained an army for the Sultan, and kept a portion for themselves.

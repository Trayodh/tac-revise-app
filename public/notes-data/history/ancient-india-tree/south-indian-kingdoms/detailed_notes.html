South Indian Kingdoms

The history of South India is marked by the Sangam Age, followed by the Pallavas and Chalukyas, and later the mighty Cholas.

1. The Sangam Age (3rd BCE - 3rd CE)

  • Assemblies (Sangams) of Tamil poets held under Pandyan patronage. Key literature: Tolkappiyam (grammar), epics Silappatikaram (Ilango Adigal) and Manimekalai (Shattanar).
  • Three kingdoms: Cheras (emblem: Bow), Cholas (emblem: Tiger, capital: Puhar, King Karikala built Grand Anicut), Pandyas (emblem: Fish, capital: Madurai).

2. Pallavas & Chalukyas (6th - 8th CE)

  • Pallavas of Kanchi: Mahendravarman I, Narasimhavarman I (defeated Chalukya King Pulakeshin II, took title *Vatapikonda*). Built the rock-cut **Rathas of Mahabalipuram** (Shore Temple).
  • Chalukyas of Vatapi: Pulakeshin II. Defeated Harshavardhana on the banks of Narmada (recorded in Aihole inscription). Defeated by Narasimhavarman I.

3. Imperial Cholas (9th - 12th CE)

  • Founded by Vijayalaya. Capital at Thanjavur.
  • Rajaraja Chola I (985 - 1014 CE): Built the grand Brihadeshwara (Rajarajeshwara) Temple at Thanjavur (tallest Vimana in India). Annexed northern Sri Lanka.
  • Rajendra Chola I (1014 - 1044 CE): Annexed entire Sri Lanka. Marched up to Ganges, took title Gangaikonda Chola and founded capital Gangaikondacholapuram. Sent naval expeditions to Southeast Asia (Srivijaya Kingdom), dominating the Bay of Bengal (called the "Chola Lake").
  • Village Self-Government: The Uttaramerur Inscription of Parantaka I (907-955 CE) details the working of local assemblies (Ur and Sabha). Members were elected by lottery (Kudavolai system) under strict eligibility rules.

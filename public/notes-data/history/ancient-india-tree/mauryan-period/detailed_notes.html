• Mauryan Period

The Mauryan Empire (322 - 185 BCE)

India's first historical empire, establishing central control over most of the subcontinent.

1. Rulers & Triumphs

  • Chandragupta Maurya (322 - 298 BCE): Overthrew Dhana Nanda with Chanakya's guidance. Defeated Seleucus Nicator (305 BCE), acquiring Baluchistan and Afghanistan. Megasthenes visited his court. Adopted Jainism under Bhadrabahu and died via Sallekhana (fasting) at Shravanabelagola.
  • Bindusara (298 - 273 BCE): Called *Amitraghata* (slayer of enemies). Received ambassadors from Syria (Deimachus) and Egypt (Dionysius). Patronised the Ajivika sect.
  • Ashoka (273 - 232 BCE): Coronation in 269 BCE. Kalinga War (261 BCE, detailed in **Major Rock Edict XIII**) caused immense casualties, prompting Ashoka to abandon war (*Bherighosha*) and adopt Dhamma (*Dhammaghosha*). Sent Buddhist missions to Sri Lanka (Mahinda/Sanghamitta).

2. Ashokan Edicts & Dhamma

  • Edicts are classified into Major Rock Edicts (14), Minor Rock Edicts, Major Pillar Edicts (7), and Cave Inscriptions. Written mostly in Prakrit (Brahmi script).
  • Major Rock Edict XIII: Mentions Kalinga War and contemporary Hellenic kings (Antiochus, Ptolemy).
  • Rummindei Pillar Inscription: Marks Buddha's birthplace (Lumbini) and records Ashoka reducing land tax (Bhaga) to 1/8th.

3. Administration & Economy

  • Highly centralized bureaucracy. King assisted by Mantriparishad. 7 limbs of state defined in Kautilya's Saptanga Theory (Swami, Amatya, Janapada, Durga, Kosha, Danda, Mitra).
  • Spy network of Gudhapurushas (Sanstha - stationary, Sanchari - wandering).
  • Tax: Land revenue (*Bhaga*) was usually 1/6th of produce. Punched-marked silver coins (*Panas*) were widely circulated.

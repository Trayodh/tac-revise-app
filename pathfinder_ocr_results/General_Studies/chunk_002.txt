Division of Buddhism
The Buddhism was divided into following sects
i. Hinayana or Lesser Wheel The followers of
Hinayana believed in the original teachings of Buddha.
They believed in individual salvation. They do not
believe in idol-worship.
ii. Mahayana or Greater Wheel The follower of
Mahayana sect believed in the heavenliness of Buddha
and sought the salvation through the grace and help
of Buddha and Bodhisattvas. It came into existence
during the reign of Kanishka.
iii. Vajrayana or Vehicle of Thunderbolt The followers
of Vajrayana believed that salvation could be attained
by acquiring the magical powers i.e. Vajra. The chief
divinities of their sect were Taras. Vajrayana became
popular in Eastern part of India, particularly in Bengal
and Bihar.

Buddhist Councils
•
The First Council (483 BC) It was held at Sattapanni
(Saptaparni) cave near Rajagriha under the chairmanship
of Mahakassapa. Here Vinaya Pitaka and Sutta Pitaka
were compiled by Upali and Ananda respectively.
Ajatashatru was the king at that time.
The Second Council (383 BC) It was held at Vaishali
during the reign of Kalashoka, the king of Shishunaga
dynasty. The chairman was Sabakami. The monk of
Vaishali wanted some changes in the rites. It resulted in
division of sect into Sthavirvadins and Mahasamghikas.
The Third Council (250 BC) It was held at
Pataliputra during the reign of Ashoka (the Mauryan
ruler). This council was held under the Presidentship of
Mogaliputta Tissa, to revise the Buddhist texts.
⚫ The Fourth Council (AD 72) It was held during the
reign of Kanishka (the Kushana ruler) in Kashmir under
the presidentship of Vasumitra and Ashwaghosha.
Buddhism got divided into Hinayana and Mahayana.

Stupas are doomed monuments containing relies of the

Full Moon This occurs on the 14th day after the
New Moon. The Moon at this time shows its fully
lighted surface. (Full Moon = Complete Moon)
When two full Moons occurs in a single month, the
second full Moon is called a Blue Moon.
Stars
•
Stars are heavenly bodies made up of hot burning gases,
thus shine by their own light. If the star is bigger than
the Sun but not more than twice as big, it will turn into
a Neutron star or Pulsar. They are formed due to
novae and supernovae explosion.
A star's colour indicates the temperature of its surface.
Blue colour denotes maximum temperature. Then comes
yellow, then red etc.
Evolutionary Stages of a Star
i. Adult Star It is the stage, where the helium core
becomes increasingly heavy, accompanied with
expanding outer layers.
ii. Red Giant This stage results into swelling and
reddening of the outer regions of the star. Such star
of gigantic dimension is called Red star.
iii. White Dwarf If the mass of the star is relatively
small like that of our Sun, the gases that reach the
outer layer are expelled. As these expelled gases cool
and contract, the star becomes a white dwarf.
THE EARTH
⚫ It is the 5th largest planet of our solar system. Only
planet of our solar system where life exists. It is also
called 'Blue Planet'. It has circumference of 40008 km
(polar) and equatorial circumference 40075 km and area
of nearly 510 million sq kms.

The history of the Earth is studied in terms of geological
years, periods and the epochs.
Shape of the Earth
The Earth is an oblate spheroid, a sphere flattened along the
axis from pole to pole such that there is a buldge around the
equator. Earth bulges at the equator and is flattened at
the poles. The shape of Earth is also called Geoid. Geodesy
is a branch of science which deals with measuring the
Earth's size and shape.
The Earth's Movement
The Earth moves in space in two distinct ways rotation and
revolution, which are as follow
i. It rotates on its own axis from West to East once in
every 24 hrs. It causes day and night.
ii. It revolves around the Sun in an orbit once in every
365 days. It causes the seasons and the year.
Rotation of the Earth
•
.
Earth spins on its imaginary axis from West to East in
23 hrs, 56 min and 40.91 sec. The rotational speed at
equator is maximum (1667 km/hr) and then decreases
towards the poles, where it is zero.
The rotation of the Earth causes day and night, change
in the direction of winds and ocean currents, rise and
fall of tides everyday and a difference of 1 hr between
the two meridians which are 15° apart.
The division between day and night over the Earth is
considered by the circle of illumination. The circle of
illumination always bisect the equator and divides Earth
into light and dark halves i.e. a day hemisphere and a
night hemisphere.
Revolution of the Earth
.
It is the Earth's motion in elliptical orbit around the
Sun. It takes 365 days, 5 hrs, 48 min and 45.51 sec. It
leads to one extra day in every fourth year.
The revolution of the Earth results in
i. change of season.
ii. variation of the length of the days and nights at
different times of the year.
iii. shifting of the wind belts.

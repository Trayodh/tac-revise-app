WEATHERING
⚫ It is the process of disintegration
or decomposition of rocks in Situ
by natural agents. It is a static
process.
There are three types of weathering as given
below
i. Physical Weathering (or mechanical
weathering)
(a) It involves rock disintegration
without any change in the chemical
constituents of the rocks.
(b) The factors responsible for physical
weathering are temperature change,
crystallisation of water into ice and
pressure release mechanism.
ii. Chemical Weathering It involves
decomposition due to chemical changes.
There are various chemical processes,
which cause chemical weathering such as
Solution, Oxidation, Carbonation,
Hydration, Hydrolysis and Chelation.
iii. Biological Weathering Plants and
animals including man largely control it.
It is divided into three types
(a) Faunal weathering
(b) Floral weathering
(c) Anthropogenic weathering
Erosion
It involves removal of rock material and then
its transportation. Therefore, erosion is
performed by mobile agents such as streams,
wind, waves and underground water.

LANDFORMS
A landform is a feature on the Earth's
surface. It may be either the terrain,
mountains, hills, plateaus or plains.
Minor landforms include buttes, canyons,
valleys and basins etc.
Tectonic plate movement under the Earth can create landforms by
pushing up mountains and hills.
Major classification of landforms on the basis of their process of origin is
given as under:
Fluvial Landforms
A fluvial landform is a feature resulting from the movement of water on
the Earth's surface. Flowing water is the most important force shaping
the Earth.
i. Erosional Landforms Erosional landforms made by river are
u-shaped valley, potholes, plunge pools, meanders, river terraces etc.
ii. Depositional Landforms Depositional landforms made by river are
alluvial fans, deltas, floodplains, natural levees, point bars etc.
Karst Landforms
Any limestone or dolomite region showing typical landforms produced
by the action of ground water through the processes of solution and
deposition is called Karst Topography.
i. Erosional Landforms Pools, sinkholes, lapies and limestone
pavement, caves are important erosional landforms made by
underground water.
ii. Depositional Landforms Stalactites, stalagmites, pillars etc are
important depositional landforms of underground water.
Glacial Landforms
The movement of glaciers is slow unlike the water flow. The movement
could be a few centimetres to a few metres a day or even less. Glaciers
move basically because of the force of gravity.
i. Erosional Landforms Cirque/Corrie U-shaped valley, horns,
serrated ridges, glacial troughs, hanging valleys etc are important
erosional landforms made by glaciers.
ii. Depositional Landforms Moraines, eskers, outwash plains,
drumlins etc are depositional landforms made by glaciers.
Coastal Landforms
Coastal landforms are formed by erosion or deposition through sea
waves, aided by currents tides and storms in coastal areas.
i. Erosional Landforms Cliffs, terraces, caves, stacks etc are
important landforms made by oceanic waves.
ii. Depositional Landforms Beaches, dunes, bars, barriers, spits etc
are important depositional landforms made by sea and oceanic
waves.
Aeolian Landforms
Wind is one of the most dominant agents in hot deserts. The wind action
creates a number of interesting erosional land depositional features in the
deserts.
Aeolian landforms are regions where erosion and deposition by wind are
dominant geomorphic forces in shaping the landform. They are formed
in hot deserts, coastal zones and fields.
i. Erosional Landforms Pediments, pediplains, playas, deflation
hollows, mushroom rock, table rock, pedestal rocks etc are erosional
landforms in deserts.
ii. Depositional Landforms Sand dunes, barchans, seifs etc are
depositional landforms made by wind.

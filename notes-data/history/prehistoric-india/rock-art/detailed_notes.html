Prehistoric Rock Art

Prehistoric rock art provides invaluable insights into the daily life, beliefs, and cognitive evolution of early humans.

1. Bhimbetka Caves (Madhya Pradesh)

  • Discovery: Discovered by archaeologist **V.S. Wakankar** in 1957. Located in Vindhya Range, MP.
  • UNESCO Status: Declared a UNESCO World Heritage site in 2003.
  • Artistic Styles: Displays continuous artistic activity from the Upper Paleolithic, Mesolithic, Neolithic, Chalcolithic, to Historical periods.
  • Themes & Pigments: Mainly depicts wild animals (bison, tigers, elephants), hunting scenes, community dances, battles, and family life.
  • Colors: Used mineral colors mixed with water/animal fat. Red (from hematite/geru for hunters) and Green (from chalcedony for dancers) are the most prominent.

High-Yield Formulas & Facts

  • Bhimbetka discoverer: V.S. Wakankar (1957) | Colors: Green (dance), Red (hunt)

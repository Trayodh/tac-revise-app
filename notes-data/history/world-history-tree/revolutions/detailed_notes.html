Industrial & Political Revolutions

Key global revolutions that shaped modern political ideologies and economic structures.

1. American Revolution (1775 - 1783)

  • Causes: British taxation without representation (Stamp Act, Townshend Acts), trade restrictions.
  • Boston Tea Party (1773): Colonists dumped British tea into the harbor to protest tea tax.
  • Declaration of Independence: Drafted by Thomas Jefferson, adopted on **4 July 1776**.
  • Treaty of Paris (1783): Britain recognized the independence of the United States.

2. French Revolution (1789)

  • Causes: Divison of French society into three estates (Clergy, Nobility, Commoners/Third Estate). Only the Third Estate paid taxes. Bankruptcy under Louis XVI.
  • Storming of the Bastille (14 July 1789): A fortress-prison, symbol of royal tyranny, was destroyed by a mob. Celebrated as France's National Day.
  • Motto: **"Liberty, Equality, Fraternity"**.
  • Result: Executed Louis XVI and Marie Antoinette, ended feudalism, established republic, and paved the way for Napoleon Bonaparte's rise.

3. Russian Revolution (1917)

  • Overthrew the Tsarist autocracy. Caused by economic hardships of WWI, food shortages, and absolute rule of Tsar Nicholas II.
  • February Revolution: Forced the Tsar to abdicate; provisional government set up.
  • October (Bolshevik) Revolution: Led by Vladimir Lenin. Overthrew the provisional government, ending private property, establishing a socialist state (leading to the creation of the USSR in 1922).

4. Industrial Revolution

  • Began in Britain in the mid-18th century. Transitioned from hand production to machine manufacturing.
  • Key inventions: James Watt's Steam Engine, Spinning Jenny, railway locomotives.
  • Impact: Rise of capitalism, urbanization, growth of working class, and colonial search for raw materials.

European Entry and British Expansion

The search for spices led European powers to establish trading outposts in India, which eventually turned into territorial ambitions.

1. Arrival Sequence (PEDDF)

  • Portuguese (1498): Vasco da Gama landed at Calicut. Francisco de Almeida (Blue Water Policy). Alfonso de Albuquerque captured Goa (1510) from Bijapur, making it the capital. Introduced printing press (1556) and tobacco.
  • Dutch (1602): First factory at Masulipatnam (1605). Displaced by British after the Battle of Bedara (1759).
  • English (1600): Queen Elizabeth granted charter. Hawkins (1608) and Roe (1615) secured trading rights from Jahangir. First factory at Surat (1613).
  • Danish (1616): Settled at Tranquebar (TN) and Serampore (Bengal). Sold all holdings to British in 1845.
  • French (1664): Founded by Colbert under Louis XIV. First factory at Surat (1667). François Martin founded Pondicherry (1674). Carnatic Wars (Anglo-French rivalry) crushed French power, culminating in their defeat at the Battle of Wandiwash (1760).

2. Foundation of British Rule

  • Battle of Plassey (23 June 1757): Robert Clive defeated Nawab Siraj-ud-Daulah of Bengal. Clive bribed army chief Mir Jafar. Established EIC as a political force.
  • Battle of Buxar (22 Oct 1764): Hector Munro defeated joint forces of Nawab Mir Qasim of Bengal, Nawab Shuja-ud-Daulah of Awadh, and Mughal Emperor Shah Alam II. Led to **Treaty of Allahabad (1765)** where Clive secured **Diwani Rights** (tax collection rights) of Bengal, Bihar, and Odisha, starting the Dual Government.

High-Yield Formulas & Facts

  • Arrival: Portuguese (1498) -> Dutch (1602) -> English -> French (1664)
  • Goa Captured: 1510 (Albuquerque)

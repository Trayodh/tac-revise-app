IN Post-Independence Consolidation

The challenges of nation-building, border demarcations, princely states integration, and linguistic reorganization after 1947.

1. Integration of Princely States

Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel (Minister of States) and V.P. Menon successfully integrated 549 princely states. Three states presented exceptions:

  • Junagadh: Nawab wanted to join Pakistan, but the Hindu majority population rebelled. Integrated via **Plebiscite (February 1948)**.
  • Hyderabad: Nizam refused to join India. Nizam's irregular militia (Razakars) terrorized the population. Integrated via military action code-named **Operation Polo (September 1948)**.
  • Jammu & Kashmir: Ruler Hari Singh wanted to remain independent. In October 1947, Pakistani armed tribal raiders invaded. Hari Singh signed the **Instrument of Accession (26 Oct 1947)**, and Indian troops flew in to defend Srinagar.

2. States Reorganization

  • Andhra Pradesh became the first linguistic state in 1953 following the death of Potti Sriramulu after a 56-day hunger strike.
  • Dhar Commission (1948) and JVP Committee (1948 - Jawaharlal, Vallabhbhai, Pattabhi) rejected language as basis of states.
  • Following Sriramulu's death, the government appointed the Fazl Ali Commission (States Reorganization Commission, 1953). Its recommendations led to the States Reorganization Act (1956), creating 14 states and 6 Union Territories.

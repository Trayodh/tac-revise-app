The Bhakti Movement

A medieval socio-religious reform movement preaching personal devotion to God as the path to salvation, bypassing priests and caste barriers.

1. Origins & Schools

  • Originated in South India (7th-9th century) led by Alvars (Vaishnavite saints) and Nayanars (Shaivite saints).
  • Spread to North India in the 14th century by Ramananda.
  • Saguna School (Worshipped God with Form): Mirabai: Devoted to Krishna; composed Bhajans in Rajasthani/Braj.
  • Tulsidas: Wrote Ramcharitmanas in Awadhi.
  • Chaitanya Mahaprabhu: Bengal; popularized Kirtan (musical chanting).
  • Nirguna School (Worshipped Formless God): Kabir: Disciple of Ramananda. Opposed rituals, caste system, idol worship. Expressed ideas in Dohas (couplets) compiled in Bijak.
  • Guru Nanak: Preached monotheism and equality.

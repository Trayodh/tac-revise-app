Post-Mauryan Period

Characterized by political fragmentation, rise of regional dynasties, and foreign invasions from Central Asia.

1. Indigenous Dynasties

  • Sunga Dynasty (185 - 73 BCE): Pushyamitra Sunga, a Brahmin army chief, assassinated the last Maurya King Brihadratha. Patronised Patanjali (grammarian). Renovated Sanchi and Bharhut stupas.
  • Kanva Dynasty: Founded by Vasudeva Kanva. Short-lived.
  • Satavahanas (Andhras): Founded by Simuka. Ruled Deccan/Central India. Gautamiputra Satakarni (the greatest ruler, called 'sole Brahmin' in Nasik inscription). **Signature features:** Issued lead coins, patronised Prakrit (Hala wrote *Gathasaptasati*), pioneered **royal land grants** to Brahmins and Buddhist monks.

2. Foreign Invasions

  • Indo-Greeks: First to invade. **Menander** (Milinda) was converted to Buddhism by Nagasena (recorded in text *Milindapanha* - Questions of Milinda). First to issue gold coins with inscriptions in India.
  • Sakas (Scythians): **Rudradaman I** (Western Kshatrapa) is famous for the **Junagadh Rock Inscription** (first major inscription in pure Sanskrit, records repair of Sudarshana Lake).
  • Kushanas: Central Asian tribe. **Kanishka (78 - 101 CE):** Founded the **Saka Era (78 CE)**. Organised the 4th Buddhist Council in Kashmir. Capital at Purushapura (Peshawar).
  • Patronised scholars: Ashvaghosha (wrote *Buddhacharita*), Nagarjuna (Einstein of India, Madhyamika philosophy), Charaka (court physician, *Charaka Samhita*).
  • Gandhara School (Greek influence, realistic Buddha) and Mathura School (indigenous, red sandstone Buddha) of art flourished.

Mahajanapadas & Rise of Magadha

The 6th century BCE marked the second urbanization in India, characterized by territorial consolidation and new religious movements.

1. The 16 Mahajanapadas

  • Mentioned in Buddhist text Anguttara Nikaya and Jain text Bhagavati Sutra .
  • Monarchies: Magadha (strongest), Kosala (capital Ayodhya/Shravasti), Vatsa (Kausambi), Avanti (Ujjain).
  • Republics (Ganasanghas): **Vajji** (Vaishali, world's earliest republic, confederacy of 8 clans), Malla (Kusinara).

2. Rise of Magadha

Magadha rose to become the supreme power due to: large iron deposits (for weapons/tools), strategic capitals (Rajgir surrounded by hills; Pataliputra at river junctions), and first-time use of elephants in wars.

  • Haryanka Dynasty: **Bimbisara (544-492 BCE):** Founder. Expanded via matrimonial alliances (Kosala, Vaishali, Madra).
  • Ajatashatru (492-460 BCE): Killed his father Bimbisara. Annexed Kashi and Vaishali (used war engines Mahasilakantaka and Rathamusala). Patronised the 1st Buddhist Council at Rajgir.
  • Udayin: Shifted the capital from Rajgir to Pataliputra.
  • Shishunaga Dynasty: Annexed Avanti, ending the long rivalry. Shifted capital to Vaishali. Kalasoka patronised the **2nd Buddhist Council** (383 BCE).
  • Nanda Dynasty: **Mahapadma Nanda:** First non-Kshatriya dynasty. Title: *Ekarat* (sole sovereign) and *Sarvakshatrantaka* (destroyer of Kshatriyas).
  • Dhana Nanda: Last ruler. Alexander the Great invaded North-West India (326 BCE, Battle of Hydaspes vs. Porus) during his reign, but Alexander's army refused to cross the Beas river fearing Nanda's massive army.

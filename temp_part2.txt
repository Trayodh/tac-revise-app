<h3>1. Matrix Classifications</h3>
<ul>
  <li>**Symmetric Matrix**: $A^{\top} = A$. Diagonal elements can be any real or complex numbers. <em>UPSC Trend Alert:</em> Recent NDA papers frequently test the property that if $A$ and $B$ are symmetric matrices, then $AB + BA$ is symmetric, whereas $AB - BA$ is skew-symmetric.</li>
  <li>**Skew-Symmetric Matrix**: $A^{\top} = -A$. Diagonal elements must be **zero** (since $a_{ii} = -a_{ii} \implies 2a_{ii} = 0 \implies a_{ii} = 0$). </li>
  <li>**Orthogonal Matrix**: $A \cdot A^{\top} = I$. Consequently, $\det(A) = \pm1$. These matrices preserve length and angles under transformation.</li>
  <li>**Idempotent Matrix**: $A^2 = A$. The eigenvalues of an idempotent matrix can only be $0$ or $1$.</li>
  <li>**Involutory Matrix**: $A^2 = I$. Since $A^2 - I = 0$, its determinant is always $\pm1$.</li>
  <li>**Nilpotent Matrix**: $A^k = O$ (where $k$ is the index of nilpotency, the smallest positive integer for which this holds). The determinant of any nilpotent matrix is always zero.</li>
</ul>
